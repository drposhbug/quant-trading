# Retail speed loses every race under a minute

As of November 2026 there is no lead-lag or latency-arbitrage opportunity faster than about a minute that a US-based retail trader can realistically capture, on any venue pair studied. At microsecond and millisecond scales the lags are real, but in equities and futures they are decided in **5–10 µs**, on microwave links within **7 µs of the speed-of-light floor**, and **six firms take over 80%** of the winnings ([NBER w29011](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29011/w29011.pdf); [IMC 2020](https://bdebopam.github.io/papers/imc2020-hft.pdf)). Prediction markets are about a thousand times slower: Polymarket crypto quotes follow Binance after a median **347 ms**, and Kalshi leads Polymarket on big NFL moves by about **7 s**. But Polymarket answered the 2025–26 bot boom by charging taker fees that peak near the size of a typical lag, putting a **150 ms** uncancellable delay on taker orders in crypto markets and **1–3 s** in sports, and selling London colocation to KYC'd firms ([Polymarket changelog](https://docs.polymarket.com/changelog.md); [Polymarket fees](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees)). The one rigorous millisecond study of Binance→Polymarket found a 43-feature model **lost money per trade after fees** ([arXiv 2607.26245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26245)). The famous winners (0x8dxd's "98% win rate") come from a short, now-closed window, and realized about **1.2% of volume**. The legal map also narrows the field for this user: Polymarket international, which the repo's sports trader currently reads, is **close-only for US persons**, so anything live has to run on Polymarket US or Kalshi. What remains is minute-scale and slower work, where latency stops mattering and the edge, if any, comes from pricing. The repo's own two minute-level studies (stocks vs Polymarket daily Up/Down, and pre-open odds vs the day's return) are pre-registered and **still running**; they decide whether the one untested pair, Polymarket stock markets against the live stock, has anything in it.

## Equities and futures settle their lags in microseconds

The S&P 500 is priced in Chicago first. E-mini futures carry most of the index's price discovery ([Hasbrouck 2003](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:58:y:2003:i:6:p:2375-2400)), and **91%** of ES–SPY arbitrage opportunities start with a move in ES. The size and shape of the lag come from Budish, Cramton and Shim's 2011 data ([BCS](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f69665.pdf)). The ES–SPY return correlation is **0.007 at 1 ms, 0.46 at 100 ms, 0.69 at 1 s and 0.91 at 10 s**, and averaged one-minute returns are above 0.99. The median arbitrage window shrank from **97 ms in 2005 to 7 ms in 2011**, while profit per opportunity stayed flat at about $92. That is the signature of a pure speed race: the prize stays the same and the winning time keeps falling. London Stock Exchange message data from 2015 shows where the race ended up. Races happen about once a minute per FTSE 100 stock, the typical race lasts **5–10 microseconds**, and races make up about **20% of volume**. Each race is worth about half a tick, and latency arbitrage works like a **0.5 bp tax** on trading, about **$5bn a year** across global equities ([FCA OP50](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/occasional-papers/occasional-paper-no-50-quantifying-high-frequency-trading-arms-race-new-methodology)). ES–SPY alone was worth about **$75M a year** ([Richmond Fed](https://www.richmondfed.org/publications/research/econ_focus/2015/q3/research_spotlight)).

The infrastructure gap cannot be closed with money a retail trader has. The fastest Chicago–New Jersey microwave route ran at **3.96171 ms** one-way in 2020, against a physical minimum of 3.955 ms, and the runner-up was 0.4 µs behind ([IMC 2020](https://bdebopam.github.io/papers/imc2020-hft.pdf)). The best fibre takes about **6.65 ms** ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/content/Productsservices/trading/CoLo/microwave.pdf)), so anyone on fibre is 2.6 ms behind on every Chicago signal, and those signals are traded well within 2.6 ms. A CME Aurora rack ran **$15–19k a month** at launch ([A-Team](https://a-teaminsight.com/cmes-aurora-co-lo-quick-facts/)). On the retail side, Alpaca's order system processes 95% of orders in under 5 ms ([Alpaca](https://alpaca.markets/blog/alpaca-launches-next-gen-order-management-system/)), but users measure **200–350 ms** API round trips and up to 1 s end-to-end on data ([Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/what-are-your-response-times/5550); [Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/latency-on-real-time-alpaca-api/19092)). Alpaca's free real-time feed is IEX only, and its quotes "can vary substantially" from the NBBO ([Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/unclear-subscription-type-and-limitations/10233)). Marketable orders go to wholesalers that pay for order flow ([Alpaca 606](https://files.alpaca.markets/disclosures/library/SEC+606a1+-+2026Q2.pdf)), and those wholesalers see the futures move before the order arrives. A retail order is therefore always the stale quote someone else picks off, never the sniper.

One caveat on dating: the newest ES–SPY numbers are from 2011, and no post-2020 study exists. Markets have only become faster since, so the conclusion stands. It also means any "futures lead SPY by a few seconds" pattern in retail bar data is almost certainly an artifact: timestamp misalignment between vendors, IEX quotes lagging the NBBO, bar-close sampling or bid-ask bounce. The ES tick (0.25 points) is coarser than SPY's ($0.01, about 0.10 index points), which alone keeps high-frequency correlation below 1 with no real lag at all ([BCS](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f69665.pdf)).

## Prediction markets lag by sub-seconds to seconds, and the venues fenced them off

### Crypto Up/Down markets: a 347 ms lag behind fees, delays and colocated makers

The best-measured lag in prediction markets is Polymarket's short-dated BTC markets against Binance. The OpenMarket dataset synchronizes **727M rows** to the millisecond (Feb–May 2026). Polymarket quotes respond to large Binance moves after a **median 347 ms**, and top-of-book spreads are a single tick. Yet a 43-feature walk-forward model did not beat the order-book-implied probability, and simulated trading lost about **−0.116 normalized payoff units per attempted trade** after fees and slippage ([arXiv 2607.26245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26245)). This is the most important result in this report: it is the only study with millisecond data, out-of-sample testing and costs, and its answer is negative.

The venue rules explain why. Polymarket added taker fees to 15-minute crypto markets on Jan 5 2026, explicitly "to redirect value from latency arbitrage toward genuine liquidity provision" ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/polymarket-introduces-dynamic-fees-to-curb-latency-arbitrage-in-short-term-crypto-markets/)). It then extended them to all crypto markets. The current formula is `fee = shares × 0.07 × p × (1−p)`: **1.75¢ per share at 50¢**, or 3.5% of the premium, with makers paying nothing and receiving rebates ([Polymarket fees](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees)). The taker speed bump has changed often. A 500 ms delay was quietly removed in February 2026 ([Protos](https://protos.com/polymarket-ends-trading-loophole-for-bitcoin-quants/)), then reappeared at 250 ms, was cut to 50 ms on Aug 17 and raised to **150 ms on Sep 4 2026**. During the delay an order is **pending and cannot be cancelled**, while resting makers can cancel freely ([changelog](https://docs.polymarket.com/changelog.md); [order lifecycle](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/order-lifecycle)). The bump is asymmetric on purpose: a taker who spots a stale quote gives the quote's owner 150 ms to pull it. KYC'd firms can colocate in AWS eu-west-2 London, next to the matching engine ([Polymarket geoblock](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/geoblock.md)).

Two more traps sit underneath. The 5-, 15-minute and 4-hour contracts settle on a **Chainlink** price, not Binance, so a Binance-lag trade carries basis risk ([arXiv 2606.31675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31675)). And the money that did flow on 5-minute markets in 2026 often went to manipulation rather than speed: **821 accounts made $8.2M** in likely manipulated settlement windows, with 93% of non-market-maker losses falling on retail. Polymarket then moved to 30–60 s TWAP settlement ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/08/07/how-a-five-second-trick-let-traders-drain-millions-from-polymarket)). A small, slow follower in these markets was more likely the victim than the winner.

### Sports: Kalshi leads by seven seconds, but the follower's gap rarely clears costs

Pantera Research studied all 282 games of the 2025 NFL season on both venues. Kalshi led the initial repricing in **about 80% of large moves, by a median of about 7 s**, rising to 86–87% for moves over 10 points ([Pantera Research Lab](https://panteraresearchlab.xyz/research/the-super-bowl-of-prediction-markets-kalshi-and-polymarkets-battle-for-price-vs-liquidity/)). Academic work agrees on the direction: Kalshi tends to lead in sports and Polymarket in politics ([SSRN 5331995](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5331995)). Seven seconds is a human-scale lag, so this looks like the best retail opportunity in the report. It is not, for three reasons. First, Polymarket delays marketable sports orders by **1–3 s, set per market**: 1 s on Premier League and none on NBA as of Oct 27 2026 ([Polymarket help](https://intercom.help/polymarket/en/articles/13364444-limit-orders); [startpolymarket](https://startpolymarket.com/strategies/in-play-trading/)). Second, Polymarket market makers can watch Kalshi as easily as anyone and requote within milliseconds. Third, the gaps that do appear are small. A live matcher covering about 98% of sports markets logged 870 cross-venue gaps in 24 hours, with a **median life of 9 s and 96% closed within 30 s**. On a $20.8M-volume World Cup match, total net arbitrage was **$439** ([DEV](https://dev.to/michaelmustopo/built-a-prediction-market-arbitrage-no-sizable-arbitrage-found-36cf)). Over 30 days of esports, Polymarket–Kalshi pairs were only **0.09%** of arbitrages found; most mispricings were against sportsbooks ([DEV](https://dev.to/dozor/polymarket-kalshi-arbitrage-vs-sportsbooks-30-days-of-esports-data-1dc2)). The real edge in Kalshi's 7 s lead belongs to whoever puts information onto Kalshi first, the fastest game-data feed, not the cross-venue copier.

### Same-event arbitrage between venues: the gaps sit at the fee wall

Fees on both venues follow the same parabola. Kalshi charges `ceil(0.07 × C × p × (1−p))` ([Kalshi fee schedule](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf)), and Polymarket's sports rate is 0.05. Buying both legs of a "locked" pair therefore needs a gap of about **3¢ near 50¢** to break even. A March–August 2026 census found 385 sustained gaps of at least 2¢, with a median peak of **2.9¢**; 54% peaked below break-even, and **none of the 12 largest raw gaps survived a freshness check**, because they were quotes up to 124 days old ([PredictMarketCap](https://predictmarketcap.com/analysis/prediction-market-arbitrage-study)). One monitor's 2¢ gap netted **−1.4¢** after real fees ([DEV](https://dev.to/botcookery/our-polymarket-kalshi-arbitrage-monitor-found-one-2c-gap-after-real-fees-it-lost-14c-41b7)). The two venues also settle edge cases such as postponed games differently, so a "hedged" pair is not risk-free ([Totalis](https://www.totalis.trade/blogs/prediction-markets/prediction-market-arbitrage)). The largest arbitrage pool ever measured on Polymarket, about **$40M** between Apr 2024 and Apr 2025, came from inside a single venue (YES+NO not summing to $1, and combinatorial mispricing), not from cross-venue speed ([arXiv 2508.03474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03474)).

### Stocks, options and sportsbooks: the slow pairs

Three pairs move at minute-to-hour speed. Polymarket BTC threshold contracts sat a mean **6.3 pp** from Binance-option-implied probabilities, with Polymarket the overpriced side. A delta-hedged trade stayed profitable after costs, but with marginal statistical precision ([arXiv 2606.19517](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19517v1)), and the gap's roughly four-hour half-life says Polymarket is the noisy follower. For sportsbooks against prediction markets the evidence is thin. In the 2024 election, order-book venues (Polymarket and Betfair) held about **85%** of information share and no dealer sportsbook exceeded 11% ([Aktuğ & Torul](https://academics.bogazici.edu.tr/torul/pridis.pdf)). Dealer books lead only where a sharp book like Pinnacle sets the line. That matches the repo's own sports work: `betting/trader.py` notes Polymarket tracked Pinnacle to within about 1¢ on the one sample so far. For Polymarket and Kalshi stock and index Up/Down markets against the live stock, **no study, dashboard or wallet analysis exists** (Benzinga reports only the prices, [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/markets/prediction-markets/26/11/63418031/stock-market-will-sp-500-open-up-or-down-today-81)). That gap is why the repo's lag study was worth running.

## The winners were early, colocated, and thinner-margined than advertised

Every profit figure in this space has survivorship bias: winners get publicized by X analysts and bot vendors, and losing wallets are never published. Read that way, the evidence tells a consistent story. Wallet **0x8dxd** traded 15-minute BTC/ETH/SOL Up/Down markets and was reported up **$658K** by January 2026 ([CoinDesk](https://x.com/CoinDesk/status/2010830430207939065)). The viral "$313 to $414K in a month, 98% win rate" claim comes from one X account and was never audited ([Yahoo/BeInCrypto](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/arbitrage-bots-dominate-polymarket-millions-100000888.html)). A later tracker snapshot puts the wallet at about **$2.37M realized on $201M of volume**, about 1.2% of volume, with its last trade around August 2026 ([predicts.guru](https://www.predicts.guru/checker/0x63ce342161250d705dc0b16df89036c8e5f9ba9a)). A 98% hit rate is what you get buying the side spot has already moved toward, at 90¢-plus prices; the 1.2% margin is the real edge. That margin is below today's 1.75¢-per-share taker fee at even odds, which is why the strategy belongs to the late-2025 window of zero fees and a maker-protecting delay.

The negative results come from developers with nothing to sell. One logged 176 resolved trades and found directional 15-minute crypto trading a losing game, with only buying both sides under $1 profitable ([DEV](https://dev.to/manja316/176-trades-on-polymarket-what-my-bot-actually-made-its-not-what-you-think-3iib)). Another team's directional strategy ran at **−37.81%** and was switched off ([LayerX](https://layerx.xyz/blog/polymarketbots)). A live 5-minute BTC test saw 2–4¢ slippage per token, with combined asks at or above $1.00 after fees ([Medium](https://medium.com/@gwrx2005/ai-augmented-arbitrage-in-short-duration-prediction-markets-live-trading-analysis-of-polymarkets-8ce1b8c5f362)). A bot that published its strategy was reportedly targeted and lost about $5K in 18 hours ([Polybot Arena](https://polybot-arena.com/blog/how-polymarket-bots-trade/)). And 100–150+ commercial bots now compete on the same crypto markets ([polbots.com](https://polbots.com/)). Over 2024–Nov 2026, the profit went to roughly four groups: equity HFT firms (structurally, every year), a handful of Polymarket taker bots in **late 2025 to January 2026**, settlement manipulators on 5-minute markets until the TWAP fix, and rebate-earning makers since. Retail takers were on the paying side in every period after January 2026.

## A retail lag-catcher is buildable for $150 a month, but it cannot win

Building the system is easy; winning with it is the hard part. The venues sit far apart. Polymarket international's matching engine is in **London** (AWS eu-west-2), Kalshi reportedly in **Ohio** (us-east-2, from one third-party monitor), Coinbase in **N. Virginia** (us-east-1), Binance probably in **Tokyo**, and Alpaca's data in New York with orders in Virginia ([Polymarket](https://docs.polymarket.com/developers/CLOB/geoblock); [Coinbase](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/exchange/introduction/systems-operations.md); [CCXT](https://docs.ccxt.com/blog/how-far-is-your-exchange); [Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/alpaca-data-center-locations/5640)). Binance.com also returns HTTP 451 to US IPs, so a US system cannot even read it legitimately; Coinbase is the US-accessible crypto leader. The table puts the measured pieces together. Rows marked "est." are my estimates from fibre physics and the cited figures.

| Leg | Home PC, US East (Python) | $5–50/mo VPS in the right region | Colocated incumbent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Network RTT to Polymarket int'l (London) | ~110 ms TTFB from US-East ([DEV](https://dev.to/bluewhale-quant-lab/i-measured-polymarkets-api-latency-from-5-regions-heres-the-script-and-the-results-3mjm)) | ~1–6 ms from Dublin/Amsterdam | sub-ms in eu-west-2 |
| Network RTT to Kalshi (Ohio) / Coinbase (VA) | ~25–50 ms / ~10–40 ms (est.) | ~1–15 ms; Coinbase p50 13.6 ms from US-East ([DEV](https://dev.to/fillbench/i-benchmarked-9-crypto-exchange-apis-from-a-us-datacenter-p50p95p99-45dm)) | PrivateLink (Kalshi Premier+) |
| Client stack | Official Python client 1.37 s per market fetch vs 322 ms in Rust ([polyfill-rs](https://docs.rs/crate/polyfill-rs/0.2.2)); 340 ms signal→order in TypeScript ([DEV](https://dev.to/casatrick/my-polymarket-trading-bot-in-rust-after-typescript-kept-missing-fills-4ena)) | 5–30 ms per leg in tuned asyncio (est.) | µs–low ms |
| Venue-imposed delay (taker) | 150 ms crypto, 0–3 s sports, uncancellable | same | makers exempt |
| Realistic signal→fill | **~0.5–1.5 s** | **~0.2–0.4 s** on Polymarket crypto | **≪ 347 ms** (the median reprice they cause) |

The conclusion follows from the last row. The lag being chased (347 ms in crypto) is shorter than a home PC's reaction time and roughly equal to a good VPS's once the 150 ms bump is added, and the quotes being chased belong to makers who can cancel during that bump. For lags of about 10 s, a same-region VPS is ample and even home is fine, provided jitter, reconnects and garbage-collection pauses are handled. For lags of 60 s or more, network latency is irrelevant. Signal quality, fees and fill probability decide everything.

Costs are modest. A small VPS runs **$5–6 a month** ([bestusavps](https://bestusavps.pages.dev/reviews/vultr)). Full SIP equity data is **$99 a month** on Alpaca Algo Trader Plus ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/market-data)) or $199 on Massive ([Massive](https://massive.com/pricing)). Kalshi and Polymarket data is free with an account. A two-region stack (us-east-1 plus eu-west-1 or us-east-2) with SIP comes to about **$110–180 a month**. The engineering pattern is standard. Run one asyncio task per websocket feeding a queue, with heartbeats inside each venue's idle cutoff: Polymarket requires a PING every 10 s ([Polymarket WS](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-data/websocket/overview)). Reconnect with backoff, resync from a REST snapshot, and do not trade until resynced. Log the venue timestamp and both local wall-clock and monotonic receipt times on every message. On EC2, chrony with Amazon Time Sync gives microsecond clocks, which makes one-way latency measurable ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/compute/its-about-time-microsecond-accurate-clocks-on-amazon-ec2-instances)). A Windows home PC on W32Time does not, so measure round trips there and treat one-way numbers below about 20 ms as noise.

The legal constraints narrow the venues more than latency does. Polymarket's own documentation lists the US as **"Close-Only on Frontend and API"** ([Polymarket geoblock](https://docs.polymarket.com/developers/CLOB/geoblock)). Since May 2026 it reportedly blocks known VPN ranges and freezes suspicious accounts, with no US regulator to appeal to ([CardPlayer](https://www.cardplayer.com/poker-news/1664455-like-post-black-friday-online-poker-sites-polymarket-cracks-down-on-vpns)). Reading its public book is fine; opening positions from the US is not. The compliant venues are **Polymarket US**, a CFTC-designated exchange with a KYC'd Ed25519 API limited to 20 requests/s per IP and a separate order book ([DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/polymarket-launches-public-api-unlocks-permissionless-liquidity/)), and **Kalshi**, whose API and FIX are first-class products (Basic tier: about 10 order writes per second) ([Kalshi rate limits](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/rate_limits)). Sports contracts on both depend on the user's state. Polymarket US was reported unavailable in AZ, IL, MA, MD, MI, MT, NJ, NV and OH as of mid-2026 ([pm.wiki](https://pm.wiki/hi/learn/how-to-use-polymarket-in-the-us)). Kalshi faces a circuit split after the 9th and 6th Circuits sided with the states and the 3rd Circuit against them ([PYMNTS](https://pymnts.com/legal/2026/a-second-federal-appeals-court-rules-sports-event-contracts-are-not-swaps); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/396437/kalshi-wins-appeal-new-jersey-sports-event-contracts)). Both are CFTC venues, where spoofing and wash trading are illegal. The CFTC named them 2026 enforcement priorities with possible criminal referrals ([Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/the-cftc-is-watching-enforcement-director-outlines-new-priorities)). A bot therefore needs self-trade prevention and must never churn to earn volume-based tiers. There is no IRS guidance on event-contract tax treatment; budget for ordinary income rates ([CoinLedger](https://coinledger.io/blog/prediction-markets-tax)).

## The repo's two minute-level studies are still running

Both studies were pre-registered on 2026-11-20 and were still computing when this report was written. `reports/lag_results.md` and `reports/pm_close_results.md` contain only their protocols, the data caches were last written after midnight, and two Python processes started at 00:33–00:34 ET were still busy. Neither has produced a statistic, so nothing below is a result.

`lag_study.py` asks the one question no outside source answers: does Polymarket's daily "<ticker> Up or Down" price lag the live stock at one-minute resolution? It computes a parameter-free fair value, `Φ(ln(S/prev close) / (σ·√(T/390)))`, from Alpaca SIP bars for TSLA, NVDA, AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META, SPCX, SPY and QQQ. It then tests whether the Polymarket price closes the gap to that value over 1, 5 and 15 minutes, using a bounce-robust variant, and whether Polymarket moves predict the stock. The decisive test is a fixed paper rule: buy when the gap exceeds 5 pp with at least 30 minutes left, fill at the *next* minute's price plus 2¢, and exit at convergence or resolution. The pass rule is Holm-corrected p below 0.05 with the same sign in both halves of the sample. The pre-registration warns that the raw gap-closing slope will look positive mechanically, because noise in the quote reverts. Only the next-minute paper rule after costs answers "exploitable". If it passes, it is the first evidence of a retail-reachable lag in this whole map, at minute scale, where a home PC is fast enough. If it fails, the stock–Polymarket pair joins the closed rows above. Note one limit: the fee schedule for these markets was unverified and the rule assumes no fee. Under the 0.04 finance coefficient, a 50¢ entry plus exit adds about 2¢, so a marginal pass should be re-run with fees before it counts.

`pm_close_study.py` asks whether pre-open odds (09:25, 10:00 and 15:00 ET) carry information beyond a zero-drift random walk from the pre-market price, and whether the excess predicts the tradable open-to-close return. Its paper rule goes long at the open only when the odds exceed 60%, with 2 bp per side, and is compared against always-long. The prior written into the protocol is a null: the market resolves against the *previous* close, so most of the 09:25 price should just restate the overnight gap. A pass would give the frozen stock model's owner a candidate *daily* overlay for a future review, never an in-place change. A null would confirm the earlier finding that the odds price events, not entry points.

## Conclusion

The question "where are the lags?" has a cleaner answer than expected: lags exist at every timescale, but each one is owned by whoever controls the layer it lives in. Microsecond lags belong to firms that own microwave towers. Sub-second prediction-market lags belong to the venue, which set the fees, the speed bumps and the colocation rules and changed them at least eight times in 2026. Seconds-scale sports lags belong to whoever has the fastest game feed. For a retail system, a lag is only worth chasing when its half-life is long enough that pricing matters more than speed, and at that point it is not a latency trade but a valuation trade that happens to be time-sensitive. Polymarket's 2026 rule changes also give a practical warning: a venue that can change taker delays overnight can erase an edge built on milliseconds before it repays the engineering.

For this user the concrete plan follows. Build nothing sub-second. Keep the Alpaca stock model frozen, and let the two running studies decide whether a minute-scale stock–Polymarket signal deserves its own paper sleeve. Keep running the sports trader dry, but recognize what it is: a minutes-scale sharp-line strategy whose costs are Polymarket's 0.05 sports fee on both entry and exit (about **2.5¢ round trip at 50¢**) plus spread. Its entry margin must clear that, and its fair value is only as fresh as the Odds API budget allows. Before any live step, re-point execution away from `clob.polymarket.com`, which is close-only for a US person, to Polymarket US or Kalshi after checking that the user's state allows sports contracts. A minute-loop on the home PC is adequate for all of this. The only upgrade worth paying for is a **$5–6 Linux VPS in us-east-1** for clean timestamps and uptime, and it is worth it only if a pre-registered paper result says there is something to time.
