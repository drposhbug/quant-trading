# Options pay structure rents, not prediction premiums

For a retail system trading options on US mega-cap tech in 2026, the math is settled and cheap to implement. The edge is not in the math. Black-Scholes-Merton with a dividend yield prices these names almost exactly; what decides P&L is the volatility you feed it and the spread you pay to trade it. The variance risk premium that makes option-selling famous is large and well documented for **indexes** but thin, often statistically insignificant, and mostly eaten by bid-ask costs for **single stocks** like NVDA or MSFT. Earnings options on mega-caps look close to fairly priced: one top-50-firm study finds announcement straddles lose about 8% per event, another finds pre-announcement straddles *gain* 3.34%, and recent vendor data swings between seasons. Buying options pays when you are buying a specific structure, such as cheap financing through deep-in-the-money LEAPS on low-volatility names, or insurance for a dated risk. It does not pay as a forecasting tool: retail buyers lose systematically, mostly to spreads. Among selling rules, only "sell index puts and accept equity-like drawdowns" has long-run evidence. The tastylive package (45 DTE, 50% profit, 21 DTE, 2x stop) has no independent cost-inclusive support, and covered calls on momentum names lag their stock. This repo already confirmed the skeptic's view twice: a 20% Cboe PutWrite sleeve failed its bootstrap test, and the stock-level earnings premium failed. The live earnings iron-butterfly experiment should be read the same way. Its expected value after the costs it deliberately pays is roughly zero to slightly negative, a year of trades cannot tell skill from luck, and its real value is as a test of the order, assignment and logging pipeline.

## Pricing is solved; the volatility input is the whole game

The pricing engine for these eleven names is Black-Scholes-Merton with a continuous dividend yield q: d1 = [ln(S/K) + (r − q + σ²/2)T]/(σ√T), d2 = d1 − σ√T, call = S·e^(−qT)N(d1) − K·e^(−rT)N(d2), put = K·e^(−rT)N(−d2) − S·e^(−qT)N(−d1) ([Wikipedia: Black-Scholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black%E2%80%93Scholes_model)). Put-call parity, C − P = S·e^(−qT) − K·e^(−rT), becomes a pair of bounds for American options. Its practical use is backing out the implied forward and borrow cost from call and put mids ([Wikipedia: put-call parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Put%E2%80%93call_parity)). Listed single-stock options are American, but that matters less than it sounds. TSLA, AMD and AMZN pay no dividend, so early exercise of their calls is never optimal. For the dividend payers (AAPL, MSFT, AVGO, ORCL, META, GOOGL, TSM, and NVDA's token dividend), early call exercise is rational only just before an ex-date and only when the call's remaining extrinsic value is below the dividend. Deep-ITM puts carry a real early-exercise premium at 2024–26 interest rates near 4%. A binomial tree or the Barone-Adesi–Whaley approximation handles both cases, and OptionMetrics already computes its academic IVs with a tree, so published IV figures are early-exercise adjusted.

The Greeks follow directly ([Wikipedia: Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_(finance))). Delta is e^(−qT)N(d1) for a call. Gamma is e^(−qT)n(d1)/(Sσ√T) and peaks at the money as expiry nears. Vega is S·e^(−qT)n(d1)√T. Theta's dominant term is −S·e^(−qT)n(d1)σ/(2√T). The identity that matters for strategy is the one hidden in the pricing PDE: with r = q = 0, **Θ = −½ΓS²σ²_implied**. Theta is the rent paid for gamma. A delta-hedged long option earns ½∫Γ·S²·(σ²_realized − σ²_implied)dt over its life, so its P&L is a bet that realized variance beats implied variance, weighted by dollar gamma along the path. Every option strategy below is some version of that bet, with or without a directional view attached.

A worked example makes the magnitudes concrete. These are illustrative inputs computed in the research notes, not live quotes: NVDA at $180, 30-day ATM, σ = 45%, r = 4%.

| Quantity | Value | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Call / put / straddle | $9.54 / $8.95 / $18.49 | Straddle = **10.3% of spot**, close to the 0.8·σ·√T rule of thumb |
| Delta call / put | 0.536 / −0.464 | |
| Gamma | 0.0171 per $1 | |
| Vega | $0.205 per vol point per share | $20.50 per contract |
| Theta call | −$0.163 per calendar day | Of which $0.154 is pure gamma rent (½ΓS²σ²/365) |
| Breakeven daily move | $4.24 (**2.36%**) | A delta-hedged long call profits only on days NVDA moves more than this |
| Same option at 7 DTE / 1 DTE | Γ 0.036 / 0.094; Θ −$0.32 / −$0.85 per day | Both scale with 1/√T, so short-dated P&L variance explodes |

The straddle also translates into the market's "expected move". If the move is normal with standard deviation s, E|X| = s·√(2/π) ≈ 0.8s. An ATM straddle therefore prices the expected *absolute* move, and the move's standard deviation is about 1.25 times the straddle. Mixing these two up is the most common error in implied-versus-actual move comparisons. The engineering takeaway is short: the model is not where errors come from. Errors come from the volatility input (skew, term structure, the earnings jump) and from theta conventions. Brokers mix 365-day and 252-day theta, and a backtest that compares collected theta to realized moves must use one convention throughout.

## The single-stock volatility premium is thin, and earnings are roughly fair

### Index variance is expensive because it insures correlation, not single stocks

Carr and Wu's model-free variance-swap study is the reference measurement. The log variance risk premium (realized over implied variance) averaged **−0.594 for the S&P 500 (t = −9.5)** but only −0.277 for MSFT, −0.151 for AMZN (t = −2.0) and −0.081 for ORCL (t = −1.7) over 1996–2003. In variance units the premium was insignificant for all but 3 of 35 stocks ([Carr & Wu, RFS 2009](https://engineering.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/2019-01/CarrReviewofFinStudiesMarch2009-a.pdf)). Driessen, Maenhout and Vilkov explain the gap. Index options are expensive because they hedge *correlation* spikes, while individual variance risk is not priced on average. Their dispersion trade (sell index variance, buy component variance) earns a high frictionless alpha that individual-option bid-ask spreads largely eliminate ([DMV, JF 2009](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:64:y:2009:i:3:p:1377-1406)). Delta-hedged equity options do lose money on average, and the losses grow with idiosyncratic volatility ([Cao & Han, JFE 2013](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:108:y:2013:i:1:p:231-249)). Cao and Han read that as dealers charging more to make markets in hard-to-hedge names, not as a free premium. The practical conclusion from the academic side is blunt. Selling single-name variance on mega-cap tech harvests mostly the *systematic* slice of the premium at worse liquidity than SPX. Someone who wants to be paid for selling volatility gets more premium per dollar of cost from index options.

Costs decide the remainder. Muravyev and Pearson find that traders who time their executions pay effective spreads **under 40% of conventional measures**, while the average quoted spread on S&P 500 stock options is about 17% of the option price, a figure pulled up by cheap OTM contracts ([Muravyev & Pearson, RFS 2020](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v33y2020i11p4973-5014..html); [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/timing-option-trades-to-suppress-trading-frictions/)). Traders who do not time their orders still pay the full half-spread. A one-to-three vol-point edge on a 30-day straddle worth 10% of spot can vanish into a 5–17% spread unless orders are worked near mid. The skeptic's caveat on vintage applies too. Nearly all of the peer-reviewed VRP evidence ends by the mid-2010s, before retail call-buying waves, 0DTE growth and the AI-driven volatility regime in NVDA, AMD and AVGO. No 2023–2026 single-stock VRP estimate for these eleven names exists in the sources found. It has to be measured (IV30 against subsequent RV30, with and without earnings windows) before anyone trades on it.

### Skew and term structure carry pricing information, not a mega-cap signal

Equity skew (OTM put IV above ATM above OTM call IV) reflects the negative spot-volatility correlation and crash risk. In the cross-section, the steepest-smirk quintile underperformed the flattest by **about 10.9% a year in 1996–2005**, with predictability lasting six months and steep-smirk firms delivering worse earnings surprises ([Xing, Zhang & Zhao, JFQA 2010](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/what-does-the-individual-option-volatility-smirk-tell-us-about-future-equity-returns/ECFD16BA9ACBDC8D577D1BD866FBEA72)). A January 2026 Illinois study argues that stock borrowing fees explain away option-based return predictors ([Gies](https://giesbusiness.illinois.edu/news/2026/01/07/study--borrowing-fee-is-the-variable-that-debunks-theory-that-options-predict-future-stock-returns)). Its details were not retrieved, but the mechanism points the same way for mega-caps: borrow is cheap and options liquidity is enormous, so informed-trader concentration is diluted. Skew belongs in the pricing of put spreads and risk reversals, not in a directional signal for AAPL. Term structure is normally upward-sloping and inverts in stress. For single stocks, scheduled earnings dominate the shape: each expiry spanning the event carries a variance "lump", and the curve slopes down into the announcement.

### Earnings jumps can be read off the curve, and the evidence on their price conflicts

Dubinsky, Johannes, Kaeck and Seeger (DJKS) model the earnings move as a jump with known timing. Annualized implied variance for an expiry T spanning one event is σ_T² = σ_d² + σ_Q²/T, where σ_d is diffusive volatility and σ_Q the one-day jump volatility. Two expiries that both include the event identify the jump: **σ_Q² = (σ₁² − σ₂²)/(1/T₁ − 1/T₂)** ([DJKS, RFS 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v32y2019i2p646-687..html); [NYU slides](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/NYU%20conference%20Johannes%20final.pdf)). With 7-day IV at 90% and 35-day IV at 55%, σ_Q is 11.0%, the priced expected absolute move is about 8.8%, and the implied diffusive vol is about 42%. IV crush is just the σ_Q²/T term disappearing after the report. It is mechanical and already priced, because post-event diffusive vol is forecastable from the back months. Selling into the crush profits only if the realized jump is smaller than σ_Q.

Whether σ_Q is too high for mega-caps is genuinely contested, and the weight of evidence leans only slightly toward "overpriced":

| Evidence | Universe and period | Finding | Weight for this universe |
|---|---|---|---|
| [DJKS, RFS 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v32y2019i2p646-687..html) | 50 most option-active firms, ~2000–2015 | Implied jump vol exceeds realized; announcement straddles return **≈ −8% per event**; implied earnings vol has 85% correlation with realized | High (mega-cap-heavy), but old |
| [Gao, Xing & Zhang, JFQA 2018](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/anticipating-uncertainty-straddles-around-earnings-announcements/7B34877AD5E06304BA3C55FBA3219FDD) | All optionable firms | Straddles bought 3 days before earnings and held to the announcement earn **+3.34%** | Low: effect concentrates in small, illiquid, high-kurtosis firms and is weakest in names like Apple ([BSIC](https://bsic.it/straddling-outside-and-into-earnings/)) |
| [de Silva, Smith & So, RoF 2025](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/losing-optional-retail-option-trading-expected-announcement) | 32,791 announcements, 2010–2021 | Retail buyers lose 5–9% around announcements, **10–14%** for high-expected-volatility events | High for NVDA/TSLA-type attention names |
| [ORATS, Aug 2025](https://orats.com/blog/nvidia-earnings-options-market-expectations-vs-reality) | NVDA, 12 quarters | Average implied move 7.7% vs average actual 7.6%, "remarkably accurate on average" | Vendor; small sample |
| [ORATS, Feb 2026](https://orats.com/blog/earnings-straddles-strong-season-2026) | Reporting US firms, 4 weeks to 2026-02-11 | Straddles averaged **+45%** in that season vs a trailing 12-quarter average of about −2% | Vendor; shows regime swings |
| [EarningsWatcher](https://earnings-watcher.com/wiki/options-implied-move-vs-actual-earnings-study-2026) | ~29,000 US reports, 2023–2026 | Average implied ±9.18% vs realized ±6.32% | Vendor; all-cap, methodology unclear |

One widely repeated statistic does not belong in this table. The claim that **NVDA's implied move exceeded its actual move in 14 of the last 20 quarters**, attributed to Cboe LiveVol, appears only on spam and mirror domains with no methodology. It is unverified and is not used here. An earlier report in this repo cited it; that citation should be treated as withdrawn. The defensible NVDA statement is ORATS's: implied and realized *averages* were nearly equal. Practitioner tallies say the actual move beats the implied move in roughly 25–45% of mega-cap reports, and NVDA's beats have overshot by several points ([Earnings Watcher](https://earnings-watcher.com/wiki/earnings-expected-moves)). A high win rate for short earnings vol and positive expectancy are therefore different claims, and the first does not imply the second. The academic, practitioner and vendor views converge on three points: the earnings premium is positive on average for heavily traded names, smaller now than in 2000–2015, and fat-tailed enough that single seasons flip sign.

## Buying options pays for structure, not for forecasts

### LEAPS are cheap financing bundled with insurance you may not want

Put-call parity splits a deep-ITM call's extrinsic value into two parts. The first is interest on the strike, charged at roughly the box-spread rate ([CME](https://www.cmegroup.com/articles/2024/index-options-box-spreads-as-financing-tool.html)). The second is an embedded put. With the FOMC at 3.75–4.00% in October 2026 ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/10/28/feds-interest-rate-decision-october-28-2026)), the financing part costs about 3.9%. That beats Schwab and Fidelity margin at about **11.6%** at $25K and roughly ties IBKR Pro at 5.38% or Robinhood at 5.25% ([tradingcosts aggregator](https://tradingcosts.labs.searchatlas.com/margin-interest-rates-brokers-2026/); [IBKR](https://www.interactivebrokers.com.sg/en/trading/margin.php)). The insurance part depends on the stock's IV. The research notes priced 0.80-delta calls under Black-Scholes, assuming typical-regime IVs rather than live chains:

| Case (S = 100) | Tenor | Call | Leverage | Extrinsic | Interest part | Embedded put | Extrinsic per $ financed per year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NVDA-like, IV 45% | 1.0y | 30.41 | 2.6x | 9.41 | 3.02 | 6.42 | **13.5%** |
| MSFT-like, IV 24%, 0.8% yield | 1.0y | 18.94 | 4.2x | 5.44 | 3.31 | 2.93 (+0.8 dividends forgone) | **6.7%** |

Calling a LEAPS a "13% financing rate" is wrong. The excess over the risk-free rate is insurance, and on TSLA, NVDA and AMD that insurance is expensive. The decision rule is arithmetic: compare extrinsic/(S − C)/T with your actual margin rate. On low-IV names (MSFT, AAPL, GOOGL) LEAPS are a reasonable margin substitute. On high-IV names, cheap margin or plain stock is cleaner unless you specifically value the capped loss and the absence of margin calls. The decay curve explains the "roll with 6–9 months left" convention: a 2-year NVDA-like LEAPS held at flat spot keeps 4.4 of its 15.8 extrinsic at six months and 1.9 at three. That makes the convention sensible, but the exact delta and roll dates are practitioner folklore with no tested edge. The poor man's covered call (short near-dated calls against a LEAPS) inherits both the LEAPS insurance cost and the covered-call timing drag described below. No peer-reviewed study of it exists, and its short leg carries dividend early-assignment risk on the payers ([Option Strategist](https://optionstrategist.com/node/6671)). One unglamorous fact frames all call buying: a call equals stock plus a protective put minus borrowed cash, so every call buyer is a protective-put buyer.

### Protective puts lose to simply holding less stock

Cboe's PPUT index (S&P 500 plus a monthly 5% OTM put) had **18 monthly declines of 6% or more versus 35** for the S&P 500 over 1986–2021, a −38.9% maximum drawdown versus −51.0%, and beat other stock indices by more than 10 points in 2008 and 2020 ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/benchmark-indices-series-hedging-downside-exposure-with-pput-cll-and-cllz-indices)). The skeptic's answer is Israelov's "Pathetic Protection": matched to the same expected return, a statically de-risked portfolio has lower drawdowns, lower volatility and a higher Sharpe than a put-protected one. Puts win only when a crash lands inside the option's life, and the variance premium is the cost ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/pathetic-protection/); [Hedge Fund Alpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/strategies/pathetic-protection-via-protective-puts/)). An independent check found a beta-matched index-plus-cash mix matched PPUT's return with lower volatility ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/do-protective-equity-index-puts-work-well/)). AQR prefers trend-following to puts as a tail hedge on long-run cost ([AQR](https://aqr.com/Insights/Research/White-Papers/Tail-Risk-Hedging-Contrasting-Put-and-Trend-Strategies)). Universa's 3,612% Q1-2020 return comes from tiny, far-OTM, institutionally executed positions, and FactorResearch found tail-risk funds and cash had similar risk and return over 2007–2020 ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2020/05/06/an-aqr-warning-and-a-3-612-return-fire-up-the-black-swan-debate); [follow-up](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2020/07/13/empirical-support-for-tail-risk-strategies)). Collars swap the premium for forgone upside. On momentum mega-caps, the right tail is the return, so a 110% cap is expensive even at zero net premium. This repo reached the same conclusion from the other direction. Its stock model's drawdown control was statistically indistinguishable from holding about 64% of the equal-weight basket and the rest in cash ([MATH.md](../MATH.md)). For a dated, known risk window, an index put or collar is defensible. As a permanent overlay, sizing down is cheaper.

### Retail option buyers lose, mostly to spreads and timing

Retail lost about **$1.14 billion** on options from November 2019 to June 2021, concentrated in cheap, short-dated, lottery-like contracts, with bid-ask costs the main driver ([Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya, JF 2023](https://www.london.edu/faculty-and-research/academic-research/r/retail-trading-in-options-and-the-rise-of-the-big-three-wholesalers)). In SPX 0DTE, more than $50 million of over $70 million in measured retail losses was transaction cost ([BNN Bloomberg on Beckmeyer et al.](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options-1.1910634)). Earnings purchases are the worst-documented pattern: buy a week early, overpay relative to realized volatility, pay a wide spread, exit two weeks late ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/how-retail-losses-money-in-option-trading/)). The only academically supported "buy" signal is cross-sectional: buy options where implied volatility is low *relative to the stock's own historical volatility* and sell where it is high ([Goyal & Saretto, JFE 2009](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:94:y:2009:i:2:p:310-326)). It is a delta-hedged, long-short, institutional strategy. It is cost-sensitive, and one replication finds it disappears with real-time IV estimates ([NUS RMI](https://rmi.nus.edu.sg/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/30.-Guanglian-Hu.pdf)). Platform "IV rank below 30" rules are a loose, untested cousin of it. For retail, the closest thing to non-negative expected value is liquid, long-dated, deep-ITM contracts bought with limit orders near mid and held for exposure. That cuts every documented loss channel, but it lowers costs rather than creating alpha.

## Short premium earns an equity-like return, and the management rules are folklore

### Index put-writing is lower-beta equity, which this repo has already measured

The Cboe PutWrite index (PUT) is the longest-run evidence for systematic option selling. Over 1986–2015 it compounded **10.1% against 9.8%** for the S&P 500 at two-thirds the volatility (Sharpe 0.67 vs 0.47, gross, with pre-2007 history back-constructed). In 2006–2015 it lagged, 6.6% versus 7.1%, with a −33% drawdown against −51% ([CXO on Bondarenko](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/performance-of-cboe-putwrite-indexes); [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/white-paper-shows-volatility-risk-premium-facilitated-higher-risk-adjusted-returns-for-put-index)). ETFs that track these indices have underperformed them, which is the best available measure of real-world cost drag ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/performance-of-cboe-putwrite-indexes)). This repo's own test is harsher and more recent. **PUT over 2007–2026 earned 7.1% CAGR at a 0.56 Sharpe versus 10.7% and 0.62 for the S&P 500 total return**, lost 35% in 2008 and 29% in March 2020, and a 20% PUT sleeve did not improve the stock model (Sharpe 1.24 vs 1.26, bootstrap P = 0.72) ([MATH.md](../MATH.md)). Put-writing is a lower-beta way to hold equity with a crash-shaped left tail. It is not a free premium layered on top of equity.

Defined-risk index structures trade return for smaller drawdowns, but not uniformly. Cboe's iron condor index (CNDR) had a **−19%** worst drawdown over about 35 years and returned 0–2% in 59% of months. The ATM butterfly index (BFLY), the closest index cousin of an iron fly, drew down **−47.1%**, close to the S&P 500's −51% ([Cboe Insights](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/benchmark-indices-series-volatility-management-with-cboes-bfly-and-cndr-indices/)). Short-ATM structures with 5% wings are therefore not low-risk just because their loss per trade is capped. Cboe's own documents disagree on whether CNDR shorts 0.15 or 0.20 delta, so the methodology should be checked before anyone replicates it ([2015 launch release](https://ir.cboe.com/news/news-details/2015/CBOE-Introduces-10-Options-Based-Strategy-Performance-Benchmark-Indexes-07-29-2015/default.aspx)).

### Covered calls and the wheel sell exactly the months that matter on NVDA and TSLA

AQR's decomposition is the decisive academic result. Covered-call returns combine passive equity, short volatility, and an **uncompensated equity-timing bet**: the short call's delta grows as the market rallies. That timing leg contributes more than three times the risk of the short-vol leg, and hedging it out raised Sharpe from 0.37 to 0.52 ([Israelov & Nielsen, FAJ 2015](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Covered-Calls-Uncovered)). The result holds across 11 global indices ([Israelov, Klein & Tummala](https://risk.net/journal-of-risk/5943741/covering-the-world-global-evidence-on-covered-calls)). Live single-stock products confirm it after costs. NVDY trailed NVDA by about 6 points over a trailing year, captured roughly half of NVDA's strong months, and still fell 21% in August–December 2025 ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/options/articles/why-nvdy-shareholders-miss-half-174004944.html)). TSLY returned 55% since inception against TSLA's 86% ([Kalkine](https://kalkine.com/news/etfs/yieldmax-tsla-etf-misses-about-30-of-teslas-rally-on-call-strategy)) and was down about 39% year-to-date by August 2026 ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/etf/2026/08/21/tsly-pays-more-each-week-than-nvdy-but-holders-are-losing-money-faster/)). These funds carry fees and synthetic structures, so they are imperfect evidence, but they are live outcomes rather than vendor backtests. The wheel is a put-write followed by a covered call. It inherits the PUT index's equity-like tail and AQR's timing drag, its delta exposure depends on the assignment path, and no study of it as a strategy exists.

### The management rules, sorted by evidence

The 45-DTE, 16-delta, 50%-profit, 21-DTE package comes from tastylive's in-house SPY studies (about 2005–2017, mostly gross). Those studies report win rate and profit per day, metrics that early exits inflate mechanically ([tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/managing-winners-varying-profit-targets-12-04-2015); [21 DTE](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/best-practices/episodes/managing-21-days-early-02-25-2020)). tastylive is a broker-owned media platform that profits from turnover. The one independent replication found, on SPX from 2001 to 2020 with no costs, had the managed 45/50%/21 strangle **underperform** an unmanaged 30-DTE strangle closed at 5 DTE: Sharpe 0.52 against 0.67 ([SteadyOptions](https://steadyoptions.com/articles/does-%E2%80%9Cmanaging-winners%E2%80%9D-add-value-to-short-strangles-r618)). That test changed entry DTE and exit rule together, so it is suggestive rather than decisive.

| Rule | Status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Sell index rather than single-name variance | Evidence | Carr-Wu, DMV: single-stock VRP thin; index VRP is a correlation premium |
| Work orders near mid; never cross four spreads casually | Evidence | Muravyev-Pearson; retail-loss studies |
| Defined risk (wings) for event trades | Arithmetic | Caps the gap loss; BFLY shows it does not cap drawdowns |
| Exit before expiry week (21 DTE, or closing before expiry) | Plausible mechanism | Cuts gamma; tastylive's own data credit it with lower volatility, not higher return |
| ~45 DTE entry | Weak | Spintwig found diminishing returns past ~45 DTE ([ERN](https://earlyretirementnow.com/2020/06/17/passive-income-through-option-writing-part-5)); not out-of-sample |
| 50% profit take | Folklore | Raises win rate; independent test neutral to negative |
| 16 vs 30 delta | Folklore | More delta is more equity beta, not more edge |
| 2x-credit stop | Folklore | No study; turns mean-reverting vol spikes into realized losses |
| Enter only at high IV rank | Folklore | No published result, including from ORATS's own backtester ([ORATS](https://orats.com/blog/backtester-upgrade-signals-stats)) |
| Rolling losers | Folklore | Changes delta without being priced (AQR timing critique) |

## Paper fills, taxes and data decide whether any of this can be tested

### Costs and Alpaca's mechanics

No primary source publishes spread as a percentage of mid by delta and expiry for these tickers, so cost models have to start from tick rules and be verified against a quote snapshot. Penny-program classes quote in $0.01 below $3 and $0.05 above ([MarketData.app](https://www.marketdata.app/education/options/option-tick-sizes/)). An ATM straddle on NVDA or AAPL sits in $0.05 ticks, typically one to three ticks wide, and the wings are penny-ticked. The research notes estimate the summed spread of a four-leg iron fly at about $0.15–$0.50, so crossing every spread on entry and exit costs **3–10% of a typical $5–8 credit**. Earnings-week and morning-after quotes are usually wider, so the real figure for this repo's trade sits at the upper end or above.

Alpaca's options API supports market, limit, stop and stop-limit orders, with day or GTC time-in-force and whole-contract quantities only ([Alpaca options](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/options-trading)). Multi-leg orders use `order_class: "mleg"` with up to four legs, and the price sign is easy to get wrong. The API reference states that **a positive limit_price is a debit and a negative one a credit**, while every example on the Level 3 page uses positive prices ([order reference](https://docs.alpaca.markets/reference/postorder); [Level 3](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/options-level-3-trading)). The repo's bot sends credits as negative prices, which matches the reference. Leg ratios must have a greatest common divisor of 1, equity legs are not allowed, and naked short legs are rejected inside an mleg. Level 3 margin uses theoretical maximum loss. Exercise and assignment create operational traps. Contracts $0.01 or more in the money are auto-exercised at expiry. Positions without buying power are liquidated in the last hour. Assignments are not pushed over the websocket and, in paper, appear in account activities only the next day.

Paper fills are the crux for this repo. Alpaca matches paper orders against the NBBO only once they are marketable: **buys fill at the ask, sells at the bid**, with no price improvement, no queue position and no size check, and 10% of orders get random partial fills ([Alpaca paper trading](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/paper-trading); [forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/does-a-paid-account-give-more-accurate-paper-market-fills/14303)). Users report fills arriving 50–260 seconds after the price crossed ([forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/paper-trading-fill-delays-of-50-260-seconds-limit-orders-filled-minutes-after-price-crossed/18223)). Alpaca does not document how it judges an mleg order marketable. The free `indicative` options feed is derived from OPRA but is not the actual quotes. Alpaca staff say it "is meant primarily for testing" and should not drive live trading, while real OPRA quotes require Algo Trader Plus at about $99 a month ([historical data docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/historical-option-data); [forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/paper-trading-options-pricing/17795)). Paper is therefore pessimistic on price, because live complex orders often fill near mid, and optimistic on size. It is not a cost model either way. The only fix is to log the NBBO at submission and at fill and compare the two.

### Taxes favor index options, which Alpaca does not appear to offer

Single-stock and ETF options, including SPY and QQQ, are taxed as ordinary capital gains and are subject to wash-sale rules. Broad-based index options such as SPX and NDX are Section 1256 contracts. They are taxed **60% long-term / 40% short-term regardless of holding period**, marked to market at year-end, reported on Form 6781, and exempt from wash sales ([IRS Form 6781](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/f6781--2023.pdf); [TaxSlayer](https://support.taxslayerpro.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040712794)). At top federal rates that is a blended 26.8% against 37%, about ten points of after-tax return on a short-dated strategy. SPX and NDX are also European and cash-settled, which removes early assignment and pin risk. The research found no sign that Alpaca offers index options, so an index version of any strategy here means a second broker. Straddle-rule loss deferral (Section 1092) and IRA options permissions were not verified.

### Honest backtests need point-in-time chains with bid and ask

Alpaca's options history starts in **February 2024**, too short for any 2016–2026 test ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/historical-option-data)). The value options are an ORATS near-EOD backfill from 2007 for a one-time $599 ([ORATS](https://orats.com/near-eod-data)), Databento OPRA cbbo-1m from 2013 at about $2 per GB ([Databento](https://databento.com/pricing)), and ThetaData at $80–$160 a month for 8–12 years of tick quotes ([ThetaData](https://thetadata.net/pricing)). Cboe DataShop and historicaloptiondata.com have conflicting public prices. The data engineer's rules for this data follow the same logic as the repo's stock work. Fill at bid and ask, or at most at mid plus 25–50% of the half-spread for patient orders. Pair option quotes with the underlying price from the same snapshot, since ORATS snaps about 14 minutes before the close. Build each day's chain from that day's file, including since-expired contracts, and split-adjust strikes consistently through NVDA's, TSLA's, AMZN's, GOOGL's and AVGO's splits. Compute IV yourself from quotes available at decision time instead of trusting vendor surfaces fitted with later data. Use earnings dates as known on each day. Model dividend early assignment and expiry-day close-out costs rather than settling at intrinsic value.

## The earnings iron fly should land near zero; treat it as plumbing

[options_earnings.py](../options_earnings.py) sells an ATM straddle in the last 30 minutes of the session before a report. It buys wings at the nearest strikes to spot ± the implied move (the straddle mid of the nearest expiry within seven days), closes the next morning between 09:45 and 10:30 ET, and sizes so the maximum loss is 0.5% of equity, with at most four spreads open. It enters at the natural credit minus $0.05 because Alpaca paper fills only marketable orders. The design gets several things right against the evidence above. Risk is defined, so it avoids the TSLA-style gap that sinks a naked straddle. It never holds into expiry, which removes pin and auto-exercise risk. It sizes small and refuses illiquid legs. Its structure is pre-registered. Against the evidence on *edge*, it is selling the one premium the literature calls thin, in the most liquid names where GXZ's effect is weakest, and paying the full spread to do it.

To size expectations, this report ran a stylized Monte Carlo (an author computation, not a sourced result): spot 100, a 6% one-day jump vol, 40% diffusive vol, 4 days to expiry at entry, no skew, and a close one day later with 3 days left at 40% vol. The fly collects a credit of about **2.3 times its maximum loss**. Results are in units of maximum loss per trade, with round-trip costs set at 6% of the straddle, roughly the full spread on four legs both ways:

| Realized jump vol / implied | Gross mean | Net mean (6% cost) | Net win rate | Per-trade s.d. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.8 (strong overpricing) | +0.19 | −0.01 | 58% | 0.52 |
| 0.9 (DJKS-like premium) | +0.11 | −0.09 | 53% | 0.56 |
| 1.0 (fairly priced, ORATS NVDA) | +0.04 | −0.16 | 48% | 0.59 |
| 1.1 (a 2026-style season) | −0.02 | −0.22 | 44% | 0.61 |

With fat-tailed (Student-t) jumps of the same variance, the net means turn positive (+0.05 at fair pricing). That holds only if the market ignored kurtosis when pricing the wings, which the smile exists to prevent, so the t-case is an upper bound rather than an expectation. The honest base case is that **costs of this size consume roughly the whole premium the literature supports**. Expectancy runs from flat to about −0.15 of maximum loss per trade, with a win rate near a coin flip, not the 70–80% that folklore promises. Paper fills at natural minus $0.05, followed by exits that in practice end at natural, put this experiment at the pessimistic end of that range. Live fills near mid would cut costs by perhaps half to two-thirds, per Muravyev-Pearson, and that is the only realistic route to a small positive number.

The numbers that follow from this are plain. About eleven names reporting quarterly gives roughly 44 events a year before skips. At 0.5% maximum loss, a mean of −0.15 costs about **3% of the options account a year**, with a one-year standard deviation near 2%. A +0.1 edge would earn about 2%. Telling a 0.1 edge from zero at t = 2 needs about 120 trades, nearly three years. Telling a 0.05 edge from zero needs about 480 trades, a decade. **Twelve months of paper results therefore cannot validate or reject the strategy.** They can validate fills, logging and assignment handling. Two tail facts matter more than the mean. First, a realized loss can exceed the 0.5% budget by the exit spread, about 20% more in the simulation, because closing at natural can cost more than wing width minus credit. Second, MSFT, META, GOOGL, AMZN and AAPL usually report within the same two or three days, so the four-spread cap is the real nightly risk limit: about 2–2.5% of equity on one sector-wide surprise.

The code review against the evidence turned up four concrete items. The module docstring still says entry is "credit mid − 10% toward the natural bid" while the code enters at natural minus $0.05. The pre-registration record should state the rule that actually runs, with the date it changed. The strategy decides on the free indicative feed while paper fills use the real NBBO, so the $0.05 cushion is a guess, and logging both the indicative and the filled prices for each leg will show how far apart they run. The "implied move" is the full straddle of an expiry that can include several non-event days, so it slightly overstates the event move. Logging DJKS's σ_Q·√(2/π) from two expiries alongside it would turn each trade into a clean implied-versus-realized observation, which is the dataset the 14-of-20 claim pretended to be. Finally, earnings dates come from yfinance with "unknown time" defaulting to after-close. A wrong date turns a crush trade into a two-day short-gamma position, so each fill should be checked against the reported timestamp.

## Conclusion

The research reframes options for this system. Options do not reveal a hidden directional edge in mega-cap tech any more than the stock-level predictors did. They reprice the same risks and charge a spread for the privilege. The premiums with real evidence, index variance and index put-writing, are compensation for bearing crash and correlation risk, and this repo's own PutWrite test showed that compensation adds nothing to a portfolio that already holds equity risk. The single-name premiums are thin enough that execution quality, not strategy selection, decides their sign. That shifts where effort pays off. A cost model built from measured NBBO, point-in-time chains, and tax-aware venue choice (Section 1256 index options sit outside Alpaca) does more for expected value than any DTE or profit-taking rule.

For the live experiment, the most useful mindset is to treat it as a measuring instrument, not a strategy awaiting confirmation. If it logs the NBBO at decision and fill, the term-structure implied move, and the realized move for every event, then three years of paper and live trades become the mega-cap earnings dataset no published source provides. That is worth more than the experiment's likely P&L, which should sit within a few percent of zero either way. The decision rule worth writing down now, before results arrive, is simple: no capital beyond paper until at least about 120 trades show a positive mean at the costs live fills actually achieve.
