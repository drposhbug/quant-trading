# Prediction markets price events, not entry points

As of November 2026, Polymarket and Kalshi are good *forecasters* of discrete, scheduled events, but they are weak *trading predictors*. The documented leads over stocks, rates and crypto last minutes to hours. Nearly all of them come from windows when cash markets are closed (election night, weekends). Off those windows the next open has already absorbed the odds, and this repo's own test found **0 of 113** odds-lead-stock tests robust. Prices are well calibrated on average but biased at the tails. The books are thin enough to be pushed by whales, wash trades and insiders, and Polymarket resolves disputed outcomes by token-holder vote. On Anthropic's Nov 19 IPO, the user's idea of using the bracket-implied close as a pre-market guide and buying only below it did one thing right: the $111.50 open was above every reasonable implied central value (about $102–108), so the rule would not have bought the open-to-close fade. It did not prevent a loss. A limit at the implied central value filled (low $92.72) and closed at $97.17, about **−5.7%** on the day. The implied value is the middle of a distribution, not a floor, so the stock closes below it about half the time by construction. A dip below it is as likely to mean the forecast was wrong as that the stock is cheap. The idea is worth a small, pre-registered paper test that scores the forecast itself (PIT, CRPS) against a naive "IPO price plus typical pop" benchmark. With a few dozen liquid IPO bracket markets a year, a real edge needs two or more years of forward data to show.

## Odds lead assets only when assets are closed

The strongest lead-lag evidence comes from the 2024 election and is about market hours, not foresight. With 5-minute data, Polymarket moved before E-mini S&P futures at every stage of election night. It reached 50% of its overnight move about **25 minutes** before the future and 75–90% about **3 to 3.75 hours** before. It also priced two weekend shocks while financial markets were shut ([Aktuğ & Torul, June 2026 WP](https://academics.bogazici.edu.tr/torul/pridis.pdf)). The companion yield paper shows where the lead lives. **58%** of the average absolute daily probability move happened while cash Treasuries were closed, and the yield response loads entirely on that overnight part. On ordinary days the overnight ZN gap explained by the odds is statistically zero (**t = 0.1, N = 457**) ([Aktuğ & Torul yield WP](https://academics.bogazici.edu.tr/torul/yield.pdf)). The one daily-horizon result is Goldstein, Li and Wang: Polymarket changes predicted next-day "Trump trade" returns, but the effect was driven by noise flow and **partly reversed** ([project page](https://chenwang.one/publication/predmkt/)). That is one election, and it does not justify a standing daily strategy.

Rates and macro tell the same story from the accuracy side. The Fed's FEDS 2026-010 finds the Kalshi median and mode matched every FOMC decision the day before the meeting since 2022. It puts headline-CPI error at about **7 bp versus 8 bp** for the Bloomberg consensus, and Kalshi is never significantly worse ([FEDS 2026-010](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2026010pap.pdf); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/28/kalshi-prediction-market-federal-reserve-betting-forecast-nber-working-paper/)). The FOMC result partly reflects how the comparison is built. Futures price a continuous expected rate, while the Kalshi mode is a discrete bucket that is right whenever the decision is near-certain. No study shows Kalshi repricing before SOFR or fed funds futures at the release minute. In crypto, Polymarket BTC threshold prices sit about **5.6 pp** away from Binance option-implied probabilities, with the gap largest in the cheap tails. The gap mean-reverts with a **~4-hour half-life**, consistent with Polymarket being the noisier venue rather than the leading one ([Portnaya, arXiv 2606.19517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19517)).

The policy-shock record is worse than the headlines suggest. After Liberation Day, recession odds peaked at **66% (Polymarket) and 71% (Kalshi)** and then fell to 38–39% once tariffs de-escalated. No 2025 recession followed, so a seller using those odds would have sold near the low ([Benzinga](https://drupal-cloudfront.benzinga.com/25/05/45535042/recession-odds-tumble-on-kalshi-crypto-based-polymarket-as-trumps-tariff-concerns-subside)). Bettors also priced small odds of the April 9 tariff pause ([Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/betting-markets-see-small-odds-trump-backs-down-from-tariffs-in-the-coming-months/)). Confident misses cluster in thin, single-decision markets: the 2025 NYC primary ([Next Event Horizon](https://nexteventhorizon.substack.com/p/kalshi-moved-the-goalposts-on-nyc-mayor)), the 2026 Wisconsin governor primary ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/13/prediction-markets-reputation-surprise-result-wisconsin/)), Brazil 2026 ([Futurism](https://futurism.com/future-society/prediction-markets-wrong-brazilian-election)) and the 2024 popular vote ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/10/18/presidential-election-odds-pro-trump-bettors-polymarket-political-polls-harris/)). The famous October 2024 "Polymarket saw Trump coming" episode is co-movement within the month, with DJT, bitcoin, the dollar and yields all trading the same news flow. No source orders the moves by day ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/10/19/who-will-win-the-presidential-election-poll-trump-media-stock-bitcoin-dollar/)).

This repo's direct test agrees. It chained eight Kalshi and Polymarket series (Fed cut, recession, shutdown, China deal, best AI model, NVDA-largest, Tesla–SpaceX merger, robotaxi), read the odds at 16:00 ET, and regressed next-day and next-5-day returns of 13 tickers. **4 of 113** predictive tests passed Holm. Each pass rested on two or three days, and **none survived dropping its 5 largest odds moves**. Same-day co-movement was common (5 of 59 Holm-significant) ([pm_results.md](pm_results.md)). The power caveat matters: the smallest detectable correlation was about 0.15 at n ≈ 900, so the null rules out only *large* leads.

| Domain | Best evidence | What it shows | Tradable for a daily retail system? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elections | Aktuğ–Torul 5-min; Goldstein–Li–Wang | Leads of 15 min–3.75 h, mostly while futures or cash are closed; next-day effect partly reverses | Only overnight/weekend gaps, through futures or extended hours |
| Fed / CPI | FEDS 2026-010 | Better expectations measure, full distribution | As a regime/risk input, not as a lead |
| Crypto | Portnaya 2026 | PM drifts 5.6 pp from options, reverts in ~4 h | No; options lead or tie |
| Policy shocks | Tariff/recession 2025 | Moves with equities, overshoots | No; would have sold the low |
| Thematic stocks | Repo pm_study, 113 tests | Same-day co-movement, no robust lead | No |

## Calibration holds on average and breaks in the tails

Both venues are well calibrated in aggregate and sharpen near resolution, but cheap contracts are systematically overpriced. Across more than 300k Kalshi contracts, the average contract returns about **−20%**, contracts at 10¢ or less lose **over 60%** after fees, and contracts above 70¢ earn small but significant positive returns. Makers buying at 50¢ or more earned about **+2.6%** ([Bürgi, Deng & Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). On Polymarket, across 588M trades, buys below 10¢ lose **19.3¢ per dollar** and buys at 90¢ or more earn 0.83¢. The bias appears in crypto and politics but not in sports, and it changes sign under some weighting schemes ([Cardozo & Rivero-Wildemauwe, arXiv 2609.12878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12878)). An informal analysis of 188,509 resolved Polymarket binaries puts the overall Brier skill score at **0.398**, from 0.264 in sports to 0.565 in politics ([Princeton thesis](https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/d5708372-5ea3-4252-b669-7f9cee07387c)).

For a bracket-implied distribution, this means the open-ended tail brackets ("over $2T", "under $1.25T") are inflated. They should be shrunk before computing a mean, and the median should be preferred because it barely depends on the tails. The repo's own calibration study, `pm_calibration.py`, is **still in progress**: `reports/pm_calibration.md` does not exist yet. It samples resolved Polymarket binaries from 2023–2026 by category and volume tercile, reads the price 1, 7 and 30 days before resolution, and adds Kalshi Fed/CPI/macro series and an IPO cache. Its reliability curves should replace the published numbers above wherever the two disagree, and its IPO cell will be the first category-specific calibration check for bracket markets. Nothing about IPO-market calibration is established yet.

## Thin books and token votes make the odds manipulable

The classic literature says manipulation fails: attacks on political stock markets moved prices briefly and then reverted ([Rhode & Strumpf, NBER](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2006/daes06/rhode.pdf)). Manipulators can even improve accuracy by adding liquidity ([Hanson & Oprea summary](https://stafforini.com/works/hanson-2009-manipulator-can-aid/)). Those results assume deep, arbitraged markets with outcomes the manipulator cannot affect. The 2024–2026 failures happened outside those assumptions. A Columbia/Barnard on-chain study classifies about **25%** of Polymarket's all-time volume (about $4.5B) as likely wash trading. The share peaked near 60% of weekly volume in December 2024 and reached 45% in sports ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/11/07/polymarket-wash-trading-inflated-prediction-markets-columbia-research/); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/347842/columbia-study-25-polymarket-volume-wash-trading)). Any "trust markets above $X volume" filter is therefore corrupted at the input.

Insider trading is real and now prosecuted. The DOJ and CFTC charged a soldier who allegedly bet on the Maduro raid with classified information for **$400K+** ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/23/us-soldier-charged-with-using-polymarket-to-bet-on-nicolas-maduro-abduction)). They also charged a Google engineer who allegedly made about **$1.2M** on Year-in-Search markets using internal data, the first case built on *corporate* information ([Debevoise](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2026/06/doj-and-cftc-bring-first-ever-prediction-markets)). George Santos settled with the CFTC over Kalshi contracts on whether he would attend an event, an outcome he controlled ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-07-31-george-santos-to-pay-35000-cftc-settlement-kalshi-sotu-bet-410338)). A screen of 93K Polymarket markets flagged wallet–market pairs with a **69.9% win rate** and about **$143M** of anomalous profit ([Mitts & Ofir via Harvard corpgov](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2026/03/25/from-iran-to-taylor-swift-informed-trading-in-prediction-markets/)). Insider flow is a genuine signal, but it fires minutes to days before the news, after the price has already jumped. Knowingly trading alongside misappropriated information creates tippee risk. An IPO close market is exactly where underwriter-desk or company insiders who see the book could trade. That is an inference, but the Spagnuolo case shows the legal theory reaches corporate information.

Resolution adds a separate fat tail. Polymarket uses UMA's optimistic oracle, and disputed outcomes are decided by stake-weighted token votes. The **$237M** Zelenskyy-suit market resolved NO with three addresses holding over 70% of the vote weight ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/329210/polymarket-rules-no-237m-controversial-bet-over-zelenskyy-suit?amp=1)). The **$85M** Strategy bitcoin-sale market flipped on a late rules clarification ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/markets/usd85m-polymarket-dispute-over-strategy-s-may-bitcoin-sale-puts-uma-s-token-voting-oracle-on)). Polymarket itself said UMA got the Barron/DJT market wrong ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/302171/polymarket-contradicts-umas-resolution-on-barron-trumps-involvement-with-djt-token)). IPO close-cap markets resolve on an exchange print times a share count, which is objective, except that the *share count* is a judgment call. That ambiguity matters below.

Whether the odds are self-fulfilling depends on which side is thin. Odds clearly feed into asset prices in politics: about **+2 bp** of 2s10s steepening per +10 pp of Trump probability ([Aktuğ & Torul](https://academics.bogazici.edu.tr/torul/yield.pdf)). A survey experiment shows displayed odds change who voters *expect* to win, though not shown to change votes ([Brennan Center](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/new-study-finds-prediction-markets-are-influencing-voters-and-voters-want)). For a trillion-dollar IPO, the direction runs the other way. The Anthropic close-cap market traded about **$5.81M** in 24 hours ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/anthropic-1-5-trillion-ipo-164738953.html)), a rounding error next to day-one stock volume. Nobody can cheaply push the stock to a bracket, but anyone can cheaply push the bracket. One untested hypothesis is that headline anchoring on "$1.5T" pulled the open toward that line ($111.50 ≈ $1.48T). Treat the odds as the manipulable input and the stock as the ground truth.

## Anthropic shows what the implied close can and cannot do

Anthropic priced at $83, above its $70–78 range, giving a basic market cap of about **$1.1T** ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/anthropic-1-5-trillion-ipo-164738953.html)). The same Yahoo article (published 11:47am ET, snapshot time unstated) gives Polymarket close-cap odds of **8.65%** under $1.25T, **61.95%** for $1.25–1.5T and **27.95%** over $1.5T (25.7% for $1.5–1.75T, 2.05% over $2T). A month earlier the market had **89.6%** on over $1.5T. Per the user's tracking, the stock opened at $111.50, hit a low of $92.72 and closed at $97.17. The close cap of roughly $1.29T landed inside the favored bracket.

Converting those brackets to dollars shows the first trap. I normalized the brackets (they summed to 98.55%), assumed a uniform distribution within each bracket, used $0.25T widths for the open tails, and converted at **13.25B shares** (the $1.1T ÷ $83 basic count implied by Yahoo). That gives a mean of **$108.3**, a median of **$106.7**, a 25th percentile of **$99.2** and a 10th percentile of **$94.7**. The user's figure of **≈ $103** is what the same brackets give at about **13.9B shares**, roughly a diluted count: mean $103.2, median $101.7, 25th percentile $94.6, 10th percentile $90.3. The share count alone moves every quantile by about **5%**, about the size of any plausible edge. The resolution rule uses shares outstanding from the exchange listing ([frenzycap rules](https://www.frenzycap.com/predictions/event/polymarket/anthropic-ipo-closing-market-cap)), so the conversion must use that exact count, recorded before the event. The realized close sat at the **18th percentile** of the implied distribution with basic shares, or the 34th with diluted shares. That is one draw, consistent with a calibrated forecast and with a modest upward bias.

| Rule (Anthropic, Nov 19) | Trigger | Filled? | Day-1 result at $97.17 close |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy at open if open < implied median | $111.50 vs $102–107 | No (sat out) | Avoided −12.9% open-to-close |
| Buy at open, no filter | $111.50 | Yes | **−12.9%** |
| Limit at implied mean (user's $103) | Low $92.72 | Yes | **−5.7%** |
| Limit at implied 25th pct ($94.6–99.2) | Low $92.72 | Yes | +2.7% to −2.0% |
| Limit at implied 10th pct ($90.3–94.7) | Low $92.72 | Only on basic shares | +2.6% or no trade |
| Limit at $70 | Below the $83 IPO price | Never | No trade |

The table is the core evaluation of the user's idea. The "only below implied value" filter did its job at the open: it kept the strategy out of the worst trade of the day. But "buy dips below the implied close so as not to be caught negative" assumes the implied close acts as support, and it does not. The implied median is the point where the market thinks the close is equally likely to land above or below. A buyer at the median therefore loses on the day about half the time *if the forecast is calibrated*, and more often if dips below it happen because the forecast is too high. That is the adverse-selection problem: a limit fills exactly when the stock is weaker than the forecast expected. Deeper limits trade fill probability for return. Under a zero-drift Brownian approximation, P(low ≤ ℓ) ≈ 2Φ(ln(ℓ/reference)/σ_d). With an assumed day-one σ of 15% (not a sourced figure) and the $103 implied value as reference, a $70 limit fills about **1%** of the time. A limit near the 25th percentile fills often, but its edge depends entirely on the forecast being calibrated in the left tail.

Three further limits apply. First, the bracket market resolves on the **day-one close**, so it says nothing about day 2 or later. Large tech IPOs drifted badly after listing: in the repo's hand-picked sample of 10 from 2012–2024, the median was **−40%** versus QQQ in year one ([MATH.md](../MATH.md)), and SpaceX was about 50% below its post-IPO-week peak by late July 2026 ([MoneyWeek](https://moneyweek.com/investments/tech-stocks/spacex-ipo)). A dip-buy held past the close bets against that base rate. Second, the odds **followed the book-build**. They were 90% wrong on "over $1.5T" a month out and converged only after the $83 pricing was public ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/anthropic-1-5-trillion-ipo-164738953.html)). So the pre-open distribution may be little more than "IPO price × typical pop", which anyone can compute without Polymarket. Third, the evidence base is two mega-IPOs. SpaceX's Kalshi contract resolved YES at **98%**, but its strike ($1.2T) was easy to clear ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/15/spacex-stock-record-ipo-debut.html)). Pre-open broker indications for Figma ($105–110) missed the $85 open and undershot the $115.50 close ([Capital Brief](https://www.capitalbrief.com/briefing/figma-shares-set-to-debut-at-over-triple-ipo-price-12d4297a-69a9-49b4-a508-57676925caac/); [IPOScoop](https://www.iposcoop.com/the-ipo-buzz-figma-fig-opens-at-85-up-158-percent-tops-100-in-nyse-debut/)), so pre-open guides can be wrong in either direction. Also, the Yahoo snapshot may postdate Anthropic's open, and the repo did not snapshot the brackets before the open (commit 1b4d08d is after the close). The Anthropic case is therefore illustrative, not a clean pre-open test.

## A model built on brackets needs a frozen clock and tiny size

The construction is standard and the details decide the result. Take each bracket's mid price, falling back to last trade only when the spread is tight. Normalize away the overround. Use proportional normalization by default and a power or Shin-style variant as a sensitivity check that takes more out of the inflated tails. Also compute the distribution separately from bids and from asks to get an envelope. For Kalshi "above K" ladders, enforce monotonicity before differencing, which is the discrete analogue of Breeden–Litzenberger ([Kalshi](https://news.kalshi.com/p/how-to-read-probabilities)). Use the median and interquartile range, which are robust; the mean depends on the tail model ([Glasgow eprint](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/390184/1/390184.pdf)). Convert market cap to price with the resolution rule's share count. Then **freeze** the distribution at a fixed time T0 before the first trade. IPO bracket markets keep trading during the session and converge on the realized price, so evaluating an intraday entry against an intraday-updated distribution is circular.

Score each rule by expected value: P(fill) × E[return | fill]. Count a fill only when the low trades *through* the limit by at least a tick, and assume a place at the back of the queue. Backtests that drop unfilled orders or count touches as fills overstate both terms. For sizing, the continuous Kelly fraction μ/σ² computed from the implied distribution must be shrunk heavily. The distribution is closer to risk-neutral than physical, the tails carry favorite-longshot bias, and with a handful of events the uncertainty in the edge exceeds the edge itself, which pushes the optimal fraction well below half Kelly. Practitioners cap at 1/4–1/2 Kelly until 50+ resolved trades show verified calibration ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/prediction-markets/position-sizing-binary-markets); [Beygelzimer, Langford & Pennock](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/1201.6655)).

Data is the binding constraint, and history cannot be reconstructed after the fact. Polymarket `prices-history` returns sampled prices only: no size, no bid/ask, windows of about 15 days or less, and empty results for `interval=max` on some resolved markets ([Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/developers/CLOB/timeseries.md); [Dwellir](https://www.dwellir.com/blog/polymarket-historical-data)). Brackets get added mid-life, so a historical rebuild can miss mass. Kalshi splits live and historical endpoints at moving cutoffs ([Kalshi docs](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/historical_data)). The reliable dataset is the repo's own forward snapshots, taken every few minutes on listing day and covering every bracket's bid, ask and size together with the resolution text and share-count source. The move to a single long-running monitor job (commit fc74a90) fixes the cron gap that missed Nov 19, but the IPO snapshot needs its own pre-open schedule.

Sample size is the deeper problem. Polymarket close-cap markets have appeared for perhaps a few dozen 2026 listings (ITG, Lime, Bending Spoons, Deep Fission, Kraken, Oura, Anthropic, SpaceX, OpenAI), many with thin books ([lines.com](https://www.lines.com/prediction-markets/business/itg-ipo-closing-market-cap-20260625034819511); [Orrery](https://orrery.me/markets/kraken-ipo-closing-market-cap-above-26b)). Assume day-one returns with a 30% standard deviation (an assumption to replace with real data). Detecting a 5% mean edge at 80% power then needs about **280** events, and a 10% edge needs about **70**. Forecast-scoring statistics such as PIT uniformity and CRPS accumulate evidence faster than PnL, which is why they should be the primary endpoint.

## Pre-registered forward test for IPO brackets

This test runs as a separate paper sleeve. It does not touch the frozen model in `model.py`, its universe (ANT stays out), or the 2027-11-19 review ([MATH.md](../MATH.md)). It is paper only and fully rules-based. Commit the protocol below, dated, before the next eligible listing. Any change to it restarts the sample.

| Element | Pre-registered choice |
|---|---|
| Eligible events | Every US IPO with a Polymarket or Kalshi first-day close (market cap or price) bracket set: ≥4 brackets, ≥$250k cumulative volume and a published share-count rule at T0. Skip if the share count is ambiguous. |
| Snapshot | All brackets' bid/ask/size every 5 min from pricing to close, plus resolution text and share-count source, saved to `signals/` |
| Freeze time T0 | 09:25 ET on listing day (primary); last snapshot before first print (secondary, logged only) |
| Distribution | Mid prices, proportional normalization, uniform within brackets, open tails = adjacent bracket width; bid-only and ask-only envelopes logged. Outputs: median M, Q25, Q10 in $/share |
| Naive benchmark | IPO price × (1 + trailing median first-day return of the prior 20 US IPOs over $1B), with a lognormal spread fitted to the same 20 |
| Forecast endpoints (primary) | PIT of the realized close within F; CRPS of F vs the benchmark; paired sign test on CRPS |
| Trading rules (secondary) | R0: buy at the open, unconditional (control). R1: buy at the open only if open < M. R2: limit at Q25, valid first print to 15:55, filled only if low < limit − $0.01. Exit at the day-1 close; day-5 close logged as a secondary horizon |
| Costs | Half the first 30-minute quoted spread plus 10 bp per side |
| Size | Fixed 0.25% of paper equity per fill; no scaling before review |
| Evaluation | Skeptical prior N(0, 5%) on mean day-1 return per fill for R1 and R2 minus R0; report posterior P(edge > 0) |
| Review | At 30 eligible events or 2027-11-19, whichever is later. Stop early only if, after 15 fills, P(edge > 0) < 0.2 |
| Success bar | CRPS beats the benchmark (sign-test p < 0.05), PIT not rejected as non-uniform, and posterior P(R2 edge > 0) > 0.9. Anything less stays paper |

The design measures one thing honestly: whether the prediction market adds information beyond the IPO price. If the bracket distribution cannot beat a typical-pop benchmark on CRPS, the dip rule has nothing to stand on, whatever its PnL. Arm R0 makes clear whether any profit came from the filter or just from IPOs that went up. To raise n, the same recipe can later be pointed at Polymarket BTC threshold ladders, which resolve daily or weekly. The Portnaya result suggests that the options market already prices that information better ([arXiv 2606.19517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19517)), so it belongs in a separate pre-registered test, not pooled with IPOs.

## Conclusion

The useful reframing is that a prediction market sells you a *distribution of outcomes*, not a *price level to defend*. Its value to a trader is mostly negative information: when the open sits far above the implied range, as it did for Anthropic, the market is telling you the pop is already rich. Read that way, the bracket set works as a "don't chase" filter far more naturally than as a "buy the dip" trigger. The open-above-median filter is the part of the user's idea most worth keeping, and the part the forward test can confirm quickest. The 18th-to-34th-percentile close on Anthropic, plus the 90%-wrong reading a month earlier, also hints that these markets anchor on the book-build and on round-number headlines rather than leading them.

The broader lesson matches every other signal this repo has tested: the scarce input is clean, timestamped, pre-registered history, not cleverness. The Anthropic day was lost as data because no pre-open snapshot existed, and the share-count ambiguity alone was worth 5% of price. Until the calibration study reports and 30 frozen-at-T0 IPO distributions exist, the honest answer to "is Polymarket a usable predictor?" is that it is usable as a sanity bound on event outcomes and not yet as a source of trades.
