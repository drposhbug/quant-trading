# Most published predictors die in mega-cap tech

As of late 2026, almost no published return predictor survives out of sample and after costs as a *directional* signal for US mega-cap tech at daily-to-monthly horizons. What survives works on risk instead: it tells you how much exposure to hold, not which way prices will move. The few predictors with a credible out-of-sample record are index-level and slow. They are the variance risk premium and implied correlation (roughly quarterly horizons), aggregate short interest (a patchy record), time-series trend used as a gate, and residual (factor-stripped) short-term reversal. Classic accounting-surprise post-earnings drift (PEAD), the large-cap pre-holiday effect and the index-inclusion effect are dead. The pre-FOMC drift has mostly faded. Social-media, Trump/Musk-post and news-sentiment effects play out in minutes, and in 2026 some of the move happens *before* the post appears. Prediction markets such as Kalshi are excellent macro *forecasters*, but no published study shows their odds lead equity returns. The academic baseline explains why: the average anomaly earns about **4 bps a month** after costs and post-publication decay ([Chen & Velikov, JFQA 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/zeroing-in-on-the-expected-returns-of-anomalies/945133D5A3ECEEAF466AEE91551FD225)), and decay is worst in the cheapest-to-arbitrage, most liquid names. This repo's own Holm-corrected tests agree. **0 of 9** event and calendar tests, **0 of 44** macro/cross-asset tests, **0 of 39** candlestick tests and **1 of 270** Trump-post tests passed. The only prediction-market "passes" (4 of 113) look fragile. For a concentrated tech basket, the edge that holds up in testing is portfolio construction: inverse-vol or 1/N weights, a volatility and exposure budget, T-bill yield on idle cash, and non-equity diversifiers. Signal hunting is better treated as a disciplined test program whose expected result is a null.

## The decay arithmetic sets a brutal prior

Every verdict below rests on one result. Published predictors reproduce, but their size shrinks sharply once you leave the original sample. Across 97 predictors, returns are **26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication** ([McLean & Pontiff, JF 2016](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)). Decay is larger for predictors with high in-sample returns and for those that are cheap to arbitrage: liquid stocks with low idiosyncratic risk ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/effects-of-market-adaptation)). Restricting to post-2005 data raises decay to about **72%** ([Fed FEDS 2021-037](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2021-037pap.pdf)). After effective spreads, publication decay and modern trading technology, the expected return of the average anomaly is about **4 bps a month**. The best anomalies reach about 10 bps after a data-mining adjustment, and combinations reach about 20 bps ([Chen & Velikov](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/zeroing-in-on-the-expected-returns-of-anomalies/945133D5A3ECEEAF466AEE91551FD225)).

The academic and skeptic camps disagree less than their headlines suggest. Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen find most of 153 factors replicate, with replication rates above 75% in 10 of 13 themes ([NBER w28432](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf)). Chen and Zimmermann reproduce nearly 100% of published results and estimate publication bias at only about 12–13% ([Fed FEDS 2018-033](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2018033pap.pdf); [Open Source AP](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/chen2021open.pdf)). Hou, Xue and Zhang use NYSE breakpoints and value weighting to mute microcaps. Under those choices, **64% of 447 anomalies are insignificant at 5% and 85% fail a t > 3 hurdle**. They call microcap anomalies "more apparent than real" ([NBER conference paper](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f102921/f102921.pdf)). Both findings are true at once: the effects exist, and they are much smaller in value-weighted, large-cap, after-cost tests. That is exactly the setting of a mega-cap basket. Cost-aware construction helps only for low-turnover signals. Anomalies turning over less than 50% a month keep significant net spreads, few faster ones do, and a buy/hold band is the most effective simple fix ([Novy-Marx & Velikov, NBER w20721](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20721)).

Market-timing predictors fare no better. Goyal, Welch and Zafirov re-examined 46 equity-premium predictors through 2021. More than a third of the post-2008 variables lost even in-sample significance, half of the rest failed out of sample, and only "a small number" held up in both ([RFS 2024 abstract](https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/a-comprehensive-2022-look-at-the-empirical-performance-of-equity-premium-prediction)). ML forecast combinations also generally fail to beat the historical mean ([Xu & Liu, QF 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v24y2024i10p1445-1461.html)). The practical hurdles follow directly. Require t > 3 for any signal found by search ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://nber.org/papers/w20592)). Report the Deflated Sharpe Ratio using the actual number of trials ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)). Compute the probability of backtest overfitting (PBO) ([Bailey et al.](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/overfit-tools-at.pdf)). Haircut any in-sample premium by at least half. Trying about 45 configurations on 5 years of data is enough to manufacture an in-sample Sharpe of 1 with an expected out-of-sample Sharpe of zero ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=23736)).

Mega-cap tech adds a structural problem on top of decay. With 8–15 names, cross-sectional long-short rankings have too little breadth to estimate anything. The names also behave like one factor. Over the last two years their average raw pairwise correlation was **0.38**, and after removing QQQ beta it was **about 0.00**. The only strong residual links are supply-chain pairs such as **NVDA–TSM (0.37)** and AVGO–TSM (0.30) ([in-house correlations](correlations_results.md)). Most "signals" for this basket are therefore really time-series bets on one AI-capex factor.

## Earnings and calendar effects: one risk premium left, the rest arbitraged

### Classic PEAD is dead in large caps; return-based drift is the only variant still twitching

Martineau finds PEAD began disappearing from non-microcap stocks in 2001 and was "completely disappeared" by 2006. Prices now adjust on announcement day, and he credits decimalization and Reg NMS–era HFT ([UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again/)). Subrahmanyam's 2025 replication on 2001–2024 data gives t = 2.18 for all stocks but **t = 1.43 once microcaps are excluded**, even though microcaps are only about 3% of market cap ([same](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again/)). The defenders use different surprise measures. Dickerson, Julliard and Mueller's drift factor (JFE 2025) is built from announcement-window returns rather than accounting surprise (SUE). Text-based surprise from earnings calls (PEAD.txt) still shows drift "considerable even in recent years when the classic PEAD is close to 0" ([JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/5EB217BB68B5FB054FE38541BAAC4679/S0022109022001181a.pdf/peadtxt-post-earnings-announcement-drift-using-text.pdf)). A small 2025–26 large-cap test of 40 events found a mean 20-day drift of **+0.67% with t = 0.67** ([SSRN 7137218](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7137218)).

This repo ran the return-based variant on 509 mega-cap events from 2014 to 2026. It used SEC 8-K Item 2.02 timestamps to set day 0 and averaged events within each earnings season. The sign of the reaction-day residual times the next-20-day residual gave **71 bp per event, t = 2.38, the same sign in both halves, but a Holm-adjusted p of 0.156**. It fails the bar. An instructive artifact turned up along the way: with Tesla's delivery 8-Ks left in, "PEAD" jumped to 132 bp with t = 3.2, so the apparent drift came from delivery days, not earnings ([in-house events study](events_results.md)). That is a textbook example of event-definition errors creating a false positive. The verdict is to treat SUE-PEAD as zero. Keep return-based or text-based surprise drift on a watch list at an honest expectation of tens of bps per event, before costs, with low power.

### The earnings announcement premium is the most plausible survivor, because it is risk

Savor and Wilson find firms scheduled to report earn about **9.9% a year** in abnormal return, framed as compensation for systematic risk rather than mispricing ([Wharton draft](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Draft20111215p_edited.pdf)). Risk premia decay less than mispricing does. Predictors labelled as risk by peer review lose about 35% less after publication ([AFA](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=8400)). The size pattern is contested. Barber et al. find the global premium about 6 points a year larger for the smallest stocks than the largest ([TAU seminar PDF](https://en-coller.tau.ac.il/sites/nihul_en.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/Recanati/management/seminars/account/barber.pdf)). A UChicago dissertation finds the *market-level* effect concentrated in large "bellwether" firms ([Lombardi](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/163/files/Lombardi_uchicago_0330D_14112.pdf)), and one paper argues the premium has migrated into pre-announcement 8-K windows ([IIMB](https://www.iimb.ac.in/ARC2020/Papers/The_Disappearing_Earnings_Announcement_Premium.pdf)). The in-house pre-earnings run-up test (residual, 5 days before the reaction day) gave **49 bp per event, t = 2.30**. The halves were unstable (18 bp then 79 bp) and the Holm p was 0.17 ([events study](events_results.md)). That is suggestive but not proven. Any tradable version is tens of bps per event against very high announcement-week volatility.

The event edge with the best direct evidence is volatility, not direction. Across about 29,000 US reports in 2023–2026, the average implied move was **±9.18% against ±6.32% realized** ([EarningsWatcher](https://earnings-watcher.com/wiki/options-implied-move-vs-actual-earnings-study-2026)). NVDA's implied move overstated the realized move in **14 of 20 quarters** ([Cboe LiveVol via syndication](https://1strespondernews.com/first-dry/Nvidia-PostEarnings-Volatility-Options-Pricing-Overestimated-Swings-in-14-of-Last-20-Quarters-19-1570)). The pattern varies by name, though: META's realized move beat its implied move in 6 of 12 quarters ([ORATS](https://orats.com/blog/meta-earnings-expected-move)). Long straddles bought three days before earnings historically earned **+3.34%** ([Gao, Xing & Zhang, JFQA 2018](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/anticipating-uncertainty-straddles-around-earnings-announcements/7B34877AD5E06304BA3C55FBA3219FDD)). So a blanket "sell IV crush" rule is wrong, and the trade is a name-specific, defined-risk satellite.

### Supply-chain spillovers are real in theory and same-day in practice

Cohen and Frazzini's customer-momentum portfolio earned **1.55% a month** in-sample. Its mechanism is limited attention, and the effect weakens when the customer and supplier share mutual-fund owners ([JF 2008](https://typeset.io/pdf/economic-links-and-predictable-returns-1acnxz4kzc.pdf)). NVDA, AVGO, AMD and TSM are the most commonly owned and most heavily covered stocks on earth, which is the worst case for that mechanism. An NBER paper also finds industry, customer and supplier momentum alphas vanish after controlling for a single "CS momentum" factor ([NBER w25201](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25201/revisions/w25201.rev0.pdf)). In this repo, the sign of each announcer's reaction times the other names' next-day residual gave **1.6 bp, t = 0.53**, with the sign flipping between halves ([events study](events_results.md)). The NVDA→semis link exists, as the 0.37 residual correlation shows. It is priced in the same session.

### Calendar effects have collapsed to noise at the index level

The pre-FOMC drift (Lucca–Moench, 1994–2011) "essentially disappeared after 2015" ([Kurov, Halova Wolfe & Gilbert](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/345940053_The_Disappearing_Pre-FOMC_Announcement_Drift)). A practitioner backtest claims it revived in high-VIX regimes after a flat 2016–2019, but it reports no t-statistics ([Quantseeker](https://www.quantseeker.com/p/trading-the-fed-the-pre-fomc-drift)). The S&P 500 inclusion effect fell from about **7.4% in the 1990s to under 1%**, and deletions from −16.6% to −0.6% ([Greenwood & Sammon, JF 2025](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/a6521969-9113-4987-a523-c66fa908d420/content)). Incumbent mega-caps are irrelevant to it anyway. The pre-holiday effect now lives only in small firms ([Ko & Yang, CFR 2024](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/ko2021pre.pdf)). Turn-of-month is one of only 3 of 188 calendar anomalies significant in both half-samples ([Carchano & Pardo via CXO](https://cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/stock-index-futures-calendar-effects)), but its post-2008 support is practitioner-only. Options-expiration week (0.45% vs 0.12% a week in 28 large caps, 1996–2008) is the one calendar effect whose mechanism targets heavily optioned large caps ([Stivers & Sun, JBF 2013](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v37y2013i11p4226-4240.html)). It has no out-of-sample update, and the 2022–2026 daily-expiry boom plausibly diluted it. Since Feb 2026 the mega-caps list Monday and Wednesday expiries, and 0DTE's share of their option volume rose **from 15% to 33%** ([MEMX](https://memx.com/insights/retail-active-in-ai-infrastructure-growth-in-zero-day-options-volume)).

The in-house replication over 3,239 days is blunt. Pre-FOMC gave **+9.6 bp a day on QQQ (t = 1.01)** and +19.4 bp on the equal-weight universe (t = 1.64), consistent in both halves but nowhere near significant. Turn-of-month gave **+0.5 bp (t = 0.08)** and pre-holiday **+3.6 bp (t = 0.40)**. Forcing the pre-FOMC overlay anyway moved Sharpe from 1.284 to 1.317, with a bootstrap P of 0.16 ([events study](events_results.md)). Analyst-revision and guidance drift has no mega-cap evidence at all. With dozens of covering analysts per name, the underreaction mechanism ([JBFA 2020](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jbfnac/v47y2020i9-10p1151-1181.html)) has the least room to operate, and point-in-time revision history requires I/B/E/S or FactSet.

## Posts, news and prediction markets move prices in minutes, not days

### Trump posts: real minute-scale impact, front-run, mostly reversed

The rigorous designs all use 5-to-30-minute windows. Trump's tweets raised uncertainty and volume, but **most topics had no effect**; only trade-war tweets moved US stocks ([Decision Support Systems 2021](https://datalearner.com/academic/journal-papers/0167-9236/volumes-and-issues/309/paper-detail/4951)). Bianchi, Kind and Kung find tariff tweets have a *negligible* average stock-market effect ([NBER w26308](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w26308/revisions/w26308.rev2.pdf)). For company-naming tweets, "the initial price impact is reversed in the next few trading days" ([Ge, Kurov & Wolfe, JFR 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfnres/v42y2019i2p213-242.html)). Minute-level work finds abnormal returns and volume *before* tweets ([Machus, Mestel & Theissen 2022](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/beexfi/v33y2022ics2214635021001386.html)). The 2026 Truth Social evidence makes the timing problem worse. A minute-level study of 1,341 posts found unusual pre-post trading in 15 events, including a sell-off about 20 minutes before an Iran post. It put the odds of chance at about one in a billion, and only oil-related posts produced abnormal activity ([The Conversation/QUT](https://stories.theconversation.com/the-market-moves-before-trump-posts/)). Iran posts averaged **0.73% 5-minute futures moves against a 0.2% baseline**, and the reactions are getting "less durable" ([CoinEdition citing Axios](https://coinedition.com/how-trumps-truth-social-posts-moved-bitcoin-stocks-and-oil-in-2025-and-2026/)). JPMorgan found trading the posts was not very profitable ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/05/02/markets-swings-truth-social-posts-jpmorgan-potfolio-manager/)). Firms now pay a reported **~$100k a month** for millisecond Truth API access ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/08/04/2026/the-trade-in-trumps-social-media-previews)), which tells a retail system where it stands in the queue.

Big policy *actions* are different from posts, but they are priced as macro shocks within the session. Liberation Day hit the least tariff-exposed assets as hard as the most exposed ([Welch, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6855298)). The 90-day pause produced a **+12.2% Nasdaq day** within hours ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/equities/5-year-record-surge-for-us-stocks-as-trump-presses-pause-on-some-tariffs/260062)). The repo's own test covered 4,389 Truth Social posts (Jan–Sep 2025). It measured returns only from the first daily price printed *after* each post, which is what a daily-bar trader can capture, against 300 date-shifted placebos. **Only 1 of 270 topic×ticker tests passed Holm**: China posts → ORCL 5-day drift. That looks like chance ([in-house Trump study](trump_results.md)). One pattern is worth a pre-registered forward test but not money: Fed/rates posts were followed by positive 5-day drift across nearly every name (QQQ z ≈ 2.65), and none of those individually survived correction.

### Musk posts: noise for a daily system

The most careful design found, a Corvinus study of 3,158 tweets that excluded news-contaminated days, finds **no significant impact** on TSLA ([Corvinus J.](https://cjssp.uni-corvinus.hu/index.php/cjssp/article/download/977/pdf_1)). A Princeton thesis finds a same-day effect with "limited to no evidence" on surrounding days ([Princeton](https://dataspace.princeton.edu/handle/88435/dsp01r781wk23g)). WallStreetBets due-diligence posts stopped predicting returns after GameStop ([Bradley et al., RFS 2024](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:37:y:2024:i:5:p:1409-1459.)). SPCX, listed 2026-06-12, has too little history for any event study ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/spacex-ipo-valuation-20260612)). Its lockup and index flows will dominate whatever Musk posts. In the in-house study, Musk/Tesla/SpaceX posts → TSLA showed no effect (1-day drift z = −1.24).

### Prediction markets are superb forecasters with no proven equity lead

The strongest institutional evidence is a forecasting result, not a trading one. The Fed's "Kalshi and the Rise of Macro Markets" finds Kalshi's median and mode had a **perfect record the day before FOMC meetings**. It also reports smaller headline-CPI errors than the Bloomberg consensus, and Kalshi is the only market-based source for GDP, payroll and unemployment expectations ([FEDS 2026-010](https://federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/kalshi-and-the-rise-of-macro-markets.htm)). The paper does not test equities. Price discovery runs *between* venues: Polymarket leads Kalshi on politics, and large-trade order imbalance predicts contract returns ([SSRN 5331995](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5331995)). The one equity lead-lag claim covers the 2024 election. A blog summary reports **~13 bp of next-day return per +1pp** of Trump-win probability on a 34-asset "Trump trade" basket, with partial reversal. The basket was chosen from media narratives, and the paper itself was not verified ([Alpha in Academia](https://www.alphainacademia.com/p/the-odds-lead-the-tape)). The data itself is noisy. About 25% of Polymarket's historical volume looks like wash trading ([Gizmodo on Columbia study](https://gizmodo.com/study-finds-around-a-quarter-of-polymarket-trades-are-fake-2000683231)). The average Kalshi contract returns **−20%** before fees, consistent with a longshot bias ([UCD WP](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP2025_19.pdf)). Index-level markets mostly mirror same-day options pricing ([Tradytics, vendor](https://tradytics.com/learn/does-polymarket-predict-the-stock-market)).

This repo built the first direct test the notes could find. It chained eight Kalshi and Polymarket series without splicing across contracts: Fed cut, recession, shutdown, US–China deal, best AI model, NVDA-largest, Tesla–SpaceX merger and robotaxi. Odds were read at 16:00 ET and regressed against next-day and next-5-day returns ([in-house prediction-market study](pm_results.md)). **4 of 113 predictive tests passed.** Shutdown odds → TSM and ORCL 5-day returns used only 88 observations. The other two passes were rising Tesla–SpaceX merger odds predicting *lower* next-day TSLA and QQQ returns. A merger market forecasting QQQ, with a larger t-stat than for TSLA, is a red flag for a confound. A likely candidate is the mechanical deadline decay of a "by date X" ladder lining up with a market regime, so these should be treated as unreplicated until they hold on data after 2026-11. Same-day co-movement is real and economically sensible. NVDA-largest odds move with NVDA (corr 0.36, a built-in placebo, since that contract resolves on market cap), and recession odds move against every name. The odds and the stocks react to the same news together; the next close sees no lead. The test's power caveat is essential. At n ≈ 900 the smallest detectable correlation is about 0.13, while real daily predictors run 0.02–0.05. A null therefore rules out only *large* leads.

### News sentiment and attention: the drift lives in small caps and is shrinking

LLM-scored headlines are the strongest recent news result. GPT-4 achieves about a **90% portfolio-day hit rate on the "non-tradable initial reaction"**. The tradable drift is concentrated in small stocks and negative news, and the paper's own abstract says "strategy returns decline as LLM adoption rises" ([Lopez-Lira & Tang v6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619v6)). Its Sharpe fell **from 6.54 to 1.22 by 2024**, and the strategy is unprofitable at 20 bp per trade ([arXiv v6 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)). RavenPack-era work agrees that news predictability is short-horizon and lives in poor-information stocks ([AEA 2017](https://aeaweb.org/conference/2017/preliminary/paper/G46EGhsb)). News about small, volatile stocks takes about twice as long to price ([Ke, Kelly & Xiu](https://www.nottingham.ac.uk/economics/documents/seminar-papers/senior/2020/xiu-18-11-20.pdf)). Google search spikes predict two weeks of gains and then reversal, mainly through retail attention in small caps ([Da, Engelberg & Gao](https://rady.ucsd.edu/faculty/directory/engelberg/pub/portfolios/GOOGLE.pdf)). Mega-caps are always searched, so abnormal-attention shocks are rare and coincide with already-priced news. The one aggregate use with support is market-level. ChatGPT-scored WSJ headlines from 1996 to 2022 carry market-level predictive power that a simple negative-news ratio lacks ([arXiv 2502.10008](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/2502.10008)). That makes them a candidate index-timing feature, not a stock picker.

## Options, flow, alt data and macro: risk regimes survive, alpha does not

### Market-level option measures have the best record, at slow horizons

The variance risk premium (VIX² minus realized variance) predicts aggregate returns best at about a quarterly horizon ([Bollerslev, Tauchen & Zhou](https://repec.econ.au.dk/repec/creates/rp/08/rp08_48.pdf)). The result holds internationally and is not finite-sample bias ([Bollerslev et al., JFQA 2014](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/stock-return-predictability-and-variance-risk-premia-statistical-inference-and-international-evidence/0BE5DE1D942A0342DDBA24D7BFBEA5C8)). Pyun nonetheless calls the BTZ-style out-of-sample performance "comparably weak" ([AEA 2017](https://topcat.aeaweb.org/conference/2017/preliminary/paper/GDde2yiy)). Implied correlation predicts market excess returns up to a year ahead, in and out of sample ([Buss, Schönleber & Vilkov](https://aeaweb.org/conference/2018/preliminary/paper/Rt6FbZde)). Their proposed mechanism is concentration of market exposure, which describes 2026 exactly: NVDA, AAPL and MSFT exceeded **21% of the S&P 500** in October, a record for three stocks ([247wallst](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/07/three-stocks-are-now-21-of-the-sp-500-the-most-ever)). The inputs are cheap: Cboe publishes VIX, VIX3M, VVIX, SKEW and the COR1M/COR3M implied-correlation indices as index histories, though how far back the free COR3M history goes still needs checking. Even the survivors predict at quarterly-to-annual horizons with modest R². That is enough to tilt exposure, not enough to forecast a month.

The VIX term structure predicts *variance assets* (variance swaps, VIX futures, straddles), not the index ([Johnson, JFQA 2017](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:jfinqa:v:52:y:2017:i:06:p:2461-2490_00)). Quantpedia's out-of-sample term-structure strategy is slightly negative ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/exploiting-term-structure-of-vix-futures)). VIX backwardation is, if anything, *contrarian* for equities ([Macrosynergy](https://macrosynergy.com/?p=41000)), and the in-house test agrees on sign. Stress measured by VIX/VIX3M preceded **+6.8 bp per σ next day on QQQ (t = 2.05)**, but Holm p was 1.00 ([in-house macro study](macro_results.md)). Dealer gamma exposure (GEX) is a volatility-regime indicator, not a direction call. OptionMetrics finds its forecasting power for realized volatility concentrated in *low*-vol regimes ([OptionMetrics](https://optionmetrics.com/research/dealer-gamma-exposure-and-overnight-gap-risk-incremental-information-in-low-volatility-regimes/)), and vendor SpotGamma says GEX does not predict direction ([SpotGamma](https://spotgamma.com/gamma-exposure-explained/)). Intraday momentum appears on negative-gamma days only ([Baltussen et al., JFE](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/hedging-demand-and-market-intraday-momentum/)).

### Single-stock option signals were strongest exactly where mega-caps are not

Three classic single-stock option signals all point away from mega-caps. Steep implied-vol smirks predict underperformance for up to six months ([Xing, Zhang & Zhao, JFQA 2010](https://www.iex.nl/Forum/Upload/2014/7383995.pdf)). Stocks with relatively expensive calls beat those with expensive puts by about **50 bp a week**, but the authors report that predictability declines over their own sample and is largest when *stock* liquidity is low ([Cremers & Weinbaum, JFQA 2010](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/deviations-from-putcall-parity-and-stock-return-predictability/D9BA8F97580328AAFD7988B092FE5D50)). Pan and Poteshman's put-call signal earns more than 40 bp the next day, but it relies on proprietary *open-buy* volume, and publicly observable volume predicts for only one or two days ([NBER w10925](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10925/w10925.pdf)). AAPL, MSFT and NVDA sit at the opposite extreme of every one of these conditioning variables. No post-2015 mega-cap-only replication exists for any of the three. Single-name volatility selling is mostly a levered stock view. TSLA's long-run implied volatility (0.63) sits below its realized volatility (0.66) ([stocknear](https://stocknear.com/stocks/tsla/options/volatility)), and the true short-vol component of covered calls adds only about 2% a year ([Israelov & Nielsen, FAJ 2015](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2015/covered-calls-uncovered)).

### Flow signals: aggregate short interest is the only market-timing contender

Rapach, Ringgenberg and Zhou's Short Interest Index posts an out-of-sample annual **R² of 13.24%** over 1990–2012 and beats 14 rival predictors ([JFE 2016](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:121:y:2016:i:1:p:46-65)). Its value is concentrated in the late 1990s and 2008–09, it underperforms in 2000–07, and a later combined-predictor study puts its OOS R² at −0.78% ([CXO](https://cxoadvisory.com/short-selling/aggregate-short-interest-and-future-stock-market-returns)). The single-stock flow signals are weak for mega-caps. Opportunistic insider purchases earn about **82 bp a month**, but routine trades earn nothing ([Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski](https://www.nber.org/digest/apr11/decoding-inside-information)), and mega-cap insider activity is almost all 10b5-1 selling. Retail order imbalance predicts the cross-section for several weeks, but it needs TAQ data and does not forecast the market ([Boehmer, Jones, Zhang & Zhang slides](https://www.abfer.org/media/abfer-events-2017/annual-conference/investment-finance/22-3-a-xiaoyan-zhang.pdf); [AEA 2018](https://aeaweb.org/conference/2018/preliminary/paper/DG524NYr)). Days-to-cover predicts 5–60-day returns ([Boehmer et al., RFS 2022](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:35:y:2022:i:5:p:2428-2463.)), but mega-cap short interest is too thin to carry much information. One data trap needs flagging. FINRA's free daily short-sale *volume* is not short interest, and FINRA says the two "are not necessarily correlated" ([FINRA notice](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/information-notice-051019)).

### Alternative data: edges cluster around earnings and in paid panels

The canonical peer-reviewed result is satellite car counts. Traders anticipate retailer earnings news and earn abnormal returns around announcements ([Katona et al., JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/2F5F99D68D1F8940F61578F198D6C005/S0022109023001448a.pdf/on-the-capital-market-consequences-of-big-data-evidence-from-outer-space.pdf)). That means retailers, paid data and announcement windows, none of which match mega-cap tech's diversified global revenue and heavy coverage. The only free, high-frequency fundamental lead specific to this basket is **TSMC's monthly revenue**, released around the 10th. October 2026 was +14.7% month on month and +59.7% year on year ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/tsmcs-sales-jumped-15-in-october-nvidias-supplier-isnt-seeing-the-ai-slowdown-yet)). No study tests it as a predictor of NVDA, AVGO or SMH, and a widely circulated "12% alpha" alt-data claim has no verifiable source. TSMC revenue is the single most promising untested hypothesis in this report, because its mechanism, timing and free data line up.

### Macro and cross-asset: the Welch–Goyal verdict holds for tech

No source in the notes gives robust out-of-sample evidence that credit spreads, the dollar, oil, bitcoin or semiconductor-cycle data predict tech returns at daily-to-monthly horizons. The excess bond premium carries essentially all of credit spreads' equity-forecasting power ([Gilchrist & Zakrajšek](https://www.nber.org/papers/w17021)). Even so, that is a recession-risk channel, not a short-horizon signal. The in-house test checked 11 pre-specified predictors at two horizons on QQQ and the equal-weight basket, and **0 of 44 passed**. The strongest results were short-horizon index reversal (QQQ 5-day return → next day **−8.5 bp per σ, t = −2.79**) and rate shocks (10-year yield 5-day change → **−7.4 bp, t = −2.49**). Both had consistent signs and both failed Holm ([macro study](macro_results.md)). Bitcoin's prior-day return gave t = 1.23 and dropped to near zero in the second half. Treat it as a risk-on correlate, not a lead.

## A concentrated tech basket gains more from construction than prediction

### Weighting: 1/N and inverse-vol remain the benchmark

None of 14 optimizers consistently beats 1/N. Sample mean-variance needs about **3,000 months** of data to beat it with 25 assets ([DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal, RFS 2009](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v22y2009i5p1915-1953.html)). Evidence for hierarchical risk parity (HRP) is mixed. It cuts variance in simulations but lags minimum-variance in walk-forward tests ([CBS thesis](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/76452070/1332322_Master_Thesis_Hierarchical_Risk_Parity.pdf)) and is generally not the best out of sample on other markets ([RBFin](https://periodicos.fgv.br/rbfin/article/download/89848/84802/201368)). The repo's backtest matches. Over 2015–2026, HRP sizing (Sharpe 1.261) did not beat the shipped inverse-vol model (1.285, bootstrap P = 0.695). Adding a trend-plus-reversal signal *lowered* Sharpe to 1.096 (P = 0.985). Next-day rank IC was **0.0106 (t = 1.67)** ([in-house backtest](backtest_results.md)). The model's alpha over equal weight was 2.71% a year with **t = 1.13**, so its gain over the plain basket is risk shape, not stock selection. With correlations this high, every risk-based scheme converges to similar weights, and the reliable lever is *total* exposure.

### Volatility targeting and trend: drawdown control, not free Sharpe

Moreira and Muir report large alphas from scaling exposure by inverse variance ([NBER w22208](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208)). In real-time implementations across 103 strategies, the managed version wins 53 and loses 50, only 8 wins are significant, and the results are gross of costs ([Cederburg et al., JFE 2020](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)). For US equities, Man Group finds vol scaling lifts Sharpe modestly, from about 0.40 to 0.48–0.51, and sharply cuts vol-of-vol ([Harvey et al., JPM 2018](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)). Out-of-sample tests with costs find moving-average timing performance "highly overstated" ([Zakamulin](https://core.ac.uk/download/225890416.pdf)). Its value is avoiding deep drawdowns like 2022, at the cost of whipsaw. Time-series momentum survives mainly through vol scaling: without it, monthly futures alpha falls from 1.27% to 0.41% ([Quantpedia on Kim, Tse & Wald](https://quantpedia.com/deconstructing-the-time-series-momentum-strategy/)). Residual short-term reversal remains significant net of costs in large caps after 1990 ([Blitz et al., JFM 2013](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:16:y:2013:i:3:p:477-504)). It is the one stock-level directional effect with that credential, and it behaves like selling volatility. The repo's evidence supports the risk framing. The shipped model's max drawdown was −26.6% against −44.3% for daily-rebalanced equal weight (P = 0.036), while its Sharpe was statistically indistinguishable (P = 0.42).

### Diversifiers and concentration: the hedge has to come from outside the basket

The basket is a single AI-capex bet. In the DeepSeek shock of 27 Jan 2025 the Nasdaq-100 fell 3%, amid "correlated and crowded positioning in the AI theme" ([Reuters via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/01/28/analysis-us-tech-stock-tumble-highlights-risk-of-market-reliance-on-megacaps--)). Treasuries are a weaker hedge since 2022. AQR calls a positive stock-bond correlation "the historical norm" ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/insights/perspectives/a-positive-stock-bond-correlation-is-a-terrible-reason-to-add-more-equity-risk-to-your-portfolio)), and Man finds it turns positive when inflation runs above about 2.5% ([Man](https://www.man.com/insights/bonds-bust-equities)). Trend-following earned **+27.4% in 2022**, then suffered a **−20.4%** drawdown from May 2024 to May 2025 ([Cambridge Associates](https://www.cambridgeassociates.com/?p=48375)), a reminder that diversifiers come with long dry spells.

The in-house universe study tested 12 variants against a Bonferroni bar of P < 0.0042. Adding **IEF+GLD raised Sharpe from 1.285 to 1.442 and cut max drawdown from −26.6% to −22.0%** (P = 0.030), and GLD alone gave 1.365 (P = 0.047). Both are promising, and neither clears the bar. Earning T-bill yield on idle cash was the only *certain* improvement (P = 0.000). It is mechanical: the model sits on average 14% un-invested, and 60% on risk-off days ([in-house universe study](universe_results.md)). The repo's latest commit already moves idle cash into SGOV. In the 2022+ regime the treasury sleeves' correlation with the model flipped from about −0.2 to about +0.05, which matches AQR's account.

From a data-engineering perspective, four rules decide whether a test means anything, and each has already caught a real error in this repo. First, **timestamp the information, not the record**. The Tesla-delivery 8-K contamination showed how a mislabelled event manufactures a t = 3.2. The 2026 pre-post trading shows that publication time is not information time. Second, **archive what vendors overwrite**. Polymarket's fine-grained history goes back only about 30 days ([dev.to](https://dev.to/matveyg/i-asked-polymarket-for-90-days-of-prices-and-got-30-coarser-points-got-me-462-577k)). yfinance option chains and analyst-revision fields are snapshots, and Kalshi splits live and historical endpoints at a moving cutoff ([Kalshi docs](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/historical/get-historical-cutoff-timestamps)). Any signal you might want in two years has to be logged daily from today. Third, **report power, not just p-values**. The prediction-market study's minimum-detectable-effect column is the right template, and most nulls in this domain only rule out large effects. Fourth, **pre-register and correct jointly**. Use Holm or Bonferroni across every test run, require the same sign in both halves, use HAC or block-bootstrap errors, and run date-shift placebos. The repo's studies do all of this, which is why their nulls are informative.

The cleanest next tests follow from the table. The TSMC revenue event study needs about 190 releases since 2010. It should use the release timestamp, measure from the US open after the Asia-hours release, and set a pass bar of |t| > 2.5 across 6 tickers × 3 horizons. A Campbell–Thompson out-of-sample R² test of VRP, COR3M and the Short Interest Index should predict 21- and 63-day QQQ returns from expanding windows, report 2010–19 and 2020–26 separately, and use the predictors only as exposure scalers unless both halves are positive. A forward-only, pre-registered re-test of the Tesla–SpaceX merger-odds and Fed-posts patterns should run on data after 2026-11-18. That is the only honest way to validate a result found by searching.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "Which signals predict mega-cap tech?" has a nearly empty answer at daily-to-monthly horizons. The academic decay literature, the skeptic replications and this repo's own Holm-corrected tests all converge on that independently. That convergence is the main finding: three different methods with different biases reached the same null. The positive finding sits one level up. The information that does survive is about *how much risk to carry* rather than *which direction to bet*. It includes index option-implied measures, a slow trend gate, the volatility of earnings weeks and the stock-bond regime. A tech basket whose risk is already a single AI-capex factor gains most from controlling that factor's exposure and pairing it with assets outside it.

Going forward, the scarce resource is data history, not new ideas. The plausible new edges are TSMC monthly revenue, macro-event prediction markets and LLM-scored overnight news. Each is cheap to collect, short on history and untestable today at the power needed to separate a 3 bp effect from zero. The highest-value engineering work is to start logging those series daily, with retrieval timestamps, and to schedule the out-of-sample test before seeing the data. A year from now that archive will either confirm an edge or, more likely, save the capital that a convincing backtest would have cost.
