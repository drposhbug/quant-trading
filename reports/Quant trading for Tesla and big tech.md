# Build a risk engine, not a crystal ball

For a solo trader, the most defensible quant model for TSLA, SPCX and the six mega-cap tech names does not try to forecast prices. It is a low-turnover, long-biased system. It weights the names by inverse volatility, scales the whole book to a volatility target, uses each name's own 12-month trend as a gate, cuts exposure when index-level stress shows up, and treats earnings, lockups and merger news as known jump events to size around rather than bet on. The evidence assembled here supports that conclusion strongly. Most of the strategy families that sound exciting for these names have decayed or died on large caps: classic post-earnings drift, raw short-term reversal, cointegration pairs, LLM news sentiment and per-ticker machine learning. What survives is mostly risk control: volatility scaling, regime filters that cut drawdowns, and avoiding cost and tax drag. A few narrow, conditional edges also survive, such as trend gating, residual reversal and selective earnings-volatility trades. The bar is high. QQQ has compounded about 10.4–10.8% a year since 1999, and 79% of professional large-cap funds trailed the S&P 500 in 2025. Backtest Sharpe ratios explain almost none of live performance. So a realistic goal is a QQQ-like return with shallower drawdowns, not reliable outperformance. SPCX is the exception that needs its own module. It has about 110 trading sessions of history, a float of about 5%, a lockup expiry of about 0.8B shares on 2026-12-08, and a possible Tesla merger. That makes it an event-risk problem, not a signal problem.

## Retail base rates say "default fail" — plan around that

Any design has to start from the base rate. Among Brazilian day traders who kept going for more than 300 days, **97% lost money and only 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage**, with no sign that they learned over time ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/paper/spawpaper/2019wpecon47.htm)). In Taiwan, **fewer than 1% of day traders reliably earned positive abnormal returns net of fees** ([Barber et al., J. Financial Markets 2014](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:18:y:2014:i:c:p:1-24)). Retail options traders lean toward cheap weekly contracts with **average bid-ask spreads of 12.6%**, and they lose money in aggregate ([Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya, JF 2023](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827)). In 0DTE SPX, daily retail losses reached about **$358K after daily expirations began**, and most of that was transaction cost ([BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options-1.1910634)). The stocks Robinhood users herd into most heavily go on to lose about 5% over the next month ([UC Davis](https://www.ucdavis.edu/curiosity/blog/what-effect-brokerage-robinhood-having-stock-market)). That matters here because NVDA and TSLA are the top two retail favorites ([iTiger relay of Vanda](https://www.itiger.com/news/1128478194)).

Your universe is the hardest place to find an edge. TSLA alone averaged about **$29B a day in traded value** in Oct 2023 ([Sherwood News](https://sherwood.news/business/tesla-stock-traded-most/)). These are the most liquid and most heavily analyzed stocks in the world. The one structural edge a small trader has, capacity, mostly disappears here. What is left is behavioral and cost discipline. Trade infrequently, keep spreads small, and hold long enough to get long-term capital-gains rates (0/15/20%) rather than ordinary rates up to 37% ([IRS Topic 409](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409)). The benchmark is demanding: QQQ has returned about **10.4–10.8% a year since its March 1999 inception, including an 83% drawdown in 2000–2002** ([Total Real Returns](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/QQQ); [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/01/08/if-you-invested-1000-in-invesco-qqq-etf-27-years/)). Professionals mostly fail to clear it: **79% of active large-cap funds trailed the S&P 500 in 2025** ([SPIVA YE2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-us-year-end-2025/)).

Backtests offer little comfort. Quantopian studied 888 algorithms with at least six months out of sample. Backtest Sharpe predicted live Sharpe with **R² < 0.025**, and the more backtests a user ran, the wider the gap between backtest and live results ([Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/)). Published anomalies lose roughly a third to over half of their returns after publication ([McLean & Pontiff PDF](https://www.gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)). Practitioners who have gone live commonly report getting about half of their backtest returns ([DEV postmortem](https://dev.to/maymay5692/why-your-backtest-lies-i-got-491-returns-in-testing-then-broke-even-in-production-62a)). One regulatory change matters for small accounts. The Pattern Day Trader rule and its $25,000 minimum were removed (SEC approval April 14, 2026, effective June 4, 2026, with broker phase-in through Oct 20, 2027) and replaced with intraday margin standards ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260423-sec-approves-amendments-to-finra-rule-4210-replacing-day-trading-margin-requirements-with-a-modernized-intraday-margin-standard)). That makes frequent trading easier. Given the evidence above, the ease is a trap, not an opportunity.

## Six strategy families, ranked by what survives on eight mega caps

No peer-reviewed study tests any of these strategy families on this exact universe. Every verdict below extrapolates from broad-universe evidence, and the extrapolation is almost always unfavorable to mega caps. The table gives the bottom line, and the prose after it explains why.

| Family | Best evidence | Verdict for TSLA / mega caps / SPCX |
|---|---|---|
| Trend / time-series momentum | Strong in diversified futures and individual stocks; vol scaling is the robust ingredient | **Use as a gate and tilt, long-only.** Prior Sharpe roughly 0.3–0.7 for a single name or 8 names |
| Cross-sectional momentum (8 names) | Works across firm sizes, but small portfolios crash hard | Weak tilt only; few independent bets |
| Short-term reversal / RSI | Raw effect has faded; residual reversal survives costs in large caps | **Small satellite, residualized, vol-aware** |
| Pairs / cointegration | Cointegration rarely persists; profits largely gone after costs | **Avoid** (NVDA/AMD 2023 is the cautionary tale) |
| Factor models | Free data; exposures are stable in sign | **Risk decomposition and hedging, not timing** |
| Earnings drift (PEAD) | Dead in large caps since ~2006 | **Avoid** |
| Earnings / event volatility | Implied moves tend to overprice, but it varies by name | **Selective, defined-risk satellite** |
| Single-name vol selling | Single-stock variance premium near zero; TSLA IV is roughly equal to or below realized vol | Mostly a levered equity view; index VRP is better |
| ML / LLM / foundation models | Works in large cross-sections; fails on single-ticker daily data | **Use for features and filters, not per-ticker forecasting** |

### Momentum survives, but mostly through volatility scaling

Time-series momentum is a real effect. An asset's own 12-month return predicts its next return across 58 futures markets ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Time-Series-Momentum)), and the effect shows up in individual US stocks from 1927 to 2017 across firm sizes and sub-periods ([Lim, Wang & Yao, JBF 2018](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/128366)). The headline numbers, however, depend heavily on volatility scaling. Without it, the monthly alpha in futures falls from **1.27% to 0.41%**, and the outperformance disappears in 2009–2013 ([Quantpedia on Kim, Tse & Wald](https://quantpedia.com/deconstructing-the-time-series-momentum-strategy/)). Scaling cross-sectional momentum by its trailing six-month realized variance **"virtually eliminates crashes" and roughly doubles its Sharpe ratio, from about 0.53 to 0.97** ([Barroso & Santa-Clara, JFE 2015](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:116:y:2015:i:1:p:111-120); [Swedroe/ETF.com](https://www.etf.com/node/94292.md)). Momentum does not weaken with firm size, and **long positions supply about half of momentum profits** ([Israel & Moskowitz, JFE 2013](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/The-Role-of-Shorting-Firm-Size-and-Time-on-Market-Anomalies)). That supports a long-only design. Shorting the losers is where the crash risk sits: momentum crashes cluster in sharp rebounds after market declines ([Daniel & Moskowitz, NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)). Small, concentrated portfolios are dangerous. One 20-stock momentum portfolio had a **−88% maximum drawdown** ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/momentum-effect-in-stocks-in-small-portfolios)). With 8 correlated names, a "top-k minus bottom-k" ranking is really a bet on a handful of episodes, such as NVDA in 2023–24 or META in 2022.

Two short-horizon variants fail for single names. Intraday momentum works at the index level: SPY's first half-hour return predicts its last half-hour ([Gao et al., JFE 2018](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414)). But one published SPY breakout strategy fell from a Sharpe of 1.33 in-sample to **0.60 in an independent replication** ([QuantConnect](https://quantconnect.com/terminal/cache/embedded_backtest_32f62726550888da5f2a06694ac37930.html)). In the cross-section of individual stocks, early-day returns predict *reversal* into the close, not continuation ([Notre Dame WP](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/EOD.pdf)).

### Mean reversion works only after you strip out the factors

Raw short-term reversal used to look attractive among the 100 largest US stocks: about **+29 bp a week net and a Sharpe of about 1.09**. That sample ends in 2009, and the classic effect has since "largely disappeared in most regions" ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/short-term-reversal-in-stocks)). What survives is **residual reversal**, measured after removing factor and industry moves. It remains significant net of costs even when restricted to large caps after 1990 ([Blitz et al., J. Financial Markets 2013](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:16:y:2013:i:3:p:477-504)). Reversal pays traders for supplying liquidity, so it behaves like a short-volatility trade and its returns spike during stress (Nagel, via [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/short-term-reversal-in-stocks)). There is a structural catch for this universe. NVDA, MSFT and AAPL make up much of XLK and QQQ, so regressing them on those ETFs shrinks the residual and biases the beta. Build the residual against an ex-self or equal-weight benchmark instead.

Pairs trading is the family to skip. Among S&P 500 pairs that tested as cointegrated, **only 4.5–7.2% tested as cointegrated again** later, over 2002–2012 ([CXO on Clegg](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/essential-assumption-of-pairs-trading-wrong)). Distance-method returns fell from about 1.24% a month to 0.33% ([Quantpedia on Do & Faff](https://quantpedia.com/?p=585)), and the strategy becomes largely unprofitable after commissions, impact and short fees ([arXiv review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.01157)). Mega-cap tech is close to the worst case, because each name carries its own large narrative. **NVDA rose 214% against AMD's 72% in 2023** ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2023/07/29/better-ai-chip-stock-amd-vs-nvidia)), which would have kept a fixed-hedge spread signaling "short NVDA" all the way up. A Kalman-filtered hedge ratio does not fix this. It makes a broken spread look stationary by absorbing the drift as it happens.

### Factors describe these stocks; they do not time them

Daily factor returns are free from the [Ken French Data Library](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html), [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets) (QMJ, BAB, HML Devil) and the q-factor library, and [getfactormodels](https://github.com/x512/getfactormodels) wraps several of these sources in one downloader. Two Sigma's Venn analysis of 2015–2025 finds **TSLA's market beta was 1.95 and NVDA's 1.84**, and that most of the Mag-7's outperformance was residual return, "the hardest component to predict" ([Two Sigma Venn](https://www.venn.twosigma.com/insights/quantifying-the-magnificence-of-the-magnificent-7)). These names load negatively on value, positively on quality and often strongly on momentum ([BlackRock](https://blackrock.com/us/individual/investment-ideas/what-is-factor-investing/factor-commentary/andrews-angle/factors-and-magnificent-seven)). With seven or eight stocks all in the large-cap bucket, sorting them on factors tells you little. Use factors for three jobs: decomposing risk, constructing residuals, and hedging with beta.

### Earnings drift is dead; earnings volatility is the remaining event edge

For large stocks, post-earnings drift has been **non-existent since about 2006**, because prices absorb the surprise on announcement day ([Martineau, CFR 2022](https://nowpublishers.com/article/Details/CFR-0122)). The earnings announcement premium, about 9.9% annualized for firms scheduled to report ([Savor & Wilson](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Draft20111215p_edited.pdf)), is weak and may be fading ([IIMB](https://www.iimb.ac.in/ARC2020/Papers/The_Disappearing_Earnings_Announcement_Premium.pdf)). Most classic event anomalies have decayed too. The S&P deletion effect went from −12.4% in the 2000s to −0.6% in 2010–20 ([Alpha Architect on Greenwood & Sammon](https://alphaarchitect.com/disappearing-index-effect/)), and the pre-FOMC drift "essentially disappeared after 2015," though that finding is disputed ([Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert](https://www.skidmore.edu/economics/documents/KurovWolfeGilbert-TheDisappearingPre-FOMC-Announce-Drift-200914.pdf)). The "sell the news" pattern after TSLA delivery beats is only anecdotal ([finanzen.net](https://finanzen.net/nachricht/aktien/tesla-q3-deliveries-smash-estimates-but-wall-street-wasn-t-impressed-what-gives-14980566)). Treat deliveries, CPI and FOMC as volatility dates rather than directional signals.

The live edge is the gap between implied and realized earnings moves, and it varies by name. Across about 29,000 US reports from 2023–2026, the average implied move was **±9.18% against ±6.32% realized** ([EarningsWatcher](https://earnings-watcher.com/wiki/options-implied-move-vs-actual-earnings-study-2026)). NVDA's implied move overstated the realized move in **14 of 20 quarters** ([syndicated Cboe LiveVol report](https://1strespondernews.com/first-dry/Nvidia-PostEarnings-Volatility-Options-Pricing-Overestimated-Swings-in-14-of-Last-20-Quarters-19-1570)). META's realized move exceeded its implied move in 6 of 12 quarters ([ORATS](https://orats.com/blog/meta-earnings-expected-move)). TSLA's record conflicts. ORATS reports recent realized moves of about 4.3% against 6.3% implied, after averaging 12.9% realized in 2023–24 ([ORATS](https://orats.com/blog/tesla-earnings-market-wild-card-went-quiet)). EarningsWatcher says the moves beat the implied move about 70% of the time since 2022 ([EarningsWatcher](https://earnings-watcher.com/wiki/tsla-implied-move)). The two sources measure different things, so treat TSLA as having no reliable sign. A blanket "always sell IV crush" rule is also wrong. Long at-the-money straddles bought three days before earnings historically earned **+3.34%** ([Gao, Xing & Zhang, JFQA 2018](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/anticipating-uncertainty-straddles-around-earnings-announcements/7B34877AD5E06304BA3C55FBA3219FDD)).

### Single-name volatility selling is mostly a levered stock bet

The variance risk premium is reliably large for indices but small or close to zero on average for individual stocks. Much of the index premium is a **correlation risk premium** that a single-name seller never collects ([Driessen, Maenhout & Vilkov via CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=2110); [Carr & Wu, RFS 2009](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:22:y:2009:i:3:p:1311-1341)). TSLA's long-run average implied volatility is **0.63 against 0.66 realized**, which leaves no premium for sellers, while NVDA's is about 0.55 against 0.47 ([stocknear TSLA](https://stocknear.com/stocks/tsla/options/volatility); [stocknear NVDA](https://stocknear.com/stocks/nvda/options/volatility)). Covered calls break down mostly into equity beta plus an uncompensated timing bet. The genuine short-volatility component **adds only about 2% a year** ([Israelov & Nielsen, FAJ 2015](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2015/covered-calls-uncovered)). The single-name income ETFs show the cost: TSLY was **down 39.3% on price** year-to-date as of Aug 2026 ([24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/etf/2026/08/21/tsly-pays-more-each-week-than-nvdy-but-holders-are-losing-money-faster/)). The microstructure has also changed. Since Feb 2026, TSLA, NVDA, AAPL, AMZN, META, GOOGL, MSFT and others list Monday and Wednesday expirations, and 0DTE's share of their options volume rose **from 15% to 33%** by May 2026 ([MEMX](https://memx.com/insights/retail-active-in-ai-infrastructure-growth-in-zero-day-options-volume)). Any options backtest from before 2026 describes a different market.

### Machine learning needs breadth that eight tickers cannot supply

Gu, Kelly & Xiu's well-known result, that nonlinear models roughly double predictive R², reflects **monthly cross-sectional R² of about 0.3–0.7%** across about 30,000 stocks. The 2.45 Sharpe is equal-weighted and tilted toward small caps; value-weighted, it is 1.35 ([Mirova summary](https://www.research-center.mirova.com/en/research-library/Empirical-Asset-Pricing-via-Machine-Learning)). Most of the tradable profit disappears once microcaps are excluded and costs are added ([Avramov, Cheng & Metzker](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3450322)). Zero-shot time-series foundation models post **negative out-of-sample R² (−1.37% for Chronos, −2.80% for TimesFM)** on daily returns ([arXiv 2511.18578](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1)). LLM headline sentiment is the strongest recent result, but it is concentrated in small caps and bad news. Its Sharpe decayed **from 6.54 to 1.22 by 2024**, and it becomes **unprofitable at 20 bp per trade** ([Lopez-Lira & Tang v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)). Look-ahead bias from LLM memorization inflates many published results ([arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.23847v1)). By the fundamental law, one stock gives a breadth of one per period. Even a genuine IC of 0.03 produces a poor information ratio on TSLA alone. If you use ML, pool training across a broad universe, as Sirignano & Cont found works better than stock-specific models ([arXiv 1803.06917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06917v1)), and use it to filter or size a primary signal rather than to forecast TSLA directly.

## SPCX is an event-risk problem with 110 days of history

SpaceX, which absorbed xAI in Feb 2026, has traded on Nasdaq as SPCX since **2026-06-12**. It IPO'd at $135 with a **float of about 5%**, listed options from 06-16 and joined the Nasdaq-100 on 07-07 ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/spacex-stock-price-ipo-spcx-initial-public-offering/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_public_offering_of_SpaceX); [Saxo](https://www.home.saxo/content/articles/options/when-the-chain-opens-trading-options-on-the-fresh-spacex-spcx-listing-17062026)). The stock went from about $211 at its peak to a 52-week low of $104.83, and it was about $142–145 in mid-November ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/equities/spacex); [Gotrade](https://www.heygotrade.com/en/news/spacex-spcx-stock-news-november-18-2026/)). Its price is driven by supply. The main 180-day lockup, about **797.6M shares**, expires around **2026-12-08**. That is more than the entire IPO share count, according to third-party calendars that should be checked against the 424(b)(4) ([Quantral](https://quantral.com/blog/spacex-ipo); [Edgrapi](https://edgrapi.com/blog/spacex-lockup-expiration)). The October early release, two days after earnings, coincided with a reported **−9.1% day** ([StockAlarm](https://pro.stockalarm.io/blog/spacex-ipo-lockup-financials)). Musk and other insiders are locked up until about 2027-06-12. S&P 500 eligibility requires 12 months of trading plus profitability ([SpotGamma](https://spotgamma.com/spacex-ipo-index-changes-spotgamma/)).

The TSLA–SPCX relationship depends on the regime. The daily-return correlation was **0.10 from June to July and 0.81 in October** ([Opening Bell Daily](https://www.openingbelldailynews.com/p/stock-market-tesla-spacex-correlation-merger-earnings)). After removing Nasdaq-100 moves, the residual correlation was only **0.32** ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/tesla-is-trading-more-like-spacex-than-a-car-company-171534894.html)). Both companies have formed special committees to explore a merger ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-20/musk-says-spacex-tesla-have-formed-committees-to-explore-merger)), and an announcement could come as soon as December ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/news/spacex-tesla-merger-talks-december-texas-scrutiny/)). Once terms exist, the pair becomes a merger-arbitrage spread governed by the exchange ratio and the chance the deal breaks, so a static beta is meaningless. Implied volatility has fallen from about 169% at launch to roughly **55–65%** ([OptionCharts](https://optioncharts.io/options/SPCX)). The proxies are obsolete for price exposure. DXYZ holds only **10.6% SpaceX** and trades on its own premium or discount ([ipowatch](https://ipowatch.ca/company/dxyz/)). Alphabet's stake of about $78B is too small a share of GOOGL's market value to track SpaceX, but it does add mark-to-market noise to GOOGL's earnings every quarter ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/07/23/alphabet-just-revealed-a-94-billion-stake-in-space/)). In modeling terms, shrink SPCX's volatility and beta estimates toward a TSLA/QQQ prior, never splice pre-IPO tender marks into its return series, hard-code the event calendar, and cap its weight well below its risk-parity share until it has at least a year of history.

## Inputs and plumbing cost $0–300 a month; alternative data mostly flags risk

### Alternative data: useful as event and crowding flags, rarely as alpha

Most alternative data is weakest in exactly these names. Musk's posts have small or no effect on TSLA beyond the event day ([Corvinus J.](https://cjssp.uni-corvinus.hu/index.php/cjssp/article/view/977); [Princeton thesis](https://dataspace.princeton.edu/handle/88435/dsp01r781wk23g)), and the large effects documented for DOGE and BTC were measured over minutes ([Blockchain Research Lab](https://www.blockchainresearchlab.org/2022/01/13/the-musk-effect-new-research-on-the-impact-of-elon-musks-twitter-activity-on-cryptocurrency-markets/)). WallStreetBets due-diligence posts stopped predicting returns after GameStop ([Bradley et al., RFS 2024](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:37:y:2024:i:5:p:1409-1459.)). Google search spikes predict about two weeks of gains followed by reversal, mostly in retail-heavy small caps ([Da, Engelberg & Gao](https://rady.ucsd.edu/faculty/directory/engelberg/pub/portfolios/GOOGLE.pdf)). The most robust signal you can compute cheaply is **implied-volatility skew**. Steep smirks predict underperformance for up to six months, and the effect more than doubles around corporate events ([Xing, Zhang & Zhao](https://www.pbcsf.tsinghua.edu.cn/__local/A/24/EA/815CC33800CAA0F7D894E9292A1_6CC8B38B_27B25.pdf?e=.pdf); [Netspar](https://www.netspar.nl/wp-content/uploads/079_Driessen.pdf)). The Pan–Poteshman put/call signal needs paid open-buy classification. Free put/call volume is not the same signal ([NBER w10925](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10925/w10925.pdf)). Dealer gamma exposure is useful only as an intraday regime filter: momentum appears on negative-gamma days and not on positive-gamma days ([Baltussen et al., JFE](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/hedging-demand-and-market-intraday-momentum/)). Insider purchases matter only when they are "opportunistic," at about **82 bp a month**, so Musk's planned 10b5-1 sales carry little information ([Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16454.pdf)). Days-to-cover predicts returns over 5–60 days ([Boehmer et al., RFS 2022](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:35:y:2022:i:5:p:2428-2463.)). Weekly China insurance registrations are the main high-frequency read on Tesla sales, but it is the surprise against consensus that matters, not the level ([CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2025/03/04/china-ev-insurance-registrations-week-ending-mar-2-2025/)). Access has also tightened. X moved to pay-per-use with no free tier in Feb 2026 ([Open Terms Archive](https://opentermsarchive.org/en/memos/x-replaces-its-dollar200-entry-level-api-plan-with-a-pay-per-use-model/)), and NewsAPI's free tier is "not for use in production" ([NewsAPI](https://newsapi.org/pricing)).

### Data: daily bars are a commodity; options history is the real cost

For fewer than 20 tickers, daily bars are effectively free. A beginner stack costs $0: yfinance with local parquet caching (it is unofficial and rate-limited by IP; [GitHub #2422](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/issues/2422)), Alpaca Basic for IEX real-time and 15-minute-delayed SIP data, SEC EDGAR companyfacts for fundamentals (10 requests a second, User-Agent required; [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/os/accessing-edgar-data)), FRED for macro, and French/AQR for factors. The serious-hobbyist upgrade starts with **Alpaca Algo Trader Plus at $99/mo**, which adds full SIP and OPRA options quotes ([Alpaca docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/about-market-data-api)). Massive (formerly Polygon) Stocks Advanced at $199/mo is the alternative if you want 20+ years of history ([massive.com](https://massive.com/pricing)). The one costly item is honest options history: ORATS sells 2-minute snapshots from 2015 for **$199/mo or $2,000 one-time**, plus near-EOD data from 2007 ([ORATS](https://orats.com/two-minute-data); [ORATS near-EOD](https://orats.com/near-eod-data)). Databento's $125 in free pay-as-you-go credits covers an occasional tick sample. Its new subscription tiers are priced for institutions ([Databento blog](https://databento.com/blog/updates-to-subscription-pricing)). Survivorship bias barely affects a fixed list of tickers. Choosing the list by today's market caps is itself a hindsight bias, though, and it should be stated explicitly in every result.

### Brokers: Alpaca to start, IBKR to graduate, Tradier or tastytrade for options

Alpaca has the cleanest SDK, free paper trading, $0 options commissions and 24/5 API trading with limit orders only overnight ([Alpaca](https://alpaca.markets/trading-api); [Alpaca 24/5](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/245-trading)). It takes payment for order flow and limits trading requests to about 200 a minute ([Alpaca 606](https://files.alpaca.markets/disclosures/library/SEC+606a1+-+2026Q1.pdf); [Alpaca rate limits](https://alpaca.markets/support/usage-limit-api-calls)). IBKR Pro charges $0.0005–0.0035 a share, takes no PFOF and controls routing, but it needs an always-on TWS or Gateway process. Use it through **ib_async**, the maintained successor to ib_insync ([IBKR commissions](https://investors.interactivebrokers.com/en/pricing/commissions-stocks.php); [PyPI ib_async](https://pypi.org/project/ib_async)). For options-heavy work, **Tradier Pro at $10/mo charges $0 on equity options** ([Tradier](https://tradier.com/individuals/pricing)), and tastytrade charges $1 a contract to open and $0 to close ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/brokers/tastytrade-review/)). Robinhood has no documented REST API for stocks. Its "Agentic Trading" beta runs through MCP ([Robinhood](https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/robinhood-is-now-open-to-agents/)). For the backtester, **backtrader is effectively abandoned (last commit April 2023)**. Use vectorbt for fast parameter research. Use LEAN or NautilusTrader when you want one codebase for backtest and live trading; both connect to IBKR, and NautilusTrader does not support Alpaca ([GitHub snapshot via NautilusTrader README](https://github.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader)). LEAN's default slippage model is **zero**, so set one explicitly ([LEAN docs](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/reality-modeling/slippage/key-concepts)).

## Discipline beats signals: validation, sizing, regimes and combination

### Validate as if every result is a false positive

Log every configuration you try, because the trial count N drives the multiple-testing correction. Report the **Deflated Sharpe Ratio** ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)) and the **Probability of Backtest Overfitting** from CSCV ([Bailey et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)). Require a t-stat above 3.0, not 2.0 ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)). Harvey and Liu's haircut is nonlinear: **above 50% for Sharpe ratios below 0.4, and at most 25% above 1.0** ([Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF)). With about 45 variations, a Sharpe of 1.0 or higher becomes achievable by chance alone ([Bailey et al.](https://ioandc.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/backtestfail.pdf)). Prefer combinatorial purged cross-validation (CPCV) to a single walk-forward path. In a 2024 controlled study it produced lower PBO and higher DSR ([Arian et al. preprint](https://www.scribd.com/document/725401650/SSRN-id4778909)). Shift every signal one bar forward, and purge and embargo any training labels that overlap the test window. Model costs at about 2–5 bp per side, double them as a stress test, and include a 2022-style tech drawdown. For anything trend-based, also test against QQQ from 2000 onward. Quoted spreads in AAPL and MSFT are about 1 bp ([tapeboard](https://tapeboard.com/blog/what-is-the-bid-ask-spread)), so for a monthly strategy the real costs are taxes and overnight gaps, not commissions.

### Size by volatility, cap by fractional Kelly, and respect the gap

Volatility targeting is best justified as risk control, not as a source of return. For US equities, Man Group finds scaling raises the Sharpe from about **0.40 to 0.48–0.51**, cuts vol-of-vol from 4.6% to 1.8% and softens the worst months ([Harvey et al., JPM 2018](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)). The claim that it adds alpha is fragile. Real-time versions won in only 53 of 103 equity strategies ([Cederburg et al., JFE 2020](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:138:y:2020:i:1:p:95-117)). Kelly sizing is dangerous when the expected returns are estimated with error. **Half Kelly keeps about 75% of the optimal growth rate, and betting twice Kelly drives growth to about zero** ([Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_criterion); [MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf)). With eight names, inverse-volatility or 1/N weights are hard to beat, and HRP's advantage over them is modest ([SSRN 2708678](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2708678)). The eight names behave like one Nasdaq factor plus a TSLA-specific factor. The Mag-7 made up 34.3% of the S&P 500 in Dec 2025 ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/01/05/should-investors-be-worried-that-the-magnificent-s)), so measure risk in QQQ-beta terms and hedge beta with **Micro Nasdaq-100 futures**. They are $2 times the index ([CME](https://cmegroup.com/trading/equity-index/us-index/micro-e-mini-nasdaq-100_contractSpecs_futures.html)) and get 60/40 Section 1256 tax treatment ([Optimus Futures](https://optimusfutures.com/e-mini-nasdaq-futures.php)). Stops cannot protect you through earnings gaps, because the order fills at the gap open. Stop-losses lower expected return under a random walk and help only when prices have momentum or switch regimes ([Kaminski & Lo](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf)). Use slow, trend-like exits and size each position into its earnings date. Wash sales are a practical problem when you keep re-entering the same eight names. The window is 61 days, and repurchases in an IRA trigger the rule too, losing the deduction permanently ([Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/wash-sales-rules-tax)).

### Regime filters cut drawdowns; equal weights combine signals best

The strongest out-of-sample regime test found here is on the S&P 500 from 1990 to 2023, with costs and a one-day delay. A persistence-penalized jump model improved Sharpe from **0.48 to 0.68 and cut the maximum drawdown from −55% to −27%** at 44% turnover. A plain HMM traded 141% and actually *lowered* returns ([Shu, Yu & Mulvey](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05272v2)). Moving-average rules perform poorly on **individual securities** ([Philosophical Economics](https://www.philosophicaleconomics.com/?p=7296)), so drive regime signals from the index, not from TSLA's own price. VIX backwardation is a *contrarian* signal, followed by higher returns ([Macrosynergy](https://macrosynergy.com/?p=41000)), so treat it as "stress now," not "sell." The excess bond premium carries essentially all of credit spreads' forecasting power for equities ([Gilchrist & Zakrajšek](https://www.nber.org/papers/w17021)). For combining signals, the "forecast combination puzzle" applies: estimated weights rarely beat a simple average ([Frazier et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.05263)). The gain from combining comes from low correlation between signals, as value and momentum show with a correlation of about −0.49 ([Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/better-bet)). Monitor each sub-signal separately. Treat a 63-day rolling Sharpe as display only, since its standard error is about 2. Trigger kill rules on a CUSUM against the deflated expectation, or on a drawdown beyond 1.5–2 times the backtest maximum. Run the kill switch as an independent process with persisted state ([QuantInsti](https://www.quantinsti.com/articles/monitor-trading-bot-real-time/)).

## The recommended model: a vol-targeted trend core with event-aware satellites

The design follows from the evidence: put most of the risk budget in robust, low-turnover components, and give small, separately monitored budgets to the narrow edges. The thresholds below are starting values to validate under CPCV and DSR. They are not fitted results.

| Layer | Specification | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|
| Universe | TSLA, AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, GOOGL, AMZN, META, plus SPCX (capped at 5% NAV until it has 252 sessions) | SPCX's short history and lockup supply |
| Core weights (~80% of risk) | Inverse 60-day EWMA volatility; TSLA risk contribution capped at 30–35% | Inverse-vol is a hard baseline to beat at N=8 |
| Trend gate | Hold a name at full weight if its 12-1 month return > 0, half weight if it is negative but price is above its 10-month MA, zero otherwise; long only | TSMOM in single stocks; the long leg carries about half the profit |
| Book scaling | Scale to 20–25% annualized volatility; gross capped at 1.0x (1.2x maximum); cap set by quarter-Kelly using shrunk means | Vol targeting reduces tail months; overbetting is costly |
| Regime multiplier | Average of three index flags: NDX above its 10-month MA; HY OAS/EBP z-score < 1; VIX/VIX3M not inverted for 5+ days. Map to 1.0 / 0.75 / 0.5 with 5-day hysteresis | Jump-model persistence; credit EBP; index rather than single-stock filters |
| Event overlay | Before each earnings date, cut the position so a 1.5–2x implied-move gap costs ≤1% NAV. Flatten or collar SPCX into 2026-12-08 and any merger news. Flag TSLA deliveries, CPI and FOMC as event days in the vol estimator | Gaps cannot be stopped; event clustering |
| Satellite A (~10% of risk) | Residual 5-day reversal against an ex-self equal-weight tech basket, beta-neutral via MNQ, sized down when VIX is high, no positions held through earnings | Residual reversal survives costs in large caps |
| Satellite B (~10% of risk) | Defined-risk earnings iron condors or put spreads, only when implied move / trailing realized move > 1.2 for that name (NVDA-type cases). Skip names with no reliable sign (TSLA, META) | Name-specific implied-move overpricing |
| Hedge | MNQ short sized to the book's beta-dollar exposure when the regime multiplier is ≤ 0.5 | Common-factor risk; 1256 tax treatment |
| Rebalance | Monthly, with ±20% weight bands; event overlay runs daily | Low turnover limits tax and cost drag |

Combine the core and satellites by equal risk weight. Do not optimize the split; the forecast combination puzzle says the optimization will cost you. Benchmark every result against four alternatives over the same dates: QQQ buy-and-hold, an equal-weight buy-and-hold of the eight names, the same basket with inverse-volatility weights and no signals, and TSLA buy-and-hold. Compare them after tax. If the full model does not beat the plain inverse-vol basket on a deflated, after-cost basis, ship the plain basket. That outcome is a legitimate success. Set realistic expectations up front: a backtested Sharpe of 0.5–0.8 is plausible, and live results will come in at about half of the backtest edge.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "a quant model for TSLA and big tech" should mean. In this universe the edges are not in prediction. Prediction is where institutions, market makers and the herding retail crowd collide, and where nearly every documented signal has decayed. The edges that remain are in the parts most hobbyists skip: sizing to volatility, dodging gaps, keeping turnover and taxes low, and refusing to trust a backtest until it has survived deflation. Two things in 2026 make that discipline more valuable than ever. Twice-weekly extra expirations have reshaped short-dated options flow in exactly these names, and SPCX's supply calendar and possible merger with Tesla are pending.

The most useful practical change is to treat SPCX and the Tesla–SpaceX merger as a scheduled-event module rather than a signal. Over the next few months, the 12-08 lockup, possible merger terms and Q4 earnings will drive SPCX and the TSLA–SPCX link more than any model will. The second change is to accept a null result. If the full model cannot beat a vol-targeted, inverse-volatility basket of the same names after tax and after deflation, the basket is the right model, and building the system to find that out is what keeps you out of the 97%.
