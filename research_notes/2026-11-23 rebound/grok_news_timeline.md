## 5. Could a retail investor have bought the pre-jump price?

**Yes, but only before 8:55 a.m., and not at Friday’s 4 p.m. price.**

Regular hours are **9:30 a.m.–4:00 p.m.** Extended hours at most brokers run about **4:00–8:00 p.m.** and **4:00–9:30 a.m.**, usually **limit orders only**, with wide spreads. Some brokers add overnight single-stock trading (Public advertises Sunday **8:00 p.m.** through Friday **8:00 p.m.**). CME equity-index futures trade almost around the clock from Sunday **6:00 p.m.**, and that session is where the 7% limit applies. Overnight stock prints are thin. One outlet said it **could not read Nvidia trades between Friday evening and Monday’s 4:00 a.m. open.**

Where Nvidia actually was, from Friday’s **$125.53**:

- **Friday 7:59 p.m.:** $130.01 (**+3.6%**). About a tenth of Monday’s close-to-close gain was already behind it.
- **Monday 4:00–4:12 a.m.:** about **$135.30–$136.85 (+8–9%)**. Roughly a quarter of the day’s eventual ~31.5% move.
- **About 8:00–8:50 a.m.:** still about **+7% to +8.5%**. The news was not in the price.
- **9:30 a.m.:** about **$167 (+33%)**. The whole jump, and a bit more than the closing gain, was already in.

Someone awake and permissioned for premarket could still have bought near **+$8%** until 8:50 a.m. Anyone whose first window was the opening bell paid the gap. Futures were the cleaner overnight vehicle, and they were **not** limit-up until the headline; after 8:55 they locked at **+7%** and stayed there into the bell.

## 6. Judgment

This was a key-person and governance shock that reversed in eleven days, with the White House in the loop, not a change in Nvidia’s orders or in the rate outlook. The tape looks like a short-covering and sentiment squeeze: futures hit an upside limit that has been used only twice before on the way up since 2007, and one stock added on the order of $1 trillion of value in a session. Days structured like that often bounce further for a few sessions because the market is stretched and under-owned, which is the Detrick point, and the S&P did finish still under its 200-day and 7% under the October high. The base rate is not “the low is in.” Large gap-ups inside stressed markets, including the 2000–02 cluster, were higher a year later only about half the time in the count TheStreet cited, and a large share of day-one scandal reversals give back part of the gap even when the business survives. Goltermann’s list — a hawkish Fed, funding, and earnings that still have to be delivered — plus an open SEC review, an unfiled 10-Q, and December testimony are the items that decide whether Monday was a durable low or a relief rally. The weight of what was actually resolved on Monday is the CEO overhang only.
