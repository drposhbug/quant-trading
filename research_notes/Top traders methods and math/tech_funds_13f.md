# Tech-focused funds and big-tech investors: theses, moves, 13F crowding, and whether 13F cloning works (2022–2026)

Source-quality note: most fund-level figures below are reported by Bloomberg, Reuters, WSJ or Institutional Investor and then repeated by aggregators (Benzinga, iTiger, Hedgeweek, Motley Fool). 13F share counts are documented filings, but here they are often taken from third-party summaries rather than EDGAR. Fund returns are never in 13Fs. They come from investor letters that the press has seen. "Documented" below means a 13F, a 13D, or the manager's own statement on the record. "Reported" means press coverage of letters or unnamed sources.

## Q1: Major moves and outcomes (Tiger Global, ARK, Coatue, Situational Awareness, Altimeter, Duquesne, plus Lone Pine, D1, Viking, Light Street)

### Takeaway
Concentrated growth-tech funds were hit hard in 2022: Tiger -56%, D1 -30.5%, Viking -23.2%, Coatue -18.8%, ARKK about 81% off its peak. They recovered strongly in 2023–24 by holding mega-cap AI winners: in 2024 Light Street +59%, D1 public book +45%, Lone Pine +36%, Tiger +24%, Coatue +19%. Notable misses were selling NVDA early (Druckenmiller, ARK) and not owning NVDA at all (D1, and in mid-2024 Viking and Coatue had it outside their top five). The cautionary tale of the period is Situational Awareness LP: it returned 47% in H1 2025 and about 80% YTD in 2026, then fell about 67% in July 2026 on roughly 4x leverage and sold its public book to Citadel.

### Inferences
- The 2022 losses did not come mainly from mega-caps. They came from high-multiple, long-duration growth and private marks (Tiger's long-only -67%, ARKK -81%). Funds that kept or rebuilt mega-cap and semi exposure in late 2022 and 2023 recovered. Funds that sold NVDA on valuation (Druckenmiller, ARK) missed most of the 2023–24 move.
- Famous tech investors are often wrong on single names. Druckenmiller exited NVDA early, D1 did not own it, and Coatue and Viking had it outside their top five in mid-2024. Copying any one of them would have missed the period's biggest winner.
- Situational Awareness shows that thesis-correct but leveraged, concentrated AI-infrastructure books can lose two-thirds in one month. Its 13F, which shows longs only and with a lag, gave no warning of its leverage or its software shorts.

- Pension-fund positioning in the 11 names (e.g. NBIM, CalPERS) was not researched. They are largely index-like, so their weights roughly track market cap. This is an inference, not a sourced fact.

## Q2: Does 13F cloning ("guru tracking") work out-of-sample given the 45-day lag?

### Takeaway
The academic evidence says a manager's highest-conviction holdings ("best ideas") outperform even after disclosure: 2.8–4.5%/yr in the hedge fund version, more for small funds. That excludes costs. However, broad 13F-replication products have not delivered: GURU beat SPY in 2012–14, then lagged badly over the last decade. Cloning edge, if any, comes from concentrated, low-turnover managers' top positions, not from consensus aggregates.

### Cited Findings
- Cohen, Polk & Silli, "Best Ideas" (LSE/FMG DP624, 2005/2008): a mutual fund manager's highest-conviction stock outperforms the market and the manager's other holdings by about 39–127 bp/month, depending on benchmark. — [LSE PDF](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/BestIdeas.pdf); [FMG DP624](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/publications/DP624.pdf)
- The 2021 version (Antón, Cohen & Polk) extends this to hedge funds. Best ideas outperform by 2.8–4.5%/yr in the primary tests, and small funds' best ideas beat large funds' by about 15%/yr. Results exclude transaction costs and fees. — [LSE PDF](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/BestIdeas.pdf); [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/Screener/Details/42)
- A strategy based on quarterly 13F changes is reported profitable when holding roughly 1–2 months after filing (arXiv 2209.08825; seen in search summary, not full text). — [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2209.08825)
- Cao, Da, Jiang & Yang (Management Science): 13F restatements are common, and restated (initially hidden) holdings earn significant abnormal returns. Managers strategically delay disclosing their best positions, so the original 13F understates them. — [Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.08833); [ND PDF](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/Restatement.pdf)
- Fund returns fall after a fund starts filing 13Fs, and copycats are documented to erode target-fund performance (Shi 2017; Cao et al. 2018, as summarised). — [Masaryk Univ. paper](https://webcentrum.muni.cz/media/3528254/13f_smart_money.pdf)
- Mimicking 13F holdings can beat the market long-run, but choosing which funds to follow ex ante is very hard (Wharton analytics slides). — [Wharton](https://finance.wharton.upenn.edu/~mrrobert/resources/Analytics/2022-blp-phase3-slides.pdf)
- GURU ETF (tracks hedge funds' top 13F picks, equal-weighted, 0.75% expense ratio): +87.2% vs S&P +59.5% from June 2012 inception to Aug 2014. — [ETF Express](https://etfexpress.com/2014/08/05/global-x-guru-index-etf-celebrates-two-year-anniversary/); [Schwab](https://www.schwab.wallst.com/Prospect/Research/etfs/summary.asp?symbol=GURU)
- Ten-year GURU performance is about 5.9%/yr vs 10.8% for the S&P 500 on one PortfoliosLab page, and 6.8% vs 13.2% on another. Both are undated and conflict. Since inception (14.4 yrs to Oct 2026) GURU returned about 11.8%/yr. — [PortfoliosLab](https://portfolioslab.com/symbol/GURU); [PortfoliosLab GURU vs SPY](https://portfolioslab.com/tools/stock-comparison/GURU/SPY); [DividendVision](https://www.dividendvision.com/assets/tickers?ticker=GURU)
- Structural problems: 13F data is at least 45 days stale and the underlying trades may be months old. Shorts, most derivatives hedges, non-US holdings and leverage are invisible. — [ETF Trends](https://www.etftrends.com/2014/02/global-x-plans-to-expand-line-of-guru-etfs/2/); [Arkolith](https://arkolith.com/blog/copy-trading-hedge-funds)

### Inferences
- GURU's equal-weight, smid-tilted construction underweighted the mega-caps that drove 2015–2025 returns, which is probably why it lagged (ETF Trends notes the tilt hurt it). For a mega-cap-only universe, cloning adds little. Hedge funds as a group hold these names roughly in line with or below market weight (see Q3), so a consensus clone mostly resembles a noisier QQQ.
- The 45-day lag is decisive for fast books (Situational Awareness rotated its entire book within quarters, and the Q2 2026 13F could not show the July 2026 unwind). It matters less for slow, concentrated holders like Altimeter with NVDA.
- If anything is worth cloning, it is "best ideas": large, persistent, top-1-to-3 positions of smaller, concentrated managers. The edge is a few percent a year before costs and has likely decayed as 13F data became free (WhaleWisdom, Dataroma).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed out-of-sample test of 13F cloning specifically for 2022–2026 was found.
- No verified, dated GURU-vs-SPY total-return series for 2022–2026 was found. Pull it from Portfolio Visualizer or Morningstar to settle it.
- The Goldman VIP basket (Q3) is effectively a public 13F clone. Its long-run record (about 15%/yr vs 11% since 2001, per Goldman) is the best evidence for consensus cloning, but it is high-volatility and has failed sharply in crowding unwinds.

## Q3: How crowded are mega-cap tech names among hedge funds, and what does crowding imply for crash risk?

### Takeaway
Hedge funds' Magnificent-7 exposure peaked at about 21% of US net exposure in mid-2024. It was about 19% in Jan 2026, then fell to about 14.5% by mid-2026, with gross and net Mag-7 exposure near 3-year lows. Concentration in top-10 positions hit a record 72%, and semis net exposure was at the 98th percentile. Goldman's VIP basket (a 13F-consensus basket) beat the market in H1 2025, then had its worst month vs the S&P 500 in more than 20 years in July 2025 as AI crowding hit records. The academic evidence on crowding causing crashes is mixed, but crowded positions do carry fire-sale and liquidity risk, as the Situational Awareness July 2026 unwind showed.

### Cited Findings
- Goldman Sachs VIP basket: up about 10% YTD by Feb 2025 vs about 4% for the S&P 500, and about 16% YTD by the Q2 2025 list. Its long-run record is about 15%/yr since 2001 vs 11% for the benchmark, with high volatility. — [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/equities/25/08/47406673/top-hedge-fund-picks-from-goldman-sachs-outperform-sp-500-powering-16-gains-in-2025); [The Wealth Advisor](https://www.thewealthadvisor.com/article/goldman-sachs-q2-2025-hedge-fund-vip-list)
- July 2025: the VIP basket posted its worst month relative to the S&P in more than 20 years. Goldman said the AI rally had pushed hedge fund crowding to record highs. — [HedgeCo](https://hedgeco.net/news/?p=95505); [iTiger](https://www-web.itiger.com/news/1158974091)
- Goldman 2026 outlook (Jan 2026): Mag-7 was about 19% of US hedge fund net exposure, vs about 11% in early April 2025 and a peak of 21% in mid-2024. — [Goldman Marquee](https://marquee.gs.com/welcome/news/views-from-the-trading-floor/2026-hedge-fund-industry-outlook-generation-alpha)
- Mid-2026 (Goldman prime brokerage via press): Mag-7 fell to about 14.5% of US exposure, near a 3-year low. Mag-7 were net sold for 5 straight weeks to 30 Jun 2026, with gross exposure at the 4th percentile and net at the 6th percentile (3-year). Tech and Comm Services net selling was the largest in more than 10 years. Hedge fund "density" (top-10 concentration) hit a record 72%. Semis net exposure was at the 98th percentile (5-year). — [Tickmill/GS](https://www.tickmill.com/blog/institutional-insights-goldman-sachs-sp500-positioning-key-levels-30626); [FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/goldman-sachs-prime-brokerage-deep-dive-hedge-funds-start-july-on-the-defensive-202607060616); [Tradingpedia](https://www.tradingpedia.com/2026/05/04/funds-slash-tech-exposure-in-sharpest-10-year-drop/); [Kalkine](https://www.kalkine.com/news/premium/hedge-funds-cut-us-technology-stocks-as-magnificent-seven-exposure-falls)
- Large-cap mutual funds were 723 bp underweight the Mag-7, up from 710 bp in Q4 2025. This figure is for mutual funds, not hedge funds. — [Pulse/GS](https://www.pulse.bot/american-stocks/news/goldman-sachs-flags-723bp-underweight-to-mag-7-as-largecap-funds-lag-sp-500-0503bc7f-ed4c-4347-b846-9d64deb56602/)
- Academic evidence on crowding:
  - Barroso, Edelen & Karehnke find little evidence that crowding predicts momentum tail risk. — [AMF PDF](https://www.amf-france.org/fr/sites/institutionnel/files/private/2022-04/1_paul_karehnke_article.pdf)
  - Chincarini, Lazo-Paz & Moneta (2022) find anomaly returns concentrated in crowded stocks, and hypothesise that crowding raises crash, liquidity and fire-sale risk. — [EFMA 2022](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2022-Rome/papers/EFMA%202022_stage-3032_question-Full%20Paper_id-176.pdf)
  - MSCI finds a statistically significant hedge-fund-crowding factor with low correlation to standard factors. — [MSCI](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/blog-post/is-there-a-hedge-fund-crowding-factor)
  - Brown, Lundblad & Howard link herding to larger drawdowns in stressed markets. — [Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/investing/quant-investing/7420191/study-finds-a-significant-level-of-crowding-in-hedge-funds)
- Case study: the forced Situational Awareness unwind (July 2026) coincided with sharp falls in crowded AI hardware names (memory, neoclouds), and quant hedge funds were hit by AI-led crowded-trade reversals. — [SpotGamma](https://spotgamma.com/situational-awareness-unwind-margin-call-ai/); [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/ai-led-market-reversal-hits-quant-hedge-funds-as-crowded-trades-unravel/)

### Inferences
- In the 11 names, hedge fund crowding has moved away from the platform mega-caps (MSFT, AAPL, GOOGL, AMZN, META, TSLA) and toward semis and AI infrastructure (NVDA, AVGO, TSM, AMD, memory, neoclouds). The crash risk from crowding is therefore concentrated in the semi and infrastructure end.
- Crowding is a risk signal (fragility, unwind speed), not a return signal. Crowded baskets beat the market on average but suffer violent relative drawdowns (VIP July 2025, July 2026).
- Low hedge fund Mag-7 exposure plus large mutual fund underweights in mid-2026 suggests limited forced-selling overhang in the platform names. This is inference, not established.

### Gaps
- No primary Goldman Hedge Fund Trend Monitor text or per-stock crowding ranks (e.g. NVDA's VIP rank by quarter) were obtained.
- No study was found specific to mega-cap tech or AI crowding and subsequent crash frequency for 2023–2026.
- Multi-manager pod positioning (Citadel, Millennium, Point72) is not separable in the public data. Pods run near market-neutral, so their 13F longs overstate their directional exposure.

## Q4: Lessons for a retail long-only mega-cap tech system

### Takeaway
Use 13F and guru data as slow context, not as signals. The documented record shows elite tech investors routinely mis-time single names, broad clones underperform, and the worst losses came from leverage and concentration rather than wrong theses. A diversified, rules-based mega-cap system with drawdown control already captures most of what these funds earned in mega-caps.

### Cited Findings
- Elite managers sold or missed NVDA before its run: Druckenmiller exited by Aug 2024 and called it a "big mistake," ARK sold before the 2023 rally, D1 did not own it, and Viking and Coatue had it outside their top five in 1H 2024. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/10/18/billionaire-investor-druckenmiller-sold-nvidia-regrets-huang-musk-ellison/); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/index%2Ephp/article/2dxmif6h1s358kzxypou8/hedge-funds/why-these-tiger-funds-continue-to-outpace-the-market)
- Concentrated growth books fell 19–67% in 2022, and ARKK fell -81% peak-to-trough. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bstqfeujm1aoa25zfocg/portfolio/hedge-funds-lost-more-than-200-billion-last-year); [ChartRow](https://chartrow.com/quote/arkk/drawdown)
- About 4x leverage plus a long-hardware/short-software book turned a +445%-type run into a -67% month and a forced sale. — [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/07/30/2026/high-flying-hedge-fund-sells-holdings-to-citadel-after-ai-losses)
- GURU's 13F clone lagged the S&P over 10 years. — [PortfoliosLab](https://portfolioslab.com/tools/stock-comparison/GURU/SPY)
- Best-ideas alpha exists but is modest (2.8–4.5%/yr gross) and strongest in small funds. — [LSE](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/BestIdeas.pdf)

### Inferences
- Don't copy trades. Guru exits (Druckenmiller, ARK, Tiger's late-2025 trims) were not reliable sell signals for mega-caps.
- Stay unlevered and diversified across the 11 names. Every catastrophic outcome here (Tiger 2022, ARKK, Situational Awareness 2026) combined concentration with duration or leverage risk.
- Possible feature to test: a crowding or risk overlay, e.g. reducing semis weight when Goldman-reported semis net exposure is at extreme percentiles. Only data published in the press is available to retail, at irregular intervals, so a backtest would be hard.
- Possible feature to test: a "best ideas" tilt toward names that are top-3 positions across several concentrated tech funds (Altimeter NVDA about 19%, Tiger META/MSFT 10–16%) at the 45-day-lagged filing date. Prior evidence suggests a small edge at best. Expect it to mostly add tracking error versus an equal or cap-weighted mega-cap basket.

### Gaps
- No direct backtest exists of "lagged guru top-3 positions within the 11 mega-cap names" for 2022–2026. It would need an EDGAR 13F pull.
