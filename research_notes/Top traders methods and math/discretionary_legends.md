# Discretionary and Macro Legends: Documented Rules, Evidence, and Translation to a Retail Systematic Long-Only Mega-Cap Tech System

Scope: Druckenmiller, Paul Tudor Jones (PTJ), Soros, Dalio, Simons (as manager), Thorp, Livermore, Minervini, O'Neil (CAN SLIM), and Schwager's Market Wizards common principles. Research date: 2026-11-18. Budget-limited session (about 20 tool calls); many quotes come from secondary transcriptions, flagged where so.

Quote-confidence legend: **[V]** = wording confirmed in a transcript or near-primary source fetched this session; **[S]** = wording from secondary coverage, consistent across sources; **[M]** = from researcher memory of the book, NOT re-verified online this session (check the book before quoting); **[A]** = apocryphal or paraphrase-drift risk.

## Q1. What exactly did each say about sizing, stops, trend and concentration?

### Takeaway
The most consistent message across all of them is *asymmetry*: keep losses small and fast, let winners run, and size up only when conviction and evidence line up. They split on concentration. Druckenmiller, Soros and Livermore bet big. Dalio and Thorp/Simons bet small and wide, and size with math. PTJ's 200-day moving-average rule and O'Neil's 7-8% stop are the two most mechanical, implementable rules in the set.

### Cited Findings

**Stanley Druckenmiller (Lost Tree Club speech, 18 Jan 2015; Market Wizards interviews)**
- [V] "I learned something incredibly valuable, and that is when you see it, to bet big." (lesson credited to Soros) — [Lost Tree transcript gist (OCR of original PDF; may contain errors)](https://gist.github.com/timhwang21/e6a2b24e064182dd9099ad00e4f4f9a6)
- [V] Soros on the 1988 Deutschemark trade: "We should have 200 percent of our net worth in this trade, not 100 percent." Also "He was betting more money with my ideas than I was." — [Lost Tree transcript gist](https://gist.github.com/timhwang21/e6a2b24e064182dd9099ad00e4f4f9a6)
- [V] On concentration: great investors "tend to be very, very concentrated bets. They see something, they bet it, and they bet the ranch on it." And "put all your eggs in one basket and then watch the basket very carefully." He also said "only maybe one or two times a year do you see something that really, really excites you." — [Lost Tree transcript gist](https://gist.github.com/timhwang21/e6a2b24e064182dd9099ad00e4f4f9a6). Note that "eggs in one basket... watch the basket" is originally Andrew Carnegie / Mark Twain phrasing, which Druckenmiller reuses.
- [V] On being wrong: "a lot of times I'm wrong. And when you're betting the ranch and the circumstances change, you have to change." On his 1999-2000 tech round-trip: "I was just an emotional basket case and couldn't help myself... I didn't learn anything. I already knew that I wasn't supposed to do that." — [Lost Tree transcript gist](https://gist.github.com/timhwang21/e6a2b24e064182dd9099ad00e4f4f9a6)
- [V] On the time horizon and drivers: "never, ever invest in the present"; "you have to visualize the situation 18 months from now"; "earnings don't move the overall market; it's the Federal Reserve Board"; "It's liquidity that moves markets." He was skeptical of "diversification and all the stuff they're teaching at business school today." — [Lost Tree transcript gist](https://gist.github.com/timhwang21/e6a2b24e064182dd9099ad00e4f4f9a6)
- [S][A] The often-quoted line that "it's not whether you're right or wrong, but how much money you make when you're right and how much you lose when you're wrong" is Druckenmiller describing Soros's lesson (attributed to *The New Market Wizards*). The wording varies across sources, so it is a paraphrase, not a fixed verbatim quote from either man. Attributing it directly to Soros is secondhand. — [Business Insider slideshow](https://www.businessinsider.in/miscellaneous/slidelist/68189821.cms); [Morgan Stanley "Hard Lessons" video](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/videos/hard-lessons/duquesne-stan-druckenmiller-iliana-bouzali); [Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2017/11/stanley-druckenmiller-the-greatest-lesson-i-ever-learned-from-george-soros/)

- [S] The Soros/Druckenmiller sizing philosophy: what matters is the payoff asymmetry, not the hit rate, and you scale up when you are right. — [Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2017/11/stanley-druckenmiller-the-greatest-lesson-i-ever-learned-from-george-soros/)

**Paul Tudor Jones**

**Ray Dalio (*Principles*)**
- [S] The "Holy Grail": "with fifteen to twenty good, uncorrelated return streams, I could dramatically reduce my risks without reducing my expected returns" (*Principles*). In a 2011 Bloomberg interview he said 15+ uncorrelated streams improve return-to-risk "by a factor of five." The often-cited "reduces risk by 80%" is a derived or chart-based figure, not a Dalio quote. — [Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2018/01/ray-dalio-the-holy-grail-investing-strategy); [Macro Ops PDF](https://operators.macro-ops.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Ray-Dalio-on-The-Holy-Grail-Portfolio.pdf); [80% secondary claim](https://summify.io/discover/ray-dalio-the-one-rule-that-cuts-investment-risk-by-80-Uprafh)

**William O'Neil (CAN SLIM)**
- [V] 2002 interview: "My rule is simple -- any stock that I buy that declines 7% or 8% below my actual purchase price, I will always without exception, sell to cut short my loss." And "I will never average down in price." — [Motley Fool interview with Bill O'Neil, 2002](https://www.fool.com/specials/2002/06/20/foolcom-tmf-interview-bill-oneil-special.aspx)
- [S] Secondary guides add a tighter cut (about 3%) in weak markets, and say the 7-8% figure came from his historical studies of winners. — [Moomoo summary of the 7-8% rule](https://www.moomoo.com/my/learn/when-to-sell-stock-the-7-8-sell-rule); [Chartmill sell rules](https://www.chartmill.com/documentation/trading-and-investing/methodologies/524-Sell-Rules-for-Growth-Stocks-When-to-Get-Out)

**Mark Minervini (*Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard*, 2013)**
- [S] Maximum loss is about 8% or less. Stops may be staggered (for example 4% and 10%) to target an average loss around 8%, set as a fixed percentage below purchase and widened for volatile stocks. These come from secondary summaries; I did not retrieve the book text. — [Chartmill](https://www.chartmill.com/documentation/trading-and-investing/methodologies/524-Sell-Rules-for-Growth-Stocks-When-to-Get-Out); [skills.cat summary](https://skills.cat/skills/copyleftdev/sk1llz/minervini-swing-trading)

**Market Wizards (Schwager), common principles**
- [S] Schwager: "The closest thing to a single common denominator—but even that has its rare exception—is that almost all of them are pretty rigorous about risk management." — [CFA Institute interview with Schwager, 2014](https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/blog/2014/04/16/what-makes-a-great-trader-an-interview-with-jack-schwager/)
- [S] Other recurring points: many wizards failed early (blew up) before finding their edge; they judge risk at the portfolio level, not just per trade; and there is no single correct method, so fit the method to your personality. — [Validea](https://blog.validea.com/most-great-traders-failed-first-these-key-rules-saved-them-practical-lessons-from-jack-schwager/); [Antoine Buteau](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-jack-schwager/)

### Inferences
- The rules sort into four groups:
  - **Exits:** O'Neil 7-8%, Minervini about 8%, PTJ's "assume you're wrong."
  - **Regime filter:** PTJ's 200-day moving average.
  - **Sizing by conviction:** Druckenmiller/Soros. This is not mechanical. It depends on a rare, discretionary "see it" moment, one or two a year.
  - **Diversification and math:** Dalio's uncorrelated streams; Thorp's Kelly fractions.
- Druckenmiller pairs "bet the ranch" with fast reversal when "circumstances change." It is concentration *with* a tight exit, never concentration alone. The two cannot be separated.

### Gaps
- **Livermore:** I could not verify specific quotes this session. From memory [M], *Reminiscences of a Stock Operator* (Edwin Lefèvre, 1923) has the narrator say "It never was my thinking that made the big money for me. It always was my sitting," and describes pyramiding: adding only when the first position shows a profit. *Reminiscences* is semi-fictionalized, with Larry Livingston as the narrator. Many internet "Livermore quotes" actually come from Livermore's own 1940 *How to Trade in Stocks* or from later compilations. Flag any Livermore quote as [A] unless it is checked against the text.
- **Thorp:** I found no online source this session for his exact fractional-Kelly wording in *A Man for All Markets* (2017) or *Beat the Market* (1967). [M] Thorp advocated Kelly sizing and in practice used fractional Kelly (for example half-Kelly) because estimation error makes full Kelly too aggressive. Verify against the book.
- **Simons:** Not covered by fetched sources. [M] Known manager principles: hire scientists rather than Wall Street people, keep the model systematic and do not override it, and work on many small, weakly correlated bets with leverage. Medallion has been closed to outsiders since about 2005. Treat any "Simons trading rule" quote as [A] unless sourced.
- **PTJ in Market Wizards (1989):** [M] Lines such as "Don't focus on making money; focus on protecting what you have" and "Losers average losers" are recalled, but their exact wording was not verified this session.
- I did not retrieve the exact book text of O'Neil's *How to Make Money in Stocks* or Minervini's *Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard*.

## Q2. Which rules are supported by evidence, and which are not?

### Takeaway
Stops and trend filters are supported **only when returns have momentum or regime persistence**, which equities and momentum portfolios do show. Under a random walk, stops strictly cost return. The 200-day/10-month moving-average filter mostly cuts drawdowns in deep bear markets, and its out-of-sample edge after costs is much weaker than the popular backtests show. Fixed percentage stops (7-8%) have no direct academic support at that exact number. Discretionary "bet big" concentration has no systematic evidentiary basis for a retail trader.

### Cited Findings
- **Kaminski & Lo (2014), *Journal of Financial Markets* 18:234-254:**
  - "Under the Random Walk Hypothesis, simple stop-loss rules always depress expected return."
  - With momentum or Markov regime-switching returns, "stop-loss rules can stop losses."
  - Using monthly US equities 1950-2004, with long-term Treasuries as the stop-out asset, certain rules added 50-100 bps per month during stop-out periods (working-paper figure).

  — [MIT DSpace PDF](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:18:y:2014:i:c:p:234-254)
- **Han, Zhou & Zhu, "Taming Momentum Crashes: A Simple Stop-Loss Strategy":**
  - A 10% stock-level stop on a momentum portfolio, 1926-2013, cut the worst monthly loss from -49.79% to -11.36% (equal-weighted) and from -64.97% to -23.28% (value-weighted).
  - Average monthly return rose from 1.01% to 1.73%, and the Sharpe ratio went from 0.165 to 0.369.
  - These results are gross of costs. Critics flag data-snooping risk and high frictions in illiquid stocks, and note that the stops require daily monitoring.

  — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/stop-losses-to-avoid-stock-momentum-crashes/); [Alpha Architect critique](https://alphaarchitect.com/2016/08/taming-the-momentum-roller-coaster-fact-or-fiction/)
- **Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012), "Time Series Momentum," *JFE* 104(2):228-250:** a 12-month time-series momentum signal is significant across 58 liquid futures (equity index, FX, commodity, bond), 1985-2009. Returns are reported gross. This supports trend-following at the *asset-class/index* level, which is the level where PTJ's 200-day rule operates. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2089463); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:104:y:2012:i:2:p:228-250)
- **Century-long trend evidence:** Hurst/Ooi/Pedersen (AQR) "A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing." — [PDF](https://www.TrendFollowing.com/whitepaper/Century_Evidence_Trend_Following.pdf)
- **Moving-average timing critique (Zakamulin):**
  - In "Fooled by Data-Mining," out-of-sample tests with realistic costs find that the performance of Faber's 10-month SMA timing is "highly overstated," and that the 10-month rule was the best rule in-sample, which suggests data-mining bias.
  - On the S&P Composite, support for MA timing comes "mainly from the 1860-1942 subsample."
  - In a 1930-2012 out-of-sample test, only 3 of 8 strategy-index combinations beat buy-and-hold on net Sharpe, and the wins came mostly from the 1930-31, 1973-74, 2001-02 and 2007-08 bear markets.
  - Optimal lookbacks swing widely, and the rules lagged for decades at a time.

  — [Springer, J. Asset Mgmt](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/jam.2014.25); [CXO horse race](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/long-run-moving-average-horse-race-for-timing-the-u-s-stock-market); [CXO net performance](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/net-performance-of-sma-and-intrinsic-momentum-timing-strategies)
- **Diversification math (supports Dalio and argues against "bet the ranch" for anyone without an edge):** stock-only diversification gives limited risk reduction because stock correlations are about 0.6. — [the7circles](https://the7circles.uk/?p=5023)
- **Concentration and skewness:** Bessembinder, "Do Stocks Outperform Treasury Bills?" (JFE 2018). Only 42.6% of US common stocks beat 1-month T-bills over their lifetimes (1926-2016, 25,967 stocks), but nearly 70% of the largest-size decile did. Concentration in random stocks most likely underperforms, while a mega-cap universe is pre-selected toward the winners. — [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=29557)

### Inferences
- **Supported, conditionally:**
  1. **A trend/regime filter at the index level** (PTJ 200-day). It mainly cuts drawdowns in deep bears, at the cost of whipsaw and decades-long lag stretches. Expect a better Sharpe ratio and smaller maximum drawdown, not higher CAGR.
  2. **Stop-losses on momentum-type holdings** (Kaminski-Lo, Han-Zhou-Zhu). Mega-cap tech held because of trend fits the "non-random-walk" condition, but the benefit is fragile to costs and threshold choice.
  3. **"Cut losers, let winners run"** is consistent with time-series momentum, and with the evidence that never averaging down avoids the disposition effect.
- **Weak or unsupported:**
  - The specific 7-8% number. It is a heuristic; no academic test isolates it, and for mega-caps with 30-50% annual volatility, a fixed 7-8% stop is within normal noise.
  - "Bet big when you see it." It needs a discretionary edge that cannot be verified ex ante.
  - Livermore's tape-reading anecdotes.
- **Pyramiding** (add on strength) is roughly a sizing-by-momentum rule. It is consistent with TSMOM but adds path dependence and cost. I found no direct academic test of it this session.

### Gaps
- No direct academic study of O'Neil's 7-8% rule or Minervini's stops on large-cap US stocks was found.
- I did not verify the "4% of stocks create all net wealth" figure from Bessembinder's paper (it is widely cited, but it was not in the fetched results).
- No direct academic test of pyramiding was found.
- I did not find a study specific to a 200-day rule on single mega-cap stocks; the evidence is at the index or futures level.

## Q3. How much survivorship bias is there in learning from famous winners?

### Takeaway
A lot. The base rate for active discretionary traders is terrible, and the legends are a conditional sample. Schwager himself notes that many wizards blew up first, and failures are not interviewed. Their rules on *risk control* generalize better than their rules on *sizing up conviction*. Risk control is supported by evidence and costs little if wrong; conviction-sizing is exactly the part selected on luck.

### Cited Findings
- **Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?":** Brazilian futures day traders who persisted for 300+ days, 2013-2015. 97% lost money, only 0.4% earned more than a bank teller, and the best earned about US$310 per day. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101); [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/)
- **Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean (Taiwan day traders):** fewer than 1% are predictably profitable after fees, though another paper's description of the same study cites about 20% profitable net of fees in a given period. Sources conflict; persistence is the key point. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/)
- **Market Wizards selection:** many top traders failed first before finding an edge. The books interview only survivors. — [Validea on Schwager](https://blog.validea.com/most-great-traders-failed-first-these-key-rules-saved-them-practical-lessons-from-jack-schwager/)
- **The legends' own failures:**
  - Druckenmiller's 1999-2000 tech round-trip, in his words: "emotional basket case."
  - Druckenmiller's 2024 NVDA exit, in his words a "big mistake" (see Q4).

  — [Lost Tree transcript](https://gist.github.com/timhwang21/e6a2b24e064182dd9099ad00e4f4f9a6); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/10/18/billionaire-investor-druckenmiller-sold-nvidia-regrets-huang-musk-ellison/)
- **Data-mining parallel:** the "best" moving-average length in-sample (Faber's 10-month) underperforms out-of-sample, the same selection-on-outcome problem applied to rules. — [Springer (Zakamulin)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/jam.2014.25)

### Inferences
- Survivorship affects the two kinds of rules unequally:
  - *Defensive* rules (cut losses, trend filter, diversify) have independent academic support, so survivorship does not invalidate them.
  - *Offensive* rules (bet the ranch, "courage to be a pig," pyramiding into conviction) are the ones most contaminated by survivorship. Many traders who bet the ranch are not famous because they lost the ranch. The Kelly framework (Thorp) is the principled version: bet big only in proportion to a *measured* edge, and use fractional Kelly because the edge is estimated.
- These legends also had information, leverage, and access edges that a retail systematic trader lacks: macro and policy access, futures and options, and shorting, while this system is long-only.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative estimate of survivorship bias specific to famous macro managers (for example, a census of 1980s-90s macro funds and their failure rates). Hedge-fund database survivorship studies exist but were not fetched this session.

Druckenmiller bought NVDA early (Q4 2022, before ChatGPT launched) and added options in Q4 2023. He sold most of it in March 2024 and all of it by mid- or late-2024, then publicly called the sale a "big mistake." As of the Q1 2026 13F he had not re-entered NVDA, and had rotated into TSMC, then Broadcom, Arm and Intel. PTJ called markets "exactly like 1999" in Oct 2025 and was bullish on AI stocks into 2026, while warning of an eventual ugly end.

### Inferences
- **Druckenmiller's NVDA trade shows "bet big when you see it" working on entry:** an early, concentrated, levered position via calls. It also shows the discretionary *exit* failing. He sold on valuation or "heated" sentiment instead of on trend. A mechanical trend rule (stay long while above the 200-day or with positive 12-month momentum) would have kept the position through 2024-25. For a systematic trader, the lesson is to let trend, not sentiment, drive exits on winners.
- **PTJ's 2025-26 stance is consistent with his trend rule:** stay long while the trend is up, and be ready to exit fast. That is not a valuation-based short call.
- Prices for NVDA after 2024 were not fetched, so the size of Druckenmiller's "mistake" relative to Nov 2026 is not quantified here.

## Appendix-style synthesis (kept within the required sections): rules a retail systematic long-only mega-cap tech system could implement

The defensible translations, with evidence grade:

1. **Regime filter (PTJ 200-day; TSMOM).** Hold equities only when the index (for example QQQ or the equal-weighted universe) is above its 200-day SMA, or has positive 12-month excess return. Otherwise rotate to T-bills (the repo already parks idle cash in SGOV). Use a band (for example ±2%) or month-end checks to cut whipsaw. Evidence: moderate. It cuts deep-bear drawdowns; out-of-sample net Sharpe gains are modest (Zakamulin). Do not optimize the lookback.
2. **Per-stock trend exit instead of a fixed 7-8% stop.** For mega-caps, use a volatility-scaled stop (for example 2-3x ATR, or an exit when the stock is below its own 200-day or has negative 12-month momentum). Fixed 7-8% sits inside normal mega-cap noise. Evidence: Kaminski-Lo and Han-Zhou-Zhu support stops on momentum holdings; the specific threshold is not supported. Check that turnover costs do not eat the benefit.
3. **Never average down (O'Neil); add only to winners (Livermore pyramiding).** Implement as: weights rebalance toward momentum ranks, and no buys into names below their trend line. Evidence: consistent with momentum, but pyramiding has no direct test.
4. **Conviction sizing becomes capped Kelly / volatility targeting (Thorp, not Druckenmiller).** Size by inverse volatility, capped per name (for example ≤20-25%), with total risk targeted. Do not emulate "200% of net worth" without a measured edge. Evidence: fractional Kelly is mathematically grounded; discretionary "bet the ranch" is survivorship-contaminated.
5. **Acknowledge the concentration limit (Dalio).** A mega-cap tech universe is one highly correlated return stream (pairwise correlation about 0.6 or higher), so within-universe diversification buys little risk reduction. Real diversification needs an uncorrelated sleeve (T-bills or bonds via the regime filter). State this honestly in reports.
6. **Assume you are wrong (PTJ) and change when circumstances change (Druckenmiller).** Pre-commit to exit rules and run them mechanically. Druckenmiller's NVDA sale is the cautionary example of a discretionary override on a winner.
