# Options and Volatility Trading on TSLA, NVDA and Mega-Cap Tech (as of Nov 2026)

Research note for a retail quant. Most claims were checked against primary sources (papers, Cboe/MEMX pages, ORATS blog). Where only search-result summaries or secondary sources were available, the finding says so. Treat 24/7 Wall St., FlashAlpha, apexvol and stocknear figures as lower-quality.

## 1. Variance/volatility risk premium (VRP): single stocks vs indices, and TSLA/NVDA IV vs realized vol

### Takeaway
The academic evidence is that the VRP is large and reliably negative for indices (option buyers overpay) but small, mixed, or close to zero on average for individual stocks. Much of the index premium is a correlation-risk premium that a single-name seller does not collect. TSLA/NVDA data is fragmentary: NVDA often shows IV above realized; TSLA's long-run average IV is roughly equal to or below realized. Earnings-move overpricing for NVDA has held for several recent quarters but is not stable.

### Cited Findings
- Carr & Wu (RFS 2009, vol. 22(3), 1311-1341) synthesize variance swap rates for 5 indices and 35 individual stocks over 7 years. Average variance risk premiums are strongly negative for the S&P 500, S&P 100 and DJIA. Individual stocks show large cross-sectional variation, and premiums are more negative for stocks with higher variance beta — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:22:y:2009:i:3:p:1311-1341); [NYU PDF](https://engineering.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/2019-01/CarrReviewofFinStudiesMarch2009-a.pdf)
- The 2004 working-paper version reports that VRPs for the Nasdaq-100 and most individual stocks are also negative but smaller in absolute size (search-summary level; check the published version) — [Carr-Wu WP 2004](https://econwpa.ub.uni-muenchen.de/econ-wp/fin/papers/0409/0409015.pdf)
- Bakshi & Kapadia (RFS 2003) use delta-hedged option gains. With no VRP, expected delta-hedged gains are zero. They find negative delta-hedged gains on S&P 500 options, consistent with a negative market VRP that survives controlling for jump fears. This is an index study — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v16y2003i2p527-566.html)
- Follow-up work by Bakshi & Kapadia on individual equity options ("Volatility Risk Premiums Embedded in Individual Equity Options: Some New Insights") finds the individual-stock VRP does not match the index-implied premium (search-summary level) — [EconBiz](https://econbiz.de/Record/volatility-risk-premiums-embedded-in-individual-equity-options-some-new-insights-kapadia-nikunj/10012786432)
- Driessen, Maenhout & Vilkov: individual variance risk is not priced on average. The index VRP comes from a correlation risk premium, and index options are priced as if correlations are more than 20% higher than historically realized. Individual stock options do not imply a significant VRP (S&P 100, 1996-2003) — [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=2110); [Netspar PDF](https://www.netspar.nl/wp-content/uploads/061_Driessen.pdf)
- Another study of S&P 100 and Nasdaq-100 constituents finds stock options tend to be "cheap": the stock variance risk premium is positive or close to zero on average, depending on the sample (search-snippet level) — [Bocconi JMP PDF](https://finance.unibocconi.eu/sites/default/files/files/media/attachments/PiattiJMP20140130151934.pdf)
- A 2023 study finds that delta-hedged returns on individual stock options consistently decrease with volatility-of-volatility (search-summary level) — [City Univ. PDF](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/30606/1/2023Mar_VOV_QJF.pdf)
- NVDA (stocknear snapshot, date range unclear): average IV 0.55 vs average realized vol about 0.47-0.48, roughly 7-8 vol points of premium — [stocknear NVDA](https://stocknear.com/stocks/nvda/options/volatility)
- TSLA (stocknear, date range unclear): average IV 0.63 vs average realized vol 0.66. On average there was no premium for sellers — [stocknear TSLA](https://stocknear.com/stocks/tsla/options/volatility)
- Point-in-time snapshots from search results: NVDA in May 2026 showed IV 46.0% vs 20-day RV 42.5%; TSLA in Oct 2026 showed IV 36.8% vs RV 42.1% (inverted). These are single-day readings and not reliable — [Options Analysis Suite NVDA](https://www.optionsanalysissuite.com/stocks/nvda/iv-hv-history); [Options Analysis Suite TSLA](https://www.optionsanalysissuite.com/stocks/tsla/iv-hv-history)
- tastylive: implied volatility overstated the actual move 68% of the time for TSLA and 67% for NVDA (window not stated). Note that "overstated more often than not" does not mean profitable after tail losses — [tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/how-often-do-options-overstate-market-moves)
- ORATS on NVDA earnings: the average implied earnings move over the past 12 quarters was 7.6%, and actual moves "clustered near" that level. In one cycle the implied move fell to 5.6%, about 26% below average, and to about 0.9x XLK vs a historical 1.5x. ORATS argued earnings vol was underpriced in that cycle. A linked ORATS post says NVDA moved less than options implied after eight consecutive earnings reports — [ORATS: Is NVDA earnings volatility underpriced?](https://orats.com/blog/is-nvda-earnings-volatility-underpriced); [ORATS: NVDA expectations vs reality](https://orats.com/blog/nvidia-earnings-options-market-expectations-vs-reality)
- ORATS also cites NVDA pre-earnings IV averaging 44.8% vs realized vol between earnings averaging 48.2% over 12 quarters. On this measure realized was above implied (search-snippet level) — [ORATS](https://orats.com/blog/nvidia-earnings-options-market-expectations-vs-reality)

### Inferences
- A retail seller of TSLA/NVDA vol is mostly not harvesting the well-documented index VRP. The single-name premium is thinner, noisier and stock-specific. Systematic short-vol on indices (SPX/XSP/SPY) has stronger evidence than on single names.
- A high "IV overstates move X% of the time" hit rate is consistent with a negatively skewed P&L where rare large losses cancel frequent small wins. Measure the premium in P&L (delta-hedged or IV-minus-subsequent-RV in variance terms), not by hit rate.
- TSLA looks structurally closer to zero or negative VRP, given that its average realized vol is at least as high as its IV. NVDA's sign depends on the regime and window.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that estimates the VRP specifically for TSLA or NVDA over 2020-2026. A user would need to compute it from ORATS/Cboe data, e.g. 30-day ATM IV vs subsequent 30-day realized, or delta-hedged returns.
- The stocknear averages do not state their windows. The snapshot numbers may be from 2026 but are single days.

## 2. Covered calls, cash-secured puts, iron condors, earnings straddles/strangles (IV crush): evidence and tail risks

### Takeaway
Index put-writing and buy-write benchmarks show equity-like returns with lower volatility over decades. Decomposition work shows most covered-call risk is just equity beta plus an uncompensated "timing" bet; the short-vol piece adds only about 2%/yr. Single-name option-income ETFs on TSLA/NVDA have capped upside and eroded NAV. Long pre-earnings straddles historically earned positive returns (Gao-Xing-Zhang), so blindly shorting "IV crush" is not a free lunch. No credible single-stock iron condor backtest was found.

### Cited Findings
- Cboe PUT index (July 1986-Aug 2023): 9.40% annualized vs 9.91% for the S&P 500, with standard deviation 10.26% vs 15.38%. When the S&P's rolling 1-year return was below 0, PUT beat it 95% of the time. Source is the index sponsor — [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/generating-income-and-managing-risk-cash-secured-put-writing-in-a-low-equity-return-environment/)
- A Cboe-commissioned Wilshire study (1986-2016) found all five option-selling indexes (including BXM and PUT) had lower volatility than every other index studied except fixed income. Index backtests exclude transaction costs and taxes — [Cboe IR](https://ir.cboe.com/news/news-details/2016/New-Wilshire-Analytics-Study-Finds-Options-Indexes-Offered-Noteworthy-Returns-Over-30-Years-09-21-2016/default.aspx); [Cboe study PDF](https://s202.q4cdn.com/174824971/files/doc_news/2016/02/study-analyzes-performance-of-cboe-spx-options-selling-indexes-pdf1691637391968.pdf)
- PUT outperformed BXM in about 68% of years, but the monthly outperformance eroded meaningfully after 2015 (informal blog analysis) — [R-bloggers](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2020/09/writing-conundrums/)
- Israelov & Nielsen, "Covered Calls Uncovered" (FAJ 2015, AQR): covered call returns split into passive equity, equity market timing and short volatility. Short vol has a Sharpe ratio near 1.0, contributes under 10% of risk, and adds about 2%/yr. The equity-timing component is about 25% of risk and uncompensated. Hedging out the timing exposure improves Sharpe and cuts downside beta. Authors are AQR managers with a commercial interest — [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2015/covered-calls-uncovered); [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Covered-Calls-Uncovered)
- Israelov, Klein & Tummala (Journal of Risk) find the same across 11 global indexes: equity timing returns are uncompensated on average but carry significant risk — [Risk.net](https://risk.net/journal-of-risk/5943741/covering-the-world-global-evidence-on-covered-calls)
- Single-name covered-call ETFs (TSLY on TSLA, NVDY on NVDA, YieldMax): call selling caps upside, and a meaningful share of distributions was return of capital. TSLY's payouts fell from about $13/share to a roughly $3 annualized run rate, and TSLY was down 39.3% YTD on price in Aug 2026. Sources conflict on total return: one July 2026 article says TSLY's 1-yr total return (+34%) beat TSLA (+31.6%). These are low-quality secondary articles, so verify with [totalrealreturns](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/TSLA,TSLY,NVDA,NVDY) — [24/7 Wall St. Aug 2026](https://247wallst.com/investing/etf/2026/08/21/tsly-pays-more-each-week-than-nvdy-but-holders-are-losing-money-faster/); [24/7 Wall St. Jul 2026](https://247wallst.com/investing/etf/2026/07/18/tsly-investors-are-missing-teslas-rally-how-option-income-strategy-costs-you-30-in-upside/)
- Gao, Xing & Zhang, "Anticipating Uncertainty: Straddles around Earnings Announcements" (JFQA 2018, 53(6), 2587-2617): single-stock straddles generally earn negative returns. However, ATM straddles held from 3 days before earnings to the announcement earn a highly significant +3.34%. Returns are larger for small, high-vol, high-kurtosis, illiquid firms. The authors argue investors underestimate earnings uncertainty, and the returns are not compensation for systematic risk — [Cambridge/JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/anticipating-uncertainty-straddles-around-earnings-announcements/7B34877AD5E06304BA3C55FBA3219FDD); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:jfinqa:v:53:y:2018:i:06:p:2587-2617_00)
- A 2026 student project (informal, unreviewed) reports the pre-earnings long straddle on recent S&P 500 data now earns at best 0.06% (entry T-2, exit T+0) with a 43% win rate, i.e. arbitraged away — [student PDF](https://nextjsportfolio-51100421492c.herokuapp.com/projects/NUSA%20Spring%2026%20RS%20-%20Equity%20Vol.%20Project.pdf)
- BSIC student research covers straddling into and outside earnings — [BSIC](https://bsic.it/straddling-outside-and-into-earnings/)
- Iron condors and strangles: tastytrade studies are mostly on SPY at 45 DTE: 25-delta strangles with $5/$10/$20 wings; 30-delta strangles vs condors managed at 50% of credit, 2005-present. The condor's max loss is known at entry, a strangle's is not — [tastylive Market Measures](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/iron-condors-management-and-wing-width-05-09-2016); [tastylive strangle studies](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/our-3-favorite-strangle-studies)
- apexvol single-name numbers: 16-delta 45-DTE strangle with 68% win rate and -24% max drawdown, vs a condor with 70% and -18%. The site says the figures are "illustrative" and not from live fills, so they are not usable as evidence — [apexvol](https://apexvol.com/strategies/strangle/backtest)
- Backtesting short-dated options with simplified daily pricing models can introduce significant error — [TradesViz](https://www.tradesviz.com/blog/options-strategy-backtest/)

### Inferences
- Tail risks specific to TSLA/NVDA:
  - Earnings gaps: NVDA's implied move is about 6-8%, and TSLA regularly moves more than 10%.
  - Event and headline gaps outside earnings, which matter especially for TSLA.
  - Short-put assignment into a drawdown: CSPs on TSLA in 2022 would have lost heavily, since a CSP is roughly long stock with capped upside.
  - Covered calls cap the right tail, which is where most of NVDA's 2023-2025 return came from.
  - Early assignment around dividends is minor for these names.
  - Iron condor wings limit the loss but still give a negative-skew payoff, where one gap can wipe out many months of credit.
- Whether shorting earnings vol on NVDA pays depends on the regime. ORATS documents both overpricing (eight consecutive moves smaller than implied) and periods where the market cut the implied move below the historical average. A rule needs to condition on implied move vs history, not simply always sell.
- For a retail quant, the CSP/covered call on single names is mostly a levered equity view. The evidence-based VRP harvest is index-based, ideally with risk controls (defined-risk spreads, small sizing).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or reproducible backtest of iron condors, CSPs or covered calls specifically on TSLA/NVDA was found. This needs to be run with ORATS or Cboe data, including earnings gaps and realistic fills.
- No reliable numbers were found on how the IV crush on TSLA/NVDA has changed after the addition of Mon/Wed expirations in 2026.

## 3. 0DTE and weekly options trends 2023-2026; evidence of retail losses

### Takeaway
0DTE reached about 59% of SPX volume in 2025 (records of about 62% in single months). Since Feb 2026, TSLA, NVDA and seven other mega-caps have Monday and Wednesday expirations, and single-name 0DTE share jumped from 15% to 33% of their volume by May 2026. NVDA and TSLA are each about 9% of all US single-stock options volume. The academic evidence (Bryzgalova-Pavlova-Sikorskaya; Beckmeyer-Branger-Gayda) is that retail options traders lose in aggregate, mostly through spreads and fees. Cboe says 0DTE does not destabilize markets, but does not publish data on retail P&L.

### Cited Findings
- Full-year 2025: SPX 0DTE average daily volume hit a record 2.3M contracts, 59% of total SPX volume — [MarketsMedia (Cboe report)](https://www.marketsmedia.com/cboe-reports-record-trading-volume-for-2025/)
- Aug 2025: SPX 0DTE averaged about 2.4M/day, a record 62.4% of SPX volume, with retail estimated at 53% of that volume. In Feb 2025 the share was 56%, partly because Robinhood opened index options to all customers in late Jan 2025 — [Cboe Aug 2025](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/spx-0-dte-options-jump-to-record-62-share-in-august); [Cboe Feb 2025](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/spx-0-dte-options-jumped-to-record-56-share-in-feb/)
- OCC: 15.21B US-listed contracts in 2025 (+24.4% vs 2024, a sixth consecutive record year). Single-stock options were 54% of volume, and the single-day record was 110M contracts on Oct 10, 2025 (aggregator citing OCC; verify on OCC) — [edgeX summary](https://pro.edgex.exchange/en-US/news/article/us-options-trading-statistics-2026)
- Cboe Q2 2026: options ADV 72.8M (+19% YoY), with the year on pace for well above 18B contracts. Single-stock options grew only 6% YTD vs index options +25% and ETF options +27%. 0DTE volume across products was +46.2% YTD at more than 20M/day, and SPX 0DTE nearly tripled since early 2024. NVDA and TSLA each took 9% of single-stock options volume (AAPL 3%). Mon/Wed expirations on TSLA, NVDA, AAPL, IBIT, AMZN, META, AVGO, GOOGL and MSFT total close to 6M contracts/day, and more symbols are expected in Q3. Retail accounts under $25k increased activity — [Cboe Q2 2026](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/state-of-the-options-industry-options-market-continued-to-break-records-in-q-2-2026)
- MEMX (June 2026): the 9 single stocks added Mon/Wed expirations in Feb 2026. Their 0DTE share went from 15% of volume (Jan) to 33% (May 2026), and 0DTE volume rose 219% (1.21M to 3.87M) while industry volume rose 1%. On Mon/Wed/Fri, 0DTE is more than 40% of daily volume in these names. SPY/QQQ 0DTE is about 65%. Retail flow in May 2026 concentrated in AI names (NVDA, AMD, INTC, MU, AVGO, TSM) and in leveraged ETPs (TQQQ, SOXL, TSLL) — [MEMX](https://memx.com/insights/retail-active-in-ai-infrastructure-growth-in-zero-day-options-volume)
- Citadel Securities: nearly half of all retail options volume it executes is in 0DTE contracts (timing unclear) — [Citadel Securities Retail Detail](https://www.citadelsecurities.com/news-and-insights/retail-detail/the-return-of-momentum-and-retail-influence/)
- Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya (Journal of Finance 2023, 78(6)): retail investors lost about $1.14B trading options from Nov 2019 to June 2021 (10-day holding assumption). Bid-ask spreads drive losses ("the more they trade, the more they lose"), and retail prefers lottery-like options — [LBS](https://www.london.edu/faculty-and-research/academic-research/r/retail-trading-in-options-and-the-rise-of-the-big-three-wholesalers); [LBS news](https://london.edu/news/amateur-investors-lost-big-during-pandemic-2034)
- Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda (2023), "Retail Traders Love 0DTE Options... But Should They?": about 75% of retail SPX trades were 0DTE. Average daily retail losses were $184k over the full sample and $358k/day after daily expirations began on May 16, 2022. About 60% of losses come from transaction costs. Gross profits were briefly positive (late 2021-mid 2022) but near zero net of fees. The authors attribute the motive to gambling and entertainment — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-04-21/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options); [WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-love-same-day-options); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=44172)
- Neudata review: retail lost about $125M in aggregate, averaging about $350k/day, since weekday expirations were introduced. Secondary source; figures differ from CXO's roughly $70M — [Neudata](https://www.neudata.co/literature-reviews/zero-day-to-expiry-options-a-losing-bet-for-retail-traders)
- Dim, Eraker & Vilkov (2024), "0DTEs: Trading, Gamma Risk and Volatility Propagation": no evidence that higher 0DTE gamma raises index volatility, and it is associated with lower intraday vol. Vilkov's "0DTE Trading Rules" finds a real but fragile edge, largely explained by realized skewness (secondary summaries) — [Hull Tactical](https://hulltactical.com/?p=1919); [Harbourfront Quant](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/the-rise-of-0dte-options-cause-for)
- Cboe estimates retail at about 50-60% of SPX 0DTE. Cboe's derivatives head says 0DTE is not mainly used as a cheap leverage bet, a view the academic work disputes. Cboe publishes no retail P&L figures — [Cboe 0DTEs Decoded](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact)

### Inferences
- Since early 2026, TSLA/NVDA effectively trade three expirations per week. Short-dated gamma flows in these names are now a meaningful part of intraday price action, a regime that did not exist in most historical backtest samples.
- The retail-loss evidence points mainly to costs: spreads plus fees plus buying expensive lottery tickets. For a retail quant, execution (limit orders at mid, liquid strikes, fewer round trips) matters more than signal, especially in 0DTE.
- All the rigorous retail-loss studies are on 2019-2023 data and mostly on SPX. No academic estimate yet covers single-name 0DTE losses after 2026.

### Gaps
- No primary academic study measuring retail P&L in single-stock 0DTE or weekly options (TSLA/NVDA) for 2024-2026 was found.
- The Beckmeyer et al. and Dim-Eraker-Vilkov figures are taken from secondary summaries. The original PDFs were not fetched.
- Loss estimates conflict ($70M vs $125M aggregate) because of different samples and methods.

## 4. Options data sources, backtesting tools, and Greeks/IV computation in Python

### Takeaway
Typical retail-quant stack: ORATS (historical chains with precomputed IV/Greeks, about $99/mo, long history) for backtesting; Polygon (rebranded Massive in late 2025) for raw quotes/trades and flat files at low cost; Cboe DataShop for official, auditable trade-level data at institutional prices. In Python, use optopsy (v2.x) for strategy backtests and py_vollib / py_vollib_vectorized (or newer fast-vollib) for IV and Greeks. Most vendor comparisons found are vendor-written, so confirm pricing on the official pages.

### Cited Findings
- ORATS: positioned for historical backtesting, with about 25 years of history from about $99/month and a 14-day trial. Historical data comes with IV and Greeks attached. One comparison claims some ORATS data starts only in March 2023, which conflicts with the depth claim, so verify per dataset. ORATS also offers an intraday backtester and near-EOD data — [Raxx vendor notes](https://raxx-internal-docs.pages.dev/data-science/historical-options-data-vendors.html); [ORATS intraday backtester](https://orats.com/intraday-backtester); [ORATS near-EOD](https://orats.com/near-eod-data); [ORATS trusted data](https://orats.com/blog/trusted-options-data)
- Polygon.io, rebranded Massive in late 2025: historical quotes and trades for all listed US option contracts, plus flat files. It is raw infrastructure, so you build your own analytics. Options plans are cited at $29 and $79/month, with sources disagreeing — [FlashAlpha comparison (vendor)](https://flashalpha.com/articles/flashalpha-vs-polygon-options-data-raw-vs-computed); [DEV Community 2026 API comparison](https://dev.to/tomasz_dobrowolski_35d32c/best-options-data-apis-2026-7-compared-pricing-free-tiers-1lf5)
- Cboe DataShop: official raw historical data, priced for institutions, with no public pricing. Commercial use needs a direct agreement. Best for auditable trade-level research — [Raxx vendor notes](https://raxx-internal-docs.pages.dev/data-science/historical-options-data-vendors.html)
- optopsy: Python options-strategy backtesting and statistics library, GPL-3.0. v2.x requires Python 3.12-3.13, pandas 2.0+ and NumPy 1.26+. It has strategy modules (e.g. iron condors/butterflies), delta-based filtering, a data CLI for cached historical data, and an optional AI chat UI. It moved from michaelchu/optopsy to goldspanlabs — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/optopsy/); [GitHub](https://github.com/goldspanlabs/optopsy); [docs: iron strategies](https://goldspanlabs.github.io/optopsy/strategies/iron-strategies)
- py_vollib: prices, IV and Greeks under Black, Black-Scholes and Black-Scholes-Merton. py_vollib_vectorized adds numba-accelerated batch computation over pandas/numpy arrays. Some third parties describe py_vollib as abandoned, which is unconfirmed — [py_vollib_vectorized PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/py-vollib-vectorized); [Greeks docs](https://py-vollib-vectorized.readthedocs.io/en/latest/pkg_ref/greeks.html)
- Newer alternatives: fast-vollib (NumPy/PyTorch/JAX/CUDA backends; arXiv 2604.27210), pyvolr and OpenGreeks (Rust-core drop-ins) — [fast-vollib arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27210); [fast-vollib PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/fast-vollib/); [OpenGreeks PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/opengreeks/)
- LSEG developer article on finding expired options and backtesting a short iron condor in Python — [LSEG](https://developers.lseg.com/en/article-catalog/article/finding-expired-options-and-backtesting-a-short-iron-condor-stra)
- Cboe on the need for real-time options data for retail participants — [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/the-necessity-of-real-time-options-data-for-retail-participants)

### Inferences
- TSLA/NVDA options are American-style, so Black-Scholes IV from py_vollib is an approximation. Error is small for calls on non-dividend payers like TSLA, but larger for deep ITM puts. ORATS' vendor IVs (computed with their own smoothing and dividend/rate handling) avoid this. For your own computations, use a binomial/BAW model, e.g. QuantLib, or accept BSM on OTM options only.
- Minimum viable VRP study for TSLA/NVDA:
  - Compare ORATS daily 30-day ATM IV vs subsequent 30-day close-to-close realized vol, measured in variance terms.
  - Compute delta-hedged returns of 30-delta options (Bakshi-Kapadia style).
  - Report earnings and non-earnings windows separately.
  - Model fills at mid plus a fraction of the spread, since the literature shows spreads dominate retail P&L.

### Gaps
- No official current price lists for ORATS, Massive or Cboe DataShop were fetched. Prices above come from third-party and vendor-competitor comparisons.
- Whether optopsy v2 handles intraday/0DTE data or American early exercise was not confirmed.
- py_vollib's maintenance status was not confirmed from an official source.
