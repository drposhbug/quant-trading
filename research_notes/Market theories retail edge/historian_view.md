## Thread 1: How did past technology and infrastructure bubbles (1840s railways, 1920s radio/RCA, Nifty Fifty, dot-com) peak and end, how far did their leaders fall, who survived, and how does AI capex in 2023-2026 compare?

### Takeaway
In every analogue, the technology was real and the investors in the leaders still lost 64-98% peak to trough. Recovery took decades: Cisco needed about 25 years and RCA about 35. Only a few leaders (Amazon, a handful of the Nifty Fifty) compounded afterwards. By most estimates AI capex has already passed the 2000 telecom peak as a share of GDP, but it is still well below the railway peak. It is funded mostly from cash flow, but debt funding is rising quickly.

### Cited Findings

**Round 1. Q: How big were the busts in the leaders?**
- **Railway Mania, 1840s:**
  - Odlyzko's best-known railway share index fell from 167.9 in summer 1845 to 60.5 at end-1849, a 64% drop. — [Odlyzko, "Collective hallucinations"](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/hallucinations.pdf)
  - An index of all 863 railway securities rose 106% from Jan 1843 to a peak on 6 Oct 1845. It fell 20.2% in about a month and 64.1% by April 1850. — [Campbell, VoxEU/CEPR](https://cepr.org/article/railway-mania-not-so-great-expectations)
- **RCA, 1920s:**
  - RCA rose roughly 200-fold in the 1920s and peaked at a split-adjusted 114.75 in Sept 1929. — [The Bubble Blog](https://thebubbleblog.substack.com/p/rcas-radiant-rise-and-radio-ruin)
  - By May 1932 it had fallen to about 2.625, roughly -98%. — [Global Financial Data/Finaeon](https://www.globalfinancialdata.com/rca-and-the-roaring-twenties)
  - Its P/E at the peak is cited as 72, from a secondary source with no stated method. — [Internet News](https://www.internetnews.com/it-management/more-lessons-of-history/)
- **Nifty Fifty:**
  - The average P/E at end-1972 was 41.9, against 18.9 for the S&P 500. — [Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2024/02/21/magnificent-seven-bubble-nifty-fifty)
  - The group fell 40%+ in 1973-74, while the S&P fell about 42-45%. Sources vary. — [A Wealth of Common Sense](https://awealthofcommonsense.com/2020/07/the-nifty-fifty-and-the-old-normal/); [Morningstar AU](https://www.morningstar.com.au/markets/investors-should-know-about-this-market-crash)
- **Dot-com:**
  - The Nasdaq Composite closed at 1114 on 9 Oct 2002, down 78% in 30 months. The Nasdaq-100 also fell 78%. — [247wallst](https://247wallst.com/investing/2025/04/27/the-nasdaq-fell-78-in-2000-it-could-happen-again/); [DQYDJ](https://dqydj.com/nasdaq-drawdown-history/)
  - Amazon fell "as much as 90%". Other figures run from 77% in 2000 alone to 95% on user charts. I found no source for 94%. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2018/12/18/dotcom-bubble-amazon-stock-lost-more-than-90percent-long-term-investors-still-got-rich.html); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/economics/nasdaq-dive-prompts-dot-com-crash)
  - Cisco peaked at about $555B of market cap in March 2000, the world's largest company. It fell more than 85%. — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/cisco-stock-still-isnt-back-to-its-2000-high-chart-of-the-week-123018268.html); [247wallst](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/01/06/ciscos-25-year-journey-back-to-its-dot-com-peak-offers-a-cautionary-tech-lesson/)
- **2022 dress rehearsal:** The S&P fell about 25%, growth about 35%, and the Magnificent 7 almost 50%. Grantham argues ChatGPT's launch in December 2022 cut that bear market short. — [GMO, Jan 2026](https://www.gmo.com/americas/research-library/valuing-ai-extreme-bubble-new-golden-era-or-both/)

**Round 2. Follow-up: Did the leaders survive, and how long did buy-at-the-peak investors wait?**
- **Cisco:** It took more than 25 years to regain its 27 Mar 2000 high of $80.06 (it reached $80.25 in late 2025), despite revenue growing from about $12.5B to about $57B. — [Nasdaq.com](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/25-years-later-cisco-finally-recovers-dot-com-crash); [247wallst](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/01/06/ciscos-25-year-journey-back-to-its-dot-com-peak-offers-a-cautionary-tech-lesson/)
- **RCA:** It regained its 1929 high around the 1960s; one source says 1966. — [Internet News](https://www.internetnews.com/it-management/more-lessons-of-history/); [The Absolute Sound](https://www.theabsolutesound.com/articles/a-brief-history-of-rca-tech-darling-1920-1970/)
- **Japan:** It took about 35 years to recover its 1989 peak. — [GMO, Jan 2026](https://www.gmo.com/americas/research-library/valuing-ai-extreme-bubble-new-golden-era-or-both/)
- **Nifty Fifty, Dec 1972-1998:**
  - The group returned 12.5% a year rebalanced (12.2% unrebalanced), against 12.7% for the S&P. Buying at the peak roughly matched the market over 26 years. Earnings grew 11% a year versus 8% for the S&P. — [Advisor Perspectives (summarising Siegel 1998)](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2024/02/21/magnificent-seven-bubble-nifty-fifty)
  - The 25 highest-P/E names returned about half as much as the 25 lowest. Names above 30x tended to underperform. — same source; [Morningstar AU](https://www.morningstar.com.au/markets/investors-should-know-about-this-market-crash)
  - Polaroid's 94.8x P/E was about 8x what its later returns justified. — [Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2024/02/21/magnificent-seven-bubble-nifty-fifty)
- **Amazon:** It fell 90%+ yet long-term holders "still got rich". This is survivorship: one winner among many dot-com failures. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2018/12/18/dotcom-bubble-amazon-stock-lost-more-than-90percent-long-term-investors-still-got-rich.html)
- **Railways:** Campbell finds railway shares were *not* clearly overpriced at the 1845 peak once dividends, growth and risk are controlled for, and dividends rose and fell with prices. This is a counter-view to Odlyzko's "hallucination" thesis. — [Campbell, VoxEU](https://cepr.org/article/railway-mania-not-so-great-expectations)

**Round 3. Follow-up: What ended each boom, and how does AI capex compare in scale?**
- **Catalysts:**
  - RCA: the Oct 1929 crash, price competition from Zenith and Philco, and the Depression. — [The Bubble Blog](https://thebubbleblog.substack.com/p/rcas-radiant-rise-and-radio-ruin)
  - Telecom: forecasts assumed internet traffic doubling every 100 days. Odlyzko found it doubled every 12 months, an overestimate of about 8x that drove fibre overbuild. — [GMO, Jan 2026](https://www.gmo.com/americas/research-library/valuing-ai-extreme-bubble-new-golden-era-or-both/)
  - The common pattern is that the busts came from overcapacity and demand forecasts proving wrong, with the technology itself proving real.
- **Scale versus GDP:**
  - Railways: British investors committed more than 8% of GDP at the peak (Odlyzko). GMO puts railway investment at about 7% of national income. — [Odlyzko](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/hallucinations.pdf); [GMO](https://www.gmo.com/americas/research-library/valuing-ai-extreme-bubble-new-golden-era-or-both/)
  - US railroads peaked at about 6% of GDP in 1870, or averaged 2.2-2.4% in the 1870s-80s, depending on the measure. — [Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/llm-impact-gdp/); [Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2025/10/09/ai-spending-boom-massive-not-unprecedented)
  - The telecom/fibre peak was about 1.2% of GDP in 2000. — [Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/llm-impact-gdp/); [IEEE ComSoc techblog](https://techblog.comsoc.org/2025/09/27/big-tech-spending-on-ai-data-centers-and-infrastructure-vs-the-fiber-optic-buildout-during-the-dot-com-boom-bust/)
- **AI capex now:**
  - Hyperscaler capex was about 1.3% of US GDP in 2025, projected at 1.6% for 2026. Other estimates for 2026 reach about 2.2%. — [GMO](https://www.gmo.com/americas/research-library/valuing-ai-extreme-bubble-new-golden-era-or-both/); [Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/llm-impact-gdp/)
  - 2026 capex plans for GOOGL, AMZN, MSFT and META total about $725B, up 77% on 2025. — [Tom's Hardware/FT](https://www.tomshardware.com/uk/tech-industry/big-tech/big-techs-ai-spending-plans-reach-725-billion)
  - Moody's projects $785B for 2026 including Oracle and CoreWeave, approaching $1T in 2027. — [DCD](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/moodys-hyperscaler-capex-forecasts-marked-up-by-85bn-to-close-in-on-1trn-by-2027/)
  - Company guidance: MSFT about $190B, GOOGL up to $190B, AMZN about $200B, META $115-135B. — [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/uk/tech-industry/big-tech/big-techs-ai-spending-plans-reach-725-billion); [CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/907046102/hyperscalers-680-billion-ai-capital-expenditure-investment-raises-the-stakes)
- **Revenue gap:**
  - Total AI revenue is estimated at under $50B, against investment of $1T or more. OpenAI had about $12B of revenue and an $8B operating loss in 2025, with projected losses of $17B in 2026 and $35B in 2027. — [GMO](https://www.gmo.com/americas/research-library/valuing-ai-extreme-bubble-new-golden-era-or-both/)
  - Futurum estimates under $35B of revenue for pure-play AI vendors in 2026. — [Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/ai-capex-2026-the-690b-infrastructure-sprint/)
  - On the other side, Google Cloud grew 63% in Q1 2026 and AWS 28%. — [DCD/Moody's](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/moodys-hyperscaler-capex-forecasts-marked-up-by-85bn-to-close-in-on-1trn-by-2027/)
- **Financing shift (late-stage marker):**
  - AI and data-center debt issuance rose from $166B in 2023 to $625B in 2025. Meta went from about +$30B net cash in 2023 to -$7B. Oracle's CDS spread widened from 40bp to 140bp in Sept-Dec 2025 while its stock fell about 40%. CoreWeave fell more than 50% from its peak. — [GMO](https://www.gmo.com/americas/research-library/valuing-ai-extreme-bubble-new-golden-era-or-both/)
  - A Fed-linked researcher warned in Sept 2026 of systemic risks from financing the buildout. — [Reuters via WHBL](https://whbl.com/2026/09/24/financing-of-historic-ai-buildout-raises-systemic-risks-in-us-researcher-says/)
  - Accounting flag: server depreciation lives were lengthened, at Meta from 3 to 5.5 years and at Google and Microsoft from 3 to 6. — [GMO](https://www.gmo.com/americas/research-library/valuing-ai-extreme-bubble-new-golden-era-or-both/)
- **Counterpoint:** AI spending is largely funded from big-tech profits rather than external debt, unlike the railway and telecom booms. But AI hardware lasts 2-5 years, while railways lasted about a century, so the overbuilt assets would leave less long-lived residual value. — [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/09/23/2025/the-ai-spending-boom-doesnt-entirely-deserve-comparisons-to-past-frenzies); [Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/llm-impact-gdp/)

### Inferences
- **Base case for leaders after a bust:** a 60-98% drawdown and 15-35 years to regain the peak in nominal terms. The cases where buying at the peak worked (the Nifty Fifty average, Amazon) depended on holding 20+ years, and on the specific names being the ones that survived.
- **Valuation dispersion matters most:** in the Nifty Fifty, the cheaper half of the "one-decision" stocks did twice as well as the dearest half. For an AI portfolio, that argues for trimming the highest-multiple names first (cited examples: Palantir above 100x sales, Tesla above 300x earnings, per [GMO](https://www.gmo.com/americas/research-library/valuing-ai-extreme-bubble-new-golden-era-or-both/)) rather than exiting the theme wholesale.
- **Where AI sits on scale:** capex at 1.3-2.2% of GDP is past telecom 2000 (1.2%) and below railway peaks (6-8%). The financing mix is shifting from cash flow toward debt (2023-2025), which in Kindleberger terms is the move from "boom" to "credit-fuelled euphoria."
- **Bust mechanism to watch:** the telecom analogue, where demand forecasts outran actual use by about 8x. The relevant comparison is AI revenue (under $50B) against capex (about $700B+ a year).

- I found no primary price tables for RCA's split-adjusted series; the figures above come from GFD and secondary sources.
- Odlyzko's 8% of GDP figure comes from a truncated snippet, and GMO's about 7% is a different measure. Treat both as approximate.
- I did not verify which 1840s railway companies survived (for example, London & North Western). This was not researched.
- I found no source for the size of the Nov 2026 drawdown from the AI-index peak.

## Thread 2: What are the Minsky/Kindleberger stages, which late-stage signs can be measured (capex/revenue, IPO frenzy, margin debt, top-10 concentration), and how well have those signs predicted busts historically?

### Takeaway
On every measurable late-stage gauge, 2026 is at or near records:
- CAPE about 40, second only to 2000's 43.5
- margin debt at a record $1.50T
- IPO proceeds near the 2021 record
- top-10 S&P weight above 2000
- capex rising faster than revenue and increasingly debt-financed

Historically these signals are real but tell you little about timing. Run-ups roughly double the odds of a crash, but they leave 20-50% of cases without one, and value-based forecasters have been years early. GMO's own bubble-top markers were "not yet clearly visible" as of Jan 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Round 1. Q: What is the stage model?**
- **Kindleberger-Minsky sequence:** a displacement (a novel profit opportunity) leads to a credit-fuelled boom, then euphoria, then distress, then revulsion, panic and crash. Credit and asset prices rise much faster than GDP. Then the pace slows and "a few savvy or lucky investors sell" to lock in gains, and distress follows as prices begin to fall. — [Aliber/Kindleberger/McCauley chapter, Springer](https://rd.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-16008-0_2); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/h/spr/sprchp/978-3-031-16008-0_5.html)
- **Productive bubbles:** Janeway, a VC through the dot-com era, argues that several economically beneficial technologies would not have been built without bubbles. — [Quinn & Turner, "The Bubble Triangle", Cambridge](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/69A0FE87E88998B0C2EF57167B4CF235/9781108421256c1_1-15.pdf/the-bubble-triangle.pdf)
- **GMO's statistical definition:**
  - A bubble is a 2-sigma deviation from the real price trend; a "superbubble" is 3-sigma. — [GMO 2022](https://www.gmo.com/americas/research-library/let-the-wild-rumpus-begin_viewpoints/); [GMO Jan 2026](https://www.gmo.com/americas/research-library/valuing-ai-extreme-bubble-new-golden-era-or-both/)
  - In developed equity markets, every 2-sigma bubble in the last 100 years eventually fully deflated back to trend. — same sources
  - Exceptions (genuine paradigm shifts) appear only in resources, such as oil after 1970, and in emerging markets such as India. — same sources
  - 2-sigma events have occurred about every 31-35 years, versus 44 under a normal distribution. Grantham admits the 2-sigma threshold is "completely arbitrary." — same sources

**Round 2. Follow-up: Where are the measurable gauges in 2026?**
- **Valuation:** CAPE has a long-run average of 17.6. It reached 32.6 in 1929, a record 43.5 in early 2000, 38.6 in late 2021, and about 40 in Jan 2026. Market cap to GDP is at an all-time high. — [GMO Jan 2026 (Chancellor)](https://www.gmo.com/americas/research-library/valuing-ai-extreme-bubble-new-golden-era-or-both/)
- **P/E at past peaks:** 21x in Oct 1929 (on peak earnings), 35x in Mar 2000, against 7.5x at the Dec 1974 bottom. — same
- **Margin debt (FINRA):**
  - A record $1,502,072M in June 2026, up 7.9% month on month and 51.5% year on year. — [Advisor Perspectives/dshort](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/06/24/margin-debt-finra)
  - About $1.45T in August 2026, the second-highest reading. One estimate puts it at about 4.5% of GDP, versus the roughly 3.6% peak in 2021. — [247wallst](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/19/investors-borrowed-1-45-trillion-to-buy-stocks-is-the-market-one-correction-away-from-a-margin-call-avalanche/)
  - Robinhood's margin book hit a record $21.6B, up 127% year on year. — [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/robinhood-margin-book-record-21-6-billion/)
- **IPO frenzy:**
  - US IPO proceeds were $128B in H1 2026, up 646% year on year, while deal count fell 35% (EY). The pattern is a few mega-deals. — [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2026/07/11/why-2026-ipo-boom-broaden-beyond-ai-mega-deals/)
  - Proceeds reached $141.2B by mid-July, against the 2021 record of $142.4B. — [Axios/Renaissance](https://axios.com/2026/07/13/us-ipo-market-record-proceeds)
  - Anthropic was reportedly eyeing about $1T. OpenAI is listed pre-IPO at about $852B. — [Renaissance Capital](https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News/121550/Fall-2026-US-IPO-Preview-AI-Giants-Take-Center-Stage)
  - AI took about 60% of US VC in 2025, more than $200B. — [GMO](https://www.gmo.com/americas/research-library/valuing-ai-extreme-bubble-new-golden-era-or-both/)
- **Capex and financing:** see Thread 1, Round 3. Debt issuance rose about 3.8x over 2023-2025, and depreciation lives were lengthened.

**Round 3. Follow-up: How predictive are these signs, and how early?**
- **Greenwood, Shleifer & You, "Bubbles for Fama" (JFE 2019):**
  - The sample is US and international industries. Take an industry's two-year return net of the market. Moving from +50% to +100% raises the chance of a crash (a 40% drawdown within 24 months) from about 20% to 53% (19% to 54% in the published version). At +150%, it rises to about 80%. — [NBER w23191](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23191/w23191.pdf); [Shleifer site](https://shleifer.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/bubbles-fama)
  - Some industries keep rising and never crash. Run-ups alone do not predict lower *average* returns, because peaks are hard to time and holders can still earn good returns. Signals that do sharpen the forecast include volatility, firm age, issuance, and price acceleration. — [NBER w23191](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23191/w23191.pdf)
- **GMO's own record:**
  - In Jan 2000, GMO forecast -1.9% real a year for the S&P over 10 years. The actual was -3.5% a year, so it was right on the decade. — [GMO Jan 2026](https://www.gmo.com/americas/research-library/valuing-ai-extreme-bubble-new-golden-era-or-both/)
  - GMO has called the US a bubble since the December 2021 peak. In Jan 2026 it conceded that, "unlike every bubble before it," this one has not yet shown the usual unwind, and the key top signals (speculative stocks collapsing, quality outperforming, broad gains slowing) are "not yet clearly visible." — [GMO Jan 2026](https://www.gmo.com/americas/research-library/valuing-ai-extreme-bubble-new-golden-era-or-both/)
  - The S&P 500 is at about 7,340 in Nov 2026. — [AP/WOWT](https://www.wowt.com/2026/11/17/another-wipeout-hits-wall-street-nvidia-other-jittery-ai-stocks-swing-lower/?outputType=amp). The index level at GMO's Dec 2021 call was not sourced, so I have not computed the gain since then (see Gaps).
- **Leading indicators of the turn:** in the Kindleberger stage model, the leading indicators are the slowing of price growth and early insider or "savvy" selling, not the level of valuation. — [Springer chapter](https://rd.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-16008-0_2)
  - Grantham's preferred tells, speculative names breaking first and quality outperforming, are qualitative signals of the same kind. In 2026 they are partially present: CoreWeave fell more than 50% and Oracle's CDS widened (GMO, Jan 2026). The Nov 5, 2026 day had AMD -15-17% against the S&P -4.4%. — [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/world/20261106/gathering-ai-storm-clouds-doom-tech-stocks-to-darkest-day-in-a-year)

### Inferences
- **Every checklist item is lit.** CAPE about 40, record margin debt (up 51% y/y in June), a near-record IPO year dominated by mega-cap AI listings, capex growing about 77% y/y against AI revenue under $50B, a shift to debt funding, and lengthened depreciation lives. That is the profile of Kindleberger's euphoria stage moving toward distress.
- **None of this gives a date.** The GSY crash probabilities of 50-80% still leave 20-50% of cases with no crash. Valuation-led bears (GMO from 2021) have been early by years. The best-documented edge is in the *combination* of signals (GSY: run-up plus volatility, issuance and acceleration), which is closer to a risk-sizing tool than a timing tool.
- **What a historian would actually do:** size the AI/tech concentration so that a 60-80% drawdown in the leaders (the historical norm) is survivable without forced selling. Avoid margin; a margin-call cascade is the mechanism that turns distress into panic. Treat further stage-model "distress" markers as triggers to de-risk in steps rather than all at once. Those markers are the leaders failing to make new highs after shocks, credit spreads widening at AI borrowers, and IPOs breaking issue price.
- **Mid-Nov 2026 versus the model:** two sharp AI-led selloffs in two weeks, a politically framed catalyst (midterms and capex scrutiny), and export curbs fit the description of "distress" onset. They are equally consistent with mid-cycle corrections like the 2024 AI drawdowns. I cannot distinguish the two with the data found.

### Gaps
- I could not source how much earlier Greenspan's "irrational exuberance" speech (Dec 1996) came than the March 2000 top, or how much the Nasdaq gained in between. It is the classic "early by years" example, but the search did not confirm the figures.
- I found no study quantifying the lead time between peak margin debt and market peaks. Margin debt is commonly described as coincident or slightly lagging, but that is unsourced here.
- I found no September-October 2026 FINRA margin data.
- I did not verify whether the Anthropic and OpenAI IPOs had priced, or at what levels, as of Nov 20, 2026.

## Thread 3: How concentrated is the market today compared with history (top-10 weight in the S&P 500 versus 1929, the 1970s and 2000), and how did the leaders perform against the rest of the market afterwards?

### Takeaway
The top-10 weight is about 38-43%, a record, against about 27% at the 2000 peak and 18-23% in 1990-2015. Historically, peak concentration did *not* reliably mark a near-term top for the index, according to Goldman. But the largest stocks have usually lagged the rest over the following decade: Arnott's "top dogs" trail by about 3-5% a year, and after 2000 the top 10 lagged for years.

### Cited Findings

**Round 1. Q: How concentrated is the index now?**
- **2025:** the top 10 reached a record 40.7%. They held about 18-23% from 1990 to 2015 and about 27% intra-year in 2000. — [RBC Wealth Management](https://www.rbcwealthmanagement.com/en-us/insights/the-great-narrowing-sp-500-concentration)
- **2026:** about 38% as of July (FactSet, via Chase), roughly 10pp above dot-com leaders. Another reading, also citing RBC, puts it at about 43%. The difference is probably date or method. — [Chase](https://www.chase.com/personal/investments/learning-and-insights/article/sp-500-impact-of-magnificent-seven); [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/equities/index-concentration/267368)
- **Theme concentration:** JPM estimates 30 AI-linked names make up 44% of S&P capitalisation. The US is more than 70% of MSCI World. In Jan 2000, 7 of the top 10 companies were TMT. ### Inferences
- **Concentration is a poor timing signal for the index** (Goldman: rallies usually continued). It is a decent 10-year signal for relative returns, since mega-caps lag by about 3-5% a year (Arnott) and the post-2000 top 10 lagged for years.
- **For an AI/tech-concentrated portfolio,** the historically supported adjustment is a gradual tilt from the top-10 and highest-multiple names toward equal-weight or non-US exposure, not a timed exit. The US is more than 70% of MSCI World, so even a "diversified" global cap-weight index is heavily AI-exposed.
- **Mega-cap quality is a real difference from 2000** (leaders are profitable, and capex is mostly cash-funded). The Nifty Fifty precedent shows that quality does not protect against a 40%+ drawdown when entry multiples are high. It only improves the odds of eventual recovery.
