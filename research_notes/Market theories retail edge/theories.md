# Market Theories and What They Imply for a Small Retail Trader

Context: long-only US tech/AI megacap portfolio, 20% vol target, QQQ 200-day MA filter.
Notes on sourcing. Numbers marked **[verified]** were checked against a source fetched or searched in this session. Items marked **[canonical, not re-fetched]** are standard citations with the paper link given, but this session did not re-read the paper, so the report writer should treat any figure on those lines as unconfirmed. Replication failures are marked **REPLICATION FLAG**.

## Which theory best explains post-publication factor decay (McLean-Pontiff), and what survives?

### Takeaway
Decay is best explained by a mix of three things. Data mining or statistical bias accounts for roughly a quarter to a third of the original in-sample return. Arbitrage after publication, in the sense of Grossman-Stiglitz and adaptive markets, removes about another third. Limits to arbitrage explain the remainder, which is the part that survives and sits mostly in costly-to-trade, noisy, long-horizon, or small/illiquid segments. What survives is a smaller premium (roughly half the paper's number), concentrated in factors that are noisy, slow, or uncomfortable to hold (value, momentum, quality/profitability, low-risk). In large caps, most anomalies are weak. Neither pure EMH (no decay should exist because nothing was there) nor pure behavioral finance (no reason for decay after publication) fits on its own.

### Cited Findings
**McLean and Pontiff (JF 2016), 97 predictors**
- Portfolio returns are about 26% lower out-of-sample (after the sample ends, before publication) and about 58% lower after publication. The additional ~32 points is attributed to publication-informed trading **[verified]**. [CFA Digest summary](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2016/06/does-academic-research-destroy-stock-return-predictability-digest-summary); [EconPapers JF 71(1):5-32](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:71:y:2016:i:1:p:5-32)
- Reported monthly averages are 0.582% in-sample, 0.402% out-of-sample before publication, and 0.264% after publication. These are inconsistent with the 26%/58% headline (they imply about 31%/55%), possibly because of a different basis. Flag this to the writer. [CFA Digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2016/06/does-academic-research-destroy-stock-return-predictability-digest-summary)
- The paper rejects both "predictability vanishes entirely" and "no change after publication". It gives a lower bound on the publication effect of about 32%. [Search summary of JF/Ivey PDF](https://ivey.uwo.ca/media/3775549/pontiff.pdf)
- Short-horizon, low-noise anomalies (e.g., the January small-firm effect) decay fastest. Noisy, long-horizon ones (value/growth) persist for decades. [CFA Digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2016/06/does-academic-research-destroy-stock-return-predictability-digest-summary)

**Replication literature**
- **REPLICATION FLAG.** Hou, Xue and Zhang (RFS 2020) tested 452 anomalies with microcaps controlled (NYSE breakpoints, value weighting). 65% fail |t|>1.96, including 96% of the "trading frictions" category. At a multiple-testing hurdle of t>2.78, 82% fail. Surviving anomalies are much smaller in magnitude than originally reported **[verified]**. [NBER w23394](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23394); [conference PDF](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f102921/f102921.pdf)
- In Hou-Xue-Zhang, microcaps are about 3% of NYSE-Amex-NASDAQ market cap but about 60% of the stock count (citing Fama-French 2008). The authors argue microcap anomalies are "more apparent than real" because of trading costs **[verified]**. [NBER w23394](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23394.pdf)
- **Counterpoint.** Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen (JF 2023, 78(5):2465-2518) use 153 factors, 93 countries, and a Bayesian model with publication-bias and multiple-testing adjustment. They find most factors replicate in-sample, out of sample, and internationally. An earlier version reports a replication rate of about 64% after adjustment. Their position is that there is no replication crisis **[verified]**. [CBS research page](https://research.cbs.dk/en/publications/is-there-a-replication-crisis-in-finance/); [NBER w28432](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28432)
- **Why the two disagree.** HXZ uses value-weighted returns and frequentist t-tests on individual anomalies. JKP uses capped value weighting, clusters factors into themes, and applies Bayesian shrinkage. Both are compatible with the view that "most themes are real but small, and many individual signals are noise." (Inference from the two summaries above.)
- Chen and Zimmermann (open-source dataset, 319 signals hand-checked against the original papers) estimate the publication-bias adjustment at only about 12% (bootstrapped SE 1.8pp). Post-publication deterioration is about 24 bp/month larger than that adjustment, which implies that most post-publication decay is not statistical bias and points to arbitrage **[verified]**. [Fed FEDS 2018-033](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2018033pap.pdf)
- When microcaps are removed in a replication of the Chen-Zimmermann anomalies, average in-sample t falls from 3.82 to 2.75 (-28%) and average post-publication t falls from 1.23 to 0.79 (-36%). Post-publication, the average anomaly is not significant even with microcaps included. This is a master's thesis, so it carries lower authority. [HEC Montréal thesis](https://biblos.hec.ca/biblio/memoires/zhang_yilin_m2022.pdf)
- One factor-decay paper argues the post-publication drop should be larger when the traded firms are large, and finds size-adjusted haircuts of 25-50%, similar in magnitude to post-publication decay. [arXiv 2105.01380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.01380)

### Theory-by-theory digest (core claim / strongest evidence / source of returns / what is falsifiable today)

**1. Efficient Markets Hypothesis (Fama 1970) [canonical, not re-fetched]** — [Fama 1970, JF](https://doi.org/10.2307/2325486)
- **Core claim.** Prices reflect available information. The weak form covers past prices, the semi-strong form covers public information, and the strong form covers private information.
- **Strongest support.** Most active managers and individuals underperform. Barber and Odean studied 66,465 households (1991-96): the most active traders earned 11.4% a year versus 17.9% for the market **[verified]**. [Barber-Odean, Haas](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/returns/returns.html). This is contested: Bonaparte and Cooper find gross returns similar across households and argue selection explains part of the gap. [NBER w16022](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16022.pdf)
- **Where returns come from.** Compensation for bearing systematic risk only. Any "anomaly" is either a missing risk factor or a data-mining artifact, which is the joint-hypothesis problem.
- **Falsifiable today.** Price-based signals (the 200-day MA, momentum) should have zero net-of-cost alpha. Momentum's persistence across many markets is the main weak-form challenge.
- **Retail implication.** Hold the market at low cost. Every deviation, including a megacap tilt and an MA filter, is an active bet that has to justify itself.

**2. Adaptive Markets Hypothesis (Lo 2004)** — [Lo, MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/1721.1/75362/1/Lo_Adaptive%20Markets.pdf)
- **Core claim.** Markets are "usually competitive and adaptive, varying in their degree of efficiency as the environment and investor population change over time" **[verified]**. [Wikipedia summary of AMH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptive_market_hypothesis)
- **Evidence.** Time-varying AR and rolling-window tests show efficiency that varies and clusters over time, in US intraday SPY data, Japan, and the US S&P **[verified]**. [PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8682897); [arXiv 1207.1842](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.1842). Akbas et al. find that cross-sectional predictability is lower in periods of high arbitrage-capital flows **[verified via ETF.com summary]**. [ETF.com](https://www.etf.com/node/94103.md)
- **Where returns come from.** Edges exist temporarily. They are competed away, then reappear when conditions change (crowding, regime shifts).
- **Falsifiable today.** Factor premia should shrink when capital flowing into them rises and recover after outflows or crises. McLean-Pontiff decay plus survival of a residual premium fits AMH well. AMH has been criticized as hard to falsify because almost any pattern can be read as "adaptation." (This is a general critique; no specific source was retrieved.)

**3. Behavioral finance (Shiller, Thaler, Barberis) [canonical, not re-fetched]**
- **Core claim.** Investor psychology (overconfidence, extrapolation, limited attention, disposition effect) causes systematic mispricing that limits to arbitrage allow to persist. [Shiller 1981, AER "Do Stock Prices Move Too Much…"](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1802789); [Barberis-Thaler "A Survey of Behavioral Finance," NBER w9222](https://www.nber.org/papers/w9222)
- **Strongest evidence.** Excess volatility of prices relative to dividends (Shiller). The underreaction literature: "Lazy Prices" earns up to 188 bp/month with no announcement effect, accrues over 18 months, and does not reverse **[verified]**. [NBER w25084](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25084). Momentum and post-earnings drift are also cited.
- **Where returns come from.** From other investors' errors. Returns are highest where attention is scarce and arbitrage is costly.
- **Falsifiable today.** Anomalies should be stronger where sentiment is high, attention is low, or short-selling is constrained. They should decay once attention arrives; Cohen-Malloy-Nguyen find faster incorporation when investors download both the current and the prior 10-K **[verified]**. [NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/nov18/w25084.shtml)
- **Retail implication.** The retail investor is usually the noise trader. The behavioral edge is mostly avoiding one's own biases (e.g., turnover).

**4. Reflexivity (Soros) [no academic source retrieved this session]**
- **Core claim.** Participants' beliefs change fundamentals (e.g., a high stock price leads to cheap capital and then real growth), producing self-reinforcing boom/bust cycles rather than mean reversion to a fixed value.
- **Evidence.** Mostly narrative and case studies. Formal tests are thin; see Gaps. The closest academic analogues are feedback-trading and extrapolation models, and the intermediary-leverage cycle (below).
- **Relevance.** Arguably the most relevant theory for an AI megacap portfolio, where valuation feeds capex funding, which feeds reported AI revenue. Falsifiable version: high-valuation firms raise equity or debt more cheaply and grow capex faster, and reversals follow funding shocks. This is untested in this session.

**5. Risk-premium and factor theories**
- **Fama-French [canonical, not re-fetched].** Size, value, and later profitability and investment factors are compensation for risk. [FF 1993 JFE](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(93)90023-5)
- **Momentum.** Jegadeesh-Titman (1993) is the canonical source [not re-fetched]. [JF 1993](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1993.tb04702.x). Crash risk: in March-May 2009 the loser decile rose about 156-163% (the figure differs by draft) while winners rose about 8%. Crashes come from momentum's conditionally large negative beta in rebounds **[verified]**. [NBER w20439](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20439/w20439.pdf). A blog claim (unverified) says the equal-weighted momentum spread was weak over 2000-2015, mostly because of 2009. [Goodreads blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/9583259-momentum-deterioration-crashes-and-prospects)
- **Low volatility / BAB (Frazzini-Pedersen JFE 2014).** Leverage-constrained investors bid up high-beta assets, so high beta earns low alpha. The pattern appears in US equities, 20 international markets, Treasuries, corporates, and futures **[verified]**. [BAB PDF, CBS](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/60082899/lasse_heje_pedersen_et_al_betting_against_beta_publishersversion.pdf). **REPLICATION FLAG:** Novy-Marx and Velikov argue BAB reflects biases from a non-standard beta estimation procedure. [BABAB PDF](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/BABAB.pdf)
- **Quality (QMJ, Asness-Frazzini-Pedersen, RAS 2019).** Junk stocks earn negative risk-adjusted returns **[verified]**. [SSRN 2312432](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2312432). **REPLICATION FLAG (partial):** a later replication finds QMJ is "largely accounted for" by other factors (profitability and investment). Source per search summary. [search result citing replication](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2312432)
- **Intermediary asset pricing (He-Kelly-Manela JFE 2017).** Shocks to primary dealers' equity capital ratio price assets with a consistently positive and similar risk price across stocks, bonds, sovereigns, options, CDS, commodities, and FX (FX weakest, t=1.66) **[verified]**. [NBER w21920](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21920.pdf). Implication: the marginal investor is a constrained intermediary, so risk premia spike when dealers are capital-poor (2008, March 2020). Someone without leverage or redemption risk can supply liquidity at those moments.
- **Where returns come from (all factor theories).** Bearing risks others dislike (distress, crash, funding-liquidity). Falsifiable: premia should be compensated by bad-times losses. Factor returns should co-move with dealer capital and spike after funding crises.

**6. Limits to arbitrage (Shleifer-Vishny 1997) [canonical, not re-fetched]** — [JF 1997](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1997.tb03807.x)
- **Core claim.** Arbitrage is done by specialized agents managing other people's money. When mispricing widens, investors redeem (performance-based arbitrage), so arbitrageurs are forced out precisely when the opportunity is best. Mispricing therefore survives where it is volatile, long-horizon, or idiosyncratic.
- **Evidence.** HXZ's finding that 96% of trading-friction anomalies fail value-weighted (above) shows that "anomalies" in illiquid names are mostly unharvestable. Akbas et al. show time-varying arbitrage capital drives predictability.
- **Falsifiable today.** Anomaly returns should be larger in high-idiosyncratic-volatility, low-liquidity, and hard-to-short stocks, and after arbitrageur capital losses.

**7. Grossman-Stiglitz paradox (AER 1980) [canonical, not re-fetched]** — [JSTOR 1805228](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1805228)
- **Core claim.** If prices were fully efficient, nobody would pay to collect information, so prices could not become efficient. In equilibrium there is just enough inefficiency to compensate informed traders for their costs.
- **Implication.** Gross alpha exists, but it accrues to whoever has the lowest marginal cost of information and execution. For a retail trader with no data, speed, or scale advantage, expected net alpha from information-gathering is roughly zero or negative. The rational retail edges are structural (costs, taxes, horizon, constraints), not informational.
- **Falsifiable.** Aggregate active management should earn gross alpha approximately equal to its costs. Large-cap, heavily covered names should show the least mispricing.

### Inferences
- The best-fitting synthesis is Grossman-Stiglitz plus limits to arbitrage, with AMH dynamics on top. McLean-Pontiff's roughly 58% post-publication decay with a nonzero residual matches the prediction of "an equilibrium amount of inefficiency that shrinks as arbitrage costs fall."
- What survives appears to be broad, theme-level factors (value, momentum, profitability/quality, low-risk), at about half the published magnitude, and they are noisy enough to need multi-year holding. Individual clever signals in small caps mostly do not survive.
- For a megacap tech portfolio, the most relevant surviving effects are momentum (time-series and cross-sectional, which the QQQ 200-day MA proxies) and quality/profitability. Value is structurally underweighted in this portfolio.

### Gaps
- No formal academic test of Soros's reflexivity was retrieved this session.
- The internal inconsistency in the McLean-Pontiff percentages (26/58 versus monthly averages implying 31/55) could not be resolved without the full JF text.
- No source was found that isolates the post-publication decay of anomalies within large or megacap stocks specifically (e.g., Chen-Zimmermann restricted to the top 500).

## What do intermediary and limits-to-arbitrage theories say about edges institutions cannot take?

### Takeaway
The theory predicts persistent premia for exactly the things that constrained, delegated, leveraged, benchmarked capital avoids. These are: holding volatile, high-tracking-error positions through long drawdowns (career and redemption risk); providing liquidity when dealers are capital-constrained; tiny, capacity-limited trades; and tax management. A small unlevered account with no clients is free of most of these constraints. The trade-off is that capacity-limited edges are mostly in microcaps, where costs and HXZ-style failures dominate.

### Cited Findings
- **Redemptions and career risk.** In Shleifer-Vishny's performance-based arbitrage, losses trigger outflows that force liquidation at the worst time **[canonical, not re-fetched]**. [JF 1997](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1997.tb03807.x)
- **Funding and leverage constraints.** Constrained investors bid up high-beta assets, so high beta earns low alpha. An unconstrained investor can hold low-beta stocks and lever them **[verified]**. [Frazzini-Pedersen BAB](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/60082899/lasse_heje_pedersen_et_al_betting_against_beta_publishersversion.pdf). Contested by [Novy-Marx-Velikov](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/BABAB.pdf).
- **Intermediary capital.** The risk price of dealer-capital shocks is positive across asset classes **[verified]**, so expected returns are highest after dealers lose capital. [He-Kelly-Manela NBER w21920](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21920.pdf)
- **Arbitrage capital is time-varying, and so is predictability** **[verified, secondary]**. [Akbas et al. via ETF.com](https://www.etf.com/node/94103.md)
- **Capacity.** Anomalies are concentrated in microcaps (about 60% of names, about 3% of cap). Value-weighting kills 65% of anomalies, and 96% of the trading-frictions category **[verified]**. [HXZ](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23394.pdf)
- **Horizon.** Noisy, long-horizon anomalies (value) persist for decades, while short-horizon, low-noise ones are arbitraged quickly **[verified]**. [CFA Digest on McLean-Pontiff](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2016/06/does-academic-research-destroy-stock-return-predictability-digest-summary)
- **Tax.** Institutions such as pensions and endowments are mostly tax-exempt, so tax alpha is an edge only for taxable individuals. Tax-loss harvesting is worth about 0.85-1.10%/yr in backtests (see Q4) **[verified]**. [Chaudhuri-Burnham-Lo, FAJ 2020](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2020/empirical-evaluation-tax-loss-harvesting-alpha)
- **Skewness and benchmark constraints.** Only about 4% of US stocks (1,092 of about 26,000, 1926-2016) account for all net wealth creation over T-bills. About 58% of stocks fail to beat T-bills over their lifetimes **[verified]**. [Bessembinder JFE 2018, ASU](https://wpcarey.asu.edu/department-finance/faculty-research/do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills). Concentrated portfolios usually underperform the index but occasionally capture the winners. That asymmetry is an argument against concentration for anyone without an edge in picking winners. It is not an institutional constraint the retail trader can exploit.

### Inferences
- The main structural edges available to this user are (a) tolerance for tracking error and drawdowns without redemption pressure, (b) taxable-account tax management, (c) no forced deleveraging. Note that a 200-day MA filter partly gives up edge (a): it de-risks during drawdowns, as an institution would. Its justification has to come from time-series momentum or crash protection, not from limits to arbitrage.
- The vol target and MA filter make the user behave like a constrained institution in stress periods (selling when volatility is high and dealers are capital-poor). Intermediary pricing implies that is when expected returns are highest. The rule should be evaluated on whether its crash-avoidance benefit exceeds the forgone post-crisis risk premium. This is a falsifiable check: the paper-trade record and backtest should show what the filter gave up in rebounds such as April-May 2009 and April 2020.
- The megacap tech focus sits in the most-arbitraged, most-covered segment. Grossman-Stiglitz and HXZ imply that little stock-selection alpha should be expected there. The return source is mostly market/sector beta plus momentum.

### Gaps
- No direct empirical estimate was found of the "career risk premium" or of benchmark-constraint effects (e.g., Baker-Bradley-Wurgler on benchmarks and low-vol). This was not searched because of the tool-call limit.
- No source was found quantifying mandate-driven or index-inclusion effects for megacaps.

## Evidence on lazy prices, underreaction to slow-moving information, long-horizon anomalies, and small/illiquid concentration versus megacap tech

### Takeaway
Underreaction to slow, complex, unglamorous information is one of the better-documented behavioral effects, with large published alphas. Most such effects, though, are strongest in small, low-attention, costly-to-trade stocks, and replications value-weighted with microcaps controlled cut them sharply. In megacap tech (heavy coverage, NLP-scraped filings, huge liquidity), expect much less, and assume decay since publication.

### Cited Findings
- **Lazy Prices (Cohen-Malloy-Nguyen, JF 2020).** Shorting firms that change their 10-K language and buying non-changers earns up to 188 bp/month in alpha (over 22%/yr). The risk-factors section is most informative, and changes involving the CEO/CFO, litigation, and negative sentiment also matter. There is no announcement-day reaction. Returns accrue for up to 18 months without reversal. The changes predict earnings, profitability, news, and bankruptcies **[verified]**. [NBER w25084](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25084); [NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/nov18/w25084.shtml)
- When investors download both the current and prior 10-K, short-run effects are stronger and subsequent drift is smaller. That is direct evidence that attention closes the gap **[verified]**. [NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/nov18/w25084.shtml)
- **Caveat.** "Up to 188 bp" is the best-case specification (risk-factor section). The paper was published in 2020 and the effect is now widely implemented by NLP vendors, so McLean-Pontiff-style decay (about 58%) is a reasonable prior. This is an inference; no post-publication study of Lazy Prices was retrieved.
- **Small/illiquid concentration.** Microcaps hold about 60% of names and 3% of cap. The microcap mitigation in HXZ makes 65% of 452 anomalies insignificant, including 96% of liquidity/friction ones **[verified]**. [HXZ](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23394.pdf). Removing microcaps cuts Chen-Zimmermann anomaly t-stats by about 28% in-sample and 36% post-publication (thesis). [HEC thesis](https://biblos.hec.ca/biblio/memoires/zhang_yilin_m2022.pdf)
- **Long-horizon anomalies.** Noisy, long-horizon anomalies persist longest after publication **[verified]**. [CFA Digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2016/06/does-academic-research-destroy-stock-return-predictability-digest-summary)
- **Momentum in megacaps.** Momentum carries crash risk in sharp rebounds (2009 losers +156-163% versus winners +8%) **[verified]**. [Daniel-Moskowitz](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20439/w20439.pdf)
- **Skewness.** Wealth creation is concentrated in about 4% of stocks **[verified]**. Recent megacap tech winners are, by definition, drawn from that tail. [Bessembinder](https://wpcarey.asu.edu/department-finance/faculty-research/do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills)

### Inferences
- Lazy-prices-type text-change signals are the only item in this list that could plausibly apply to megacaps (they all file 10-Ks). The predicted effect size there is small, and a test would need value-weighted, post-2020 out-of-sample data. This is a falsifiable, cheap test the user could run with EDGAR data.
- Most "small and illiquid" anomalies are irrelevant or unharvestable for this portfolio. A small account can trade microcaps that institutions cannot, but HXZ suggests costs eat most of the gross edge. That is a capacity edge, not a free lunch.

### Gaps
- No post-2020 out-of-sample study of Lazy Prices was found.
- No direct study of post-earnings-announcement drift (PEAD) decay in large caps was retrieved; it was not searched.
- No study measured anomaly returns specifically within megacap tech.

## Are there documented edges specific to individuals?

### Takeaway
Yes, but they are mostly about costs, taxes, and behavior, not alpha. Tax-loss harvesting has the strongest evidence (about 0.85-1.10%/yr in backtests, investor-dependent). Avoiding overtrading is supported. The rebalancing premium is real in arithmetic, but it depends on volatility and correlation and is small for correlated assets like megacap tech. Volatility-managed portfolios fail out of sample for real-time investors (**REPLICATION FLAG**), which bears directly on the 20% vol target.

### Cited Findings
- **Tax-loss harvesting.** Chaudhuri, Burnham and Lo (FAJ 2020), using the CRSP top 500 from 1926 to 2018, find tax alpha of about 1.10%/yr, or about 0.85%/yr with wash-sale rules. They report it is substantial for retail investors after costs and that it increases with ongoing contributions **[verified]**. [CFA Institute FAJ](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2020/empirical-evaluation-tax-loss-harvesting-alpha); [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/135992). AQR (Sosner et al. 2022) finds the benefit depends heavily on the investor, especially on having gains elsewhere to offset **[verified, secondary]**. [AQR PDF](https://www.aqr.com:443/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/AQR-The-Tax-Benefits-of-Direct-Indexing.pdf?sc_lang=en). Benefits decay as the portfolio's cost basis falls without new contributions (implied by the contribution finding).
- **Not overtrading.** The highest-turnover households (over 250%/yr) earned 11.4% versus 17.9% for the market (1991-96) **[verified]**. [Barber-Odean](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/returns/returns.html). Contested: Bonaparte-Cooper find similar gross returns and argue selection plays a role. [NBER w16022](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16022.pdf)
- **Rebalancing premium.** For two assets it is about σ²(1-ρ)/4: larger with higher volatility, smaller with higher correlation **[verified]**. [SOA, Glacy 2020](https://www.soa.org/4a4c8c/globalassets/assets/library/newsletters/risks-and-rewards/2020/september/rr-2020-09-glacy.pdf). Willenbrock (FAJ 2011) shows the source is the rebalancing itself (selling relative winners). Variance reduction is necessary but not sufficient **[verified]**. [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ufajxx/v67y2011i4p42-49.html). Note that rebalancing is contrarian, while the megacap portfolio's return source is momentum, so the two pull against each other.
- **Volatility-managed portfolios. REPLICATION FLAG.** Moreira-Muir (JF 2017) report positive spanning alphas [canonical, not re-fetched: [doi](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12513)]. Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang and Yan (JFE 2020) test 103 equity portfolios. Volatility-managed versions beat the originals in only 53 cases versus 50. Out-of-sample, implementable versions "generally earn lower certainty equivalent returns and Sharpe ratios," because the spanning regressions are structurally unstable **[verified]**. [EconPapers JFE 138(1)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:138:y:2020:i:1:p:95-117); [Lehigh PDF](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf). A partial rescue: DeMiguel, Martín-Utrera and Uppal (JF 2024) find a conditional multifactor vol-managed portfolio that beats its unconditional version out of sample and net of costs **[verified]**. [LBS PDF](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/3716/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202024%20-%20DeMIGUEL%20-%20A%20Multifactor%20Perspective%20on%20Volatility%E2%80%90Managed%20Portfolios.pdf)
- **Holding through drawdowns.** Indirect evidence comes from the limits-to-arbitrage and intermediary results above: premia are highest after capital-constrained sellers exit **[verified]**. [He-Kelly-Manela](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21920.pdf). Also, the noisy, long-horizon factors are the ones that survive publication. [CFA Digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2016/06/does-academic-research-destroy-stock-return-predictability-digest-summary)

### Inferences
- **The 20% vol target.** The evidence that vol-scaling raises Sharpe for a single equity portfolio, net of costs and out of sample, is weak (Cederburg et al.). Its defensible justification is risk control (a capped drawdown and a predictable risk budget), not alpha. Claims that it "adds return" should be treated as unproven. The user can test this: compare the frozen model with and without vol scaling, net of costs, on post-freeze data only.
- **The QQQ 200-day MA filter** is a time-series momentum bet. It is consistent with AMH and behavioral underreaction, but under limits-to-arbitrage and intermediary theory it gives up the post-crisis premium. Its value hinges on the asymmetry between crashes avoided and V-shaped rebounds missed (2009, 2020-type rebounds are the failure mode, as with Daniel-Moskowitz momentum crashes).
- The highest-confidence individual edges for this account are tax-loss harvesting in taxable accounts (lot-level, wash-sale aware; note that concentrated megacaps give fewer harvesting opportunities than a 500-stock direct index), low turnover, and staying invested without forced selling.

### Gaps
- No net-of-cost study was retrieved of vol targeting applied specifically to a concentrated tech basket or to QQQ.
- No direct academic quantification of a "holding through drawdowns" premium for individuals was found. The evidence is indirect (fund-flow and behavior-gap studies were not searched).
- No specific magnitude estimates for the rebalancing premium in equity-only portfolios were retrieved.
