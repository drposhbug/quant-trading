# Investor Psychology and the Price Effects It Causes: What Persists for a Retail Trader

Scope: panic, herding, disposition effect, over/underreaction, attention, lottery preference, and the flow and price effects they produce. The focus is post-2015 survival, net of costs, and the split between small caps and megacaps. Searched 2026-11-20 (15 searches). Items marked "[background, unverified]" come from prior knowledge and were not confirmed by a source this session. Treat them as leads, not facts.

## 1. Measured price effects of psychological biases (attention/Robinhood, MAX, disposition/overhang, 52-week high, PEAD, sentiment)

### Takeaway
These biases produce real mispricing, but most of the measured edge sits in small, illiquid, hard-to-short, high-attention stocks. For large-cap tech after 2015, the robust use is mostly defensive: do not be the attention or lottery buyer. The long-short "anomaly" versions have mostly decayed or are disputed in large caps.

### Cited Findings
**Attention and Robinhood herding**
- Barber, Huang, Odean and Schwarz (JF, Dec 2022, vol 77 no 6): Robinhood users do more attention-induced trading than other retail investors. App outages disproportionately reduce trading in high-attention stocks. Intense Robinhood buying forecasts negative returns: average 20-day abnormal return of **-4.7%** for the top stocks bought each day. — [WashU profile / JF abstract](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/attention-induced-trading-and-returns-evidence-from-robinhood-use/); [working paper](https://iimb.ac.in/sites/default/files/inline-files/attention-induced-trading.pdf)
- A UC Davis summary of the same research describes about a 5% fall over the next month for top Robinhood buys and about 9% reversals for the most extreme herding events. It also reports that the Robinhood community loses about 5% relative to the market per herding episode. These figures are not directly comparable with the JF abstract's 20-day number. — [UC Davis](https://www.ucdavis.edu/curiosity/blog/what-effect-brokerage-robinhood-having-stock-market)
- Lagged absolute returns and lagged abnormal volume forecast herding, which means herding is predictable ex ante. It is partly driven by what the app displays, such as top-mover lists. — [UC Davis](https://www.ucdavis.edu/curiosity/blog/what-effect-brokerage-robinhood-having-stock-market); [working paper](https://iimb.ac.in/sites/default/files/inline-files/attention-induced-trading.pdf)

**Lottery preference / MAX effect**
- Bali, Cakici and Whitelaw (JFE 2011): the maximum daily return over the past month (MAX) is negatively related to future returns. The return spread between the low-MAX and high-MAX deciles is **more than 1% per month**. — [EconPapers JFE 99(2)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:99:y:2011:i:2:p:427-446)
- A seven-country study (Management Review Quarterly 2020) finds the MAX effect is **not stable over time**. On the same data as Bali et al., it is mainly concentrated before the 1990s. In US data it is largely a proxy for idiosyncratic volatility. It is not significant in Germany, France or Japan. — [Springer MRQ](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11301-019-00176-3)
- Gorman et al. (Critical Finance Review 2022) argue that overreaction within high-MAX returns, not lottery preference, drives the effect. — [CFR](https://nowpublishers.com/article/Details/CFR-0123); [PDF](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/gorman2021could.pdf)
- A working paper finds the MAX effect is **largely reversed when MAX comes from an earnings announcement**. A jump driven by news is not a lottery signal. — [EFMA 2017 PhD paper](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2017-Athens/phd/harvey.pdf)
- Retail options as a lottery channel: Bryzgalova, Pavlova and Sikorskaya (JF 2023, 78(6)) find that retail investors prefer cheap, lottery-like weekly options, strongly favour calls over puts, and lose money on average. Half of retail trades are in options expiring within a week. Average bid-ask spread is **12.6%**. — [JF 2023 PDF](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20BRYZGALOVA%20-%20Retail%20Trading%20in%20Options%20and%20the%20Rise%20of%20the%20Big%20Three%20Wholesalers.pdf)
- Reported loss estimates from secondary coverage: **$1.14bn** of retail options losses from Nov 2019 to Jun 2021, assuming a 10-day holding period. — [Bloomberg via BQ](https://www.bloombergquint.com/amp/markets/mom-and-pop-took-a-billion-dollar-bath-trading-pandemic-options)
- Also secondary: after daily expirations were introduced (May 2022), daily retail losses rose from about $184k to $358k, and about 60% of losses came from transaction costs (Swedroe summary). — [via WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-in-love-with-same-day-options)

**Disposition effect / capital-gains overhang**
- Grinblatt and Han: the disposition effect pushes prices away from fundamentals, and the gap closes later. Stocks with large aggregate unrealised gains have higher expected returns. Once the capital-gains overhang variable is controlled, **momentum disappears**. — [NBER w8734](https://nber.org/papers/w8734)
- Challenges: once a V-shaped selling propensity is controlled, the effect of overhang disappears. The disposition effect may also fail for stocks with extreme unrealised losses. A tax-based account (rational lock-in of gains) explains long-term reversals better than overreaction. — [Tsinghua PDF](https://www.pbcsf.tsinghua.edu.cn/__local/7/F2/46/2791B196CF976B77FC540000D33_75631A61_77521.pdf?e=.pdf); [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/7BD660E6722E45205E04D66CC1DF8519)
- Retail-level evidence: selling propensity rises with daily returns for positions at a gain and falls for positions at a loss. — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/7BD660E6722E45205E04D66CC1DF8519)

**Anchoring at the 52-week high**
- George and Hwang (JF 2004): nearness to the 52-week high explains much of momentum profits and predicts returns better than past returns do. The original US spread is about **0.45% per month**. — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/the-secret-to-momentum-is-the-52-week-high/); [gh5 paper](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/tgeorge/papers/gh5-paper.pdf)
- The effect depends on sentiment: it is strongly positive in optimistic periods and negative in pessimistic periods. Huddart, Lang and Yetman dispute whether it explains momentum. One review calls the 52-week-high strategy volatile, exposed to crashes and not statistically significant; it is unclear from the snippet whether that is the review's own view. — [Massey review](https://mro.massey.ac.nz/bitstreams/99e67c25-4ed2-4314-bd0b-062930e5742b/download); [AUT paper](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/576995/Haoxu-Wang-paper_NZFM.pdf)

**Post-earnings-announcement drift (underreaction)**
- Martineau (Critical Finance Review 2022, "Rest in Peace PEAD"): **for large stocks, PEAD has been non-existent since 2006**. It has only recently disappeared in microcaps. Prices now absorb earnings news on the announcement day. — [CFR PDF](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/martineau2021rest.pdf); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/now/jnlcfr/104.00000122.html)
- This is contested. Two 2025 papers argue PEAD is alive, and Subrahmanyam attributes the disagreement mainly to research-design choices. A 2025 S&P 500 dissertation finds only limited, fragile short-horizon predictability after announcements. — [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again); [Columbia PDF](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/CEASA/Events%20Page/PEAD_Declined_over_time.pdf)

**Extreme sentiment (Baker-Wurgler)**
- Baker and Wurgler (2006): after low sentiment, subsequent returns are higher for young, volatile, unprofitable and non-dividend stocks. These stocks are the most sensitive to speculative demand and also the costliest to arbitrage. — [NBER w13189](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w13189/w13189.pdf); [NYU archive](https://archive.nyu.edu/jspui/bitstream/2451/31359/2/wurgler_baker_investor_sentiment.pdf)
- Out-of-sample: a 2026 AEA paper finds the original BW index, the Huang et al. (2015) rebuild and the Jiang et al. (2019) manager-sentiment index **generally fail to predict returns**. Timing-adjusted and media/text-based measures do better, mainly at longer horizons. — [AEA 2026](https://benny.aeaweb.org/conference/2026/program/paper/6Z8QBKFb)
- Known index flaws: it peaked in early 2001 and mis-timed the end of some episodes. A sentiment factor built from lottery-like stocks predicts low future returns for speculative stocks and for the market. — [Lutz](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/files/45101616/chandler_lutz_the_asymmetric_effects_of_investor_sentiment_postprint.pdf); [Sussex meta-analysis](https://www.sussex.ac.uk/research/centres/qfin/documents/meta-analysis-of-sentiment-indices-in-asset-pricing-diss-final.pdf)

**Post-publication decay in general**
- McLean and Pontiff (JF 2016), 97 predictors: long-short returns are **26% lower out of sample** (upper bound on data-mining) and **58% lower after publication**, implying about **32%** decay from publication-informed trading. The decline is larger for predictors with high in-sample returns. Returns remain higher where stocks are idiosyncratically risky and illiquid. — [McLean-Pontiff PDF](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)
- Jacobs and Müller (241 anomalies, 39 markets): the **US is the only country with a reliable post-publication decline**, with a 62% (EW) / 66% (VW) decline. — [Jacobs-Müller](https://www.wiwi.hu-berlin.de/en/Professorships/bwl/finance/seminars/papers-fin-acc-res-seminars/17s/jacobs.pdf)

### Inferences
- The surviving pattern is "retail-attention buying gets punished, especially in small and hard-to-short names." For a retail trader the actionable version is avoidance: do not buy top-mover or most-bought lists, do not chase one-day spikes without news, and do not hold weekly OTM calls. Shorting these names is costly (borrow fees, squeeze risk), which is why the premium persists.
- In megacap tech, PEAD and MAX-style long-short edges are probably too small after costs. Martineau's large-cap result is the clearest evidence. Contested PEAD papers mostly gain power from smaller names.
- A disposition-driven "gains overhang" effectively adds another flavour of momentum. In large caps it gives little that is separate from standard momentum, and momentum has its own crash risk.
- Sentiment indices behave like regime indicators for speculative small and growth stocks. They are weak as timing signals for SPX or QQQ.

### Gaps
- No source found that directly measures a post-2011 decay of the MAX effect split by size.
- No post-2015 large-cap-only out-of-sample result for 52-week-high or Grinblatt-Han overhang.
- Whether Barber et al.'s -4.7% holds after 2020 (post-meme, with 24h trading and new app UIs) was not found.
- [background, unverified] Kumar (2009) and Boyer-Mitton-Vorkink on lottery and skewness preference among retail investors were not checked this session.

## 2. Behaviour gap, panic selling, fund-flow price pressure, tax-loss selling and January effect

### Takeaway
The investor behaviour gap is real but modest in Morningstar's dollar-weighted measure (about 1.2pp a year), and it is concentrated in volatile funds. Flow-driven price pressure (fire sales) is a documented, partly predictable effect, but the evidence comes from older samples. The January and tax-loss effect has largely faded since 2000.

### Cited Findings
- Morningstar Mind the Gap (10 years to 31 Dec 2025, 2026 edition): investors earned about **8.7% a year vs 9.9% for funds**, a gap of about **1.2pp a year**. The gap is **-0.4% for the least volatile funds vs -2.1% for the most volatile**. Fees matter much less than volatility. ETFs show a *wider* gap than open-end funds. The gap is in line with the 10-year windows ending 2021 to 2024. — [Morningstar 2026 PDF](https://www.morningstar.com/content/cs-assets/v3/assets/blt9415ea4cc4157833/blt852a4301adc2f5db/6a7373d51f7b5ae2da78e1e8/US_Mind_the_Gap_2026.pdf); [Mind the Gap 2025 page](https://www.morningstar.com/business/insights/research/mind-the-gap-2025); [Advisor Perspectives 2026](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/commentaries/2026/08/21/what-advisors-can-learn-from-the-investor-return-gap?firm=vettafi)
- Morningstar computes investor returns as an IRR, so periods with more money invested weigh more. — [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/business/insights/research/mind-the-gap-2025)
- Coval and Stafford (JFE 2007, 1980–2003 data): concentrated fund sales forced by outflows put significant price pressure on stocks, and prices later revert. The fire sales are partly predictable from poor past fund performance. A front-running strategy using public information earned roughly **10% a year** abnormal (per Hong et al.'s description). — [NBER w11357](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11357); [Hong et al.](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-08/H-Hong-et-al.pdf)
- Critique: Wardlaw (2020) questions the Coval-Stafford flow-pressure measure (cited in the BoE paper). — [BoE WP 2024](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/working-paper/2024/whose-asset-sales-matter.pdf)
- Flow-induced selling by quantitative funds causes transitory price falls more than **8x** as large as similar outflows from non-quant funds. — [AEA 2022](https://aeaweb.org/conference/2022/preliminary/paper/QF8Azati)
- January effect: Haug and Hirschey (FAJ 2006) found the small-cap January effect consistent through 2004 and unaffected by the 1986 tax reform. They therefore argued it is not purely tax-driven, pointing also to window dressing. — [CFA FAJ](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/en/research/financial-analysts-journal/2006/the-january-effect)
- NC State (2024): January returns topped 2.5% for both the S&P 500 and small-cap index funds in the 1980s–90s, but the effect was **small or non-existent in 2000–2023**. The Nasdaq article credits the spread of tax-advantaged accounts. — [NCSU](https://mba.ncsu.edu/2024/12/17/does-the-stock-market-feel-the-holiday-spirit); [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/january-effect-myth-or-market-opportunity)

### Inferences
- The gap of about 1.2pp a year, with about 2pp in volatile funds, is the cleanest "avoid" number: rules-based, low-turnover holding removes most of it. ETF ease of trading seems to widen it.
- Fire-sale reversals are a liquidity-provision trade. In megacaps, outflows are too small relative to depth to move prices much. Any residual effect should sit in mid and small caps held by concentrated funds. A retail trader lacks real-time 13F and flow data, so the edge is mostly theoretical.
- Tax-loss selling likely still depresses December small-cap losers somewhat, but the January bounce is not reliable after 2000. It is not worth trading in large-cap tech.

### Gaps
- Lou (2012, RFS) flow-induced trading: no source retrieved. [background, unverified] It reports that flow-driven buying predicts short-term returns and later reversal and can partly explain momentum.
- No post-2015 replication of Coval-Stafford found.
- Dalbar QAIB critiques: not retrieved. [background, unverified] Critics (e.g. Kitces, Pfau) argue Dalbar's method overstates the gap compared with Morningstar's IRR approach. Morningstar's roughly 1.2pp is the more defensible figure.
- No direct quantitative study retrieved on panic selling in crashes (e.g. March 2020 retail vs institutional flows).

## 3. Calendar and flow effects still alive after publication (turn of month, pre-holiday, index rebalancing, options expiry, pension rebalancing, 0DTE, retail call buying)

### Takeaway
The index-inclusion effect has almost disappeared. Turn-of-month evidence is disputed. The best recent evidence of a live, mechanical flow effect is pension/balanced-fund rebalancing (about 17bp next-day), which large players can front-run. 0DTE "gamma squeeze" stories are weakly supported at the index level. Retail options flow is costly to its participants.

### Cited Findings
- Greenwood and Sammon (JF 2025, "The Disappearing Index Effect"): S&P 500 addition abnormal returns fell from about **7.4–7.6% in the 1990s to under 1% (about 0.8%) in the past decade**. The deletion effect was about **-0.6% in 2010–2020**. Causes include MidCap migrations (forced buying offset by forced selling), smaller relative size of additions, and greater liquidity. There are similar declines for Russell and Nasdaq-100. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4294297); [HKS](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/centers/mrcbg/programs/growthpolicy/disappearing-index-effect); [NBER w30748](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w30748/w30748.pdf)
- Harvey, Mazzoleni and Melone ("The Unintended Consequences of Rebalancing", NBER w33554, 2025): mechanical rebalancing by pensions and balanced funds produces predictable market-wide price patterns. When equities are overweight, the resulting selling lowers equity returns by **about 17bp over the next day**. They use two signals, Threshold and Calendar (month-end), and argue that predictable orders are front-run. — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33554); [Duke Fuqua](https://www.fuqua.duke.edu/duke-fuqua-insights/what-do-pensions-lose-from-rebalancing); [CFA blog](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/index%2ephp/blogs/enterprising-investor/2025/rebalancings-hidden-cost-how-predictable-trades-cost-pension-funds-billions)
- Graziani (2024 WP, 1975–2019): there is an end-of-month time-series reversal in the S&P 500 linked to pension payments. — [CSEF PDF](https://csef.it/wp-content/uploads/JMP_GG_02Nov2024.pdf)
- Turn of month: Potomac's commercial backtest (1980 to Q3 2024) finds that holding the S&P 500 only 4 days a month earned only 0.89% less than all other days combined. This is hypothetical and comes from a party with a commercial interest. — [ETF Trends](https://www.etftrends.com/etf-strategist-channel/turn-month-effect/)
- Contradicting evidence: Patel (J. Wealth Management, Sep 2024, S&P 500 TRI 1988–2023) finds **no consistent day, date or month effect across decades**, concluding that calendar anomalies do not exist in US or world indices. — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/calendar-anomalies/); [IBKR](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-quant-news/calendar-anomalies-much-ado-about-nothing/)
- 0DTE: Cboe (2023 and 2025, industry-authored) finds market-maker net gamma in SPX 0DTE is "de minimis": **$170mm to $670mm** intraday, and at most **about 0.2% of SPX daily liquidity**. Customer flow is balanced, and gap moves have not become more frequent. — [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options/); [Cboe 0DTEs decoded](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact)
- A Princeton thesis (2026) finds 0DTE activity is related to intraday volatility, but the evidence does not support gamma-hedging feedback as the main channel. Several studies find dealer gamma is net positive and *dampens* intraday volatility. An NYU WP (2026) says 0DTE growth reinforces short-horizon, flow-driven dynamics. — [Princeton](https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/61115cd1-cb09-4a8b-9230-ff488fc2a386); [NYU](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/2026-06/Glucksman_SiddheshDalvi_HenryLaFond.pdf); [Numerix WP](https://www.numerix.com/sites/default/files/file/2026-01/Numerix_White_Paper_Gamma_Hedging_of_ODTE_Options.pdf)
- Retail call buying: retail inflows coincide with more calls left unexercised before dividend dates. This lets arbitrageurs earn near-riskless "dividend play" profits, a direct transfer from retail call holders. — [Bryzgalova et al. JF 2023](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20BRYZGALOVA%20-%20Retail%20Trading%20in%20Options%20and%20the%20Rise%20of%20the%20Big%20Three%20Wholesalers.pdf)
- Indian options data (AEA 2026): traders lose significantly, and 0DTE shows the largest losses. — [AEA 2026](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2026/program/paper/StD8aadb)

### Inferences
- Index reconstitution is no longer a retail edge. About 0.8% average, mostly realised before the effective date and arbitraged by event-driven funds, leaves nothing after costs and adverse selection.
- Rebalancing pressure is the most credible "live" flow effect, at about 17bp next day. It is an index-level (SPY/QQQ) effect, so it applies to large-cap tech through beta. It is small, so it is only worth using as a timing tilt on trades already planned (for example, delaying buys into a predicted month-end or quarter-end equity sell after a strong equity rally), not as a standalone strategy.
- Turn of month is at best a weak, disputed tilt. Do not treat it as alpha.
- 0DTE and gamma: there is no robust evidence that index 0DTE flows create exploitable directional moves. Single-stock call-buying squeezes (2020–21 meme episodes) are documented as frenzies, but the participants lose money on average through spreads.

### Gaps
- Pre-holiday effect: no post-2015 source retrieved. [background, unverified] It was historically strong (Ariel 1990; Kim-Park 1994), with mixed recent evidence.
- Options-expiry (OPEX) pinning and week-after-OPEX weakness: no academic source retrieved. [background, unverified] Ni, Pearson and Poteshman (2005) documented pinning in optionable stocks on expiry dates.
- No post-2015 quantitative study retrieved on single-stock gamma from retail call buying in megacap tech (e.g. TSLA, NVDA).

## 4. Which effects survive in large-cap tech, net of costs, after 2015?

### Takeaway
Almost none survive as standalone long-short alpha in megacaps after 2015. The evidence shows decay is strongest in the US and in liquid stocks, and PEAD and index effects are gone from large caps. What survives is (a) avoiding the behavioural costs (behaviour gap, retail attention and lottery buying, short-dated OTM calls) and (b) small, mechanical, index-level flow tilts (rebalancing).

### Cited Findings
- Anomalies decline 58% after publication. Remaining returns concentrate in high-idiosyncratic-risk, illiquid stocks (i.e. not megacaps). — [McLean-Pontiff](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)
- The US alone shows a reliable post-publication decline (62–66%). — [Jacobs-Müller](https://www.wiwi.hu-berlin.de/en/Professorships/bwl/finance/seminars/papers-fin-acc-res-seminars/17s/jacobs.pdf)
- PEAD has not existed in large stocks since 2006, though this is disputed. — [Martineau](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/martineau2021rest.pdf); [UCLA Anderson](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again)
- The index effect is under 1% and has disappeared across S&P, Russell and Nasdaq-100. — [Greenwood-Sammon](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4294297)
- MAX is unstable and concentrated pre-1990s; in the US it largely proxies for idiosyncratic volatility. — [Springer MRQ](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11301-019-00176-3)
- Rebalancing gives about 17bp next-day market-level pressure (recent sample, 2025). — [NBER w33554](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33554)
- The behaviour gap is about 1.2pp a year, about 2.1pp in volatile funds. — [Morningstar 2026](https://www.morningstar.com/content/cs-assets/v3/assets/blt9415ea4cc4157833/blt852a4301adc2f5db/6a7373d51f7b5ae2da78e1e8/US_Mind_the_Gap_2026.pdf)
- Retail options: 12.6% spreads, net losses, and about 60% of losses from transaction costs. — [Bryzgalova et al.](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20BRYZGALOVA%20-%20Retail%20Trading%20in%20Options%20and%20the%20Rise%20of%20the%20Big%20Three%20Wholesalers.pdf); [WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-in-love-with-same-day-options)

### Inferences
Summary table. Size and post-2015 verdicts are inferences from the cited findings above.

| Effect | Original size | Post-pub / post-2015 status | Small cap vs megacap | Retail use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Robinhood/attention herding | -4.7% 20d after top buys (2018–20) | Recent sample; persistence after 2021 unknown | Strongest in high-attention, small, hard-to-short names | Avoid buying top-mover lists; shorting costly |
| MAX/lottery | >1%/mo decile spread | Unstable, mostly pre-1990s, IVOL proxy | Small/illiquid | Avoid; skip news-free spikes |
| Disposition/overhang | Subsumes momentum (in-sample) | Contested specification; no recent OOS found | Unclear | Mostly a momentum variant |
| 52-week high | ~0.45%/mo | Sentiment-dependent, disputed | Unclear | Not standalone |
| PEAD | Classic drift | Gone in large caps since ~2006 (disputed) | Survives mainly in micro/small | No megacap edge |
| Sentiment (BW) | Strong cross-section in-sample | Fails OOS in 2026 AEA test | Speculative small/growth | Regime context only |
| Fire sales/flow pressure | ~10%/yr front-run (1980–2003) | No recent replication found | Mid/small held by concentrated funds | Data-limited for retail |
| January/tax-loss | >2.5% Jan (1980s–90s) | Small or absent 2000–2023 | Small cap | Skip |
| Index inclusion | 7.4–7.6% (1990s) | ~0.8% (last decade) | All families | Dead |
| Turn of month | Large historically | Disputed (Patel 2024: none) | Index level | Weak tilt at most |
| Pension rebalancing | ~17bp next day | Recent (2025) | Index level, so megacaps via beta | Timing tilt on planned trades |
| 0DTE gamma | — | Dealer gamma de minimis per Cboe; no feedback channel found | Index | No edge; avoid as buyer |
| Retail short-dated calls | 12.6% spreads | Losses rising after daily expiries | Single stocks / index | Avoid as buyer |

- The consistent pattern: psychology-driven mispricing survives where arbitrage is limited (small, illiquid, hard to short) and decays where it is not (megacaps). For a retail trader in large-cap tech, the realistic "edge" from this literature is to avoid paying the behavioural premium, not to collect one.

### Gaps
- No source found that tests these anomalies specifically within a large-cap-tech universe after 2015, net of costs. The table's megacap verdicts are inferred from size splits and general decay evidence.
- The Chen-Zimmermann open-source replication (per-anomaly post-publication returns) was not retrieved. It would be the best single source for per-anomaly post-2015 numbers.
